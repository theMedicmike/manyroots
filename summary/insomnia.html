Before anything else — when to stop and get care

emergencyInsomnia along with hopelessness, feeling like a burden, or thoughts of suicide or self-harmIf you're having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, or sleepless nights are coming with hopelessness, stop here and reach out now: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, available 24/7). Veterans: call 988, then press 1. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.
urgentFalling asleep while driving, or fighting to stay awake at the wheel, at work with machinery, or while caring for a childIf you have nodded off while driving — even for a second — do not drive until you've been evaluated. Ask someone else to drive or use other transport, and contact your doctor this week about urgent evaluation for a sleep disorder.
urgentSigns of obstructive sleep apnea: loud snoring, gasping or choking awake, or someone noticing you stop breathing in your sleepIf you snore loudly, wake up gasping or choking, or a bed partner has seen you stop breathing during sleep — especially with morning headaches, high blood pressure, or heavy daytime sleepiness — see your doctor soon and ask about testing for sleep apnea. This is not something to self-manage with sleep tips or supplements.
urgentUsing sleeping pills or sedating sleep aids together with alcohol, opioid pain medicines, or other sedativesDo not combine sleep medications (prescription or over-the-counter) with alcohol, opioid painkillers, or other sedatives — the combination can dangerously slow your breathing. If you're already mixing them, talk to your doctor or pharmacist before your next dose, and never stop a long-term prescription sedative abruptly without medical guidance.
routineTrouble sleeping at least 3 nights a week for 3 months or moreIf poor sleep has been happening 3 or more nights a week for 3 months or longer, you've crossed into chronic insomnia — a real medical condition with a well-proven first-line treatment. Ask your doctor or health plan for a referral to CBT-I (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia); in-person and digital programs both count.
