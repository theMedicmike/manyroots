Before anything else — when to stop and get care

emergencyThunderclap headache — worst headache of your life, hitting maximum intensity within seconds to a minuteCall 911 now. A headache that explodes to its worst possible intensity within seconds to a minute — the worst headache of your life — is a medical emergency until a doctor proves otherwise. Do not drive yourself. Do not wait to see whether it eases off. Tell them the words 'thunderclap headache' and what time it started, because the timing changes which scan they do.
emergencyHeadache with fever, stiff neck, a rash, or new confusion — possible meningitisCall 911 or go to an emergency department now. A headache together with fever, a stiff neck, new confusion, or a rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it can mean meningitis. You do not need all of these symptoms to go — two of them is enough. Go now rather than waiting to see how the night goes.
emergencyHeadache with any new neurological sign — one-sided weakness or numbness, trouble speaking, facial droop, vision loss, or confusionCall 911 now. Remember FAST — Face drooping on one side, Arm weakness on one side, Speech slurred or muddled, Time to call 911. Also call for sudden numbness down one side, sudden loss of vision, or new confusion. This is treated as a stroke until a doctor proves otherwise, even if you have had migraine for years and even if you have had aura before. Note the exact time symptoms started — treatment options depend on it.
urgentNew or worsening headache after a head injuryGet seen today. A new headache after a blow to the head — or a headache you already had that has clearly got worse since one — needs urgent assessment, even if the knock seemed minor at the time and even if you never lost consciousness. Call 911 instead if there is vomiting, increasing drowsiness, confusion, weakness, a seizure, or clear fluid from the nose or ears. Tell the clinician if you take a blood thinner, because that raises the stakes considerably.
urgentNew headache over age 50, especially with scalp tenderness, jaw pain when chewing, or visual change — possible giant cell arteritisGet seen today, and say the words 'giant cell arteritis' when you book. A new or clearly changed headache after about age 50 — especially with a tender scalp that hurts when you brush your hair, aching in the jaw when chewing, shoulder or hip stiffness, or any change in your vision — needs same-day assessment and blood tests. This is treatable, but untreated it can cause permanent, irreversible blindness. Do not wait for a routine appointment slot.
urgentHeadache worse lying flat, waking you from sleep, or with vomiting and steadily worsening — possible raised pressure inside the headBook an urgent appointment this week, and ask specifically for an examination of the back of your eyes. Headaches that are worse when you lie flat or bend forward, that wake you from sleep or are at their worst first thing in the morning, that spike when you cough, sneeze or strain, or that come with vomiting and are getting steadily worse week by week, need assessment for raised pressure inside the head. Go to an emergency department if it is escalating quickly or your vision is changing.
urgentNew severe headache in pregnancy or after birth, especially with high blood pressure, swelling, or visual change — possible pre-eclampsiaContact your maternity team today, or go to an emergency department if it is severe. A new severe headache during pregnancy or in the weeks after giving birth — especially with raised blood pressure, swelling in your face or hands, pain under your ribs on the right, or visual changes like flashing lights, spots or blurring — needs same-day assessment for pre-eclampsia. Do not assume it is 'just' a migraine because you have had migraines before; pregnancy changes what a new headache can mean.
urgentA first-ever aura, an unusually long aura, or aura with weakness on one side — needs assessment to separate migraine aura from TIA or strokeGet this assessed rather than assuming it is migraine. A first-ever aura, an aura lasting more than an hour, an aura that does not fully clear, or any aura accompanied by weakness down one side of the body needs medical assessment to tell migraine aura apart from a mini-stroke (TIA) or stroke. Call 911 if the symptoms are still happening now, if they came on abruptly rather than spreading gradually over several minutes, or if there is one-sided weakness for the first time. A TIA is a warning sign that is worth acting on immediately.
urgentButterbur — use only pyrrolizidine-alkaloid-free products, and stop immediately at any sign of liver injuryIf you are considering butterbur for migraine prevention, only ever use a product certified free of pyrrolizidine alkaloids — usually labelled 'PA-free'. Uncertified butterbur contains liver-toxic compounds, and product quality is the whole risk here. Stop taking it and seek medical attention the same day if you notice yellowing of your skin or the whites of your eyes, dark urine, severe unexplained tiredness, or pain under your ribs on the right side. Tell any clinician you see that you have been taking butterbur — it is easy to miss if they do not know to ask, and supplements often go unmentioned.
routineMedication-overuse headache — acute headache medicines taken too many days a month can themselves cause daily headacheIf you are taking something for headache on more than 10 days a month, book a review with a clinician — but do not simply stop on your own. Acute headache medicines can start causing the very headaches they are meant to relieve. The thresholds differ by drug: roughly 15 or more days a month for plain paracetamol/acetaminophen, aspirin or other NSAIDs, and roughly 10 or more days a month for triptans, ergots, opioids, or combination painkillers. Coming off them genuinely works, but it can get worse before it gets better, so it should be done alongside a preventive treatment your clinician starts at the same time. Keeping a simple headache and medication diary before the appointment will make that visit far more useful.
routineMigraine with aura combined with combined hormonal contraception and/or smoking — a recognised stroke-risk combinationIf you get migraine with aura and you use a combined pill, patch or vaginal ring — or you smoke — book a contraception review. Migraine with aura is itself a stroke risk factor, and combined hormonal contraception and smoking each multiply that risk on top of it. This is a conversation worth having soon, not an emergency. Progestogen-only and non-hormonal options exist that do not carry the same concern, so do not stop your contraception without arranging an alternative first. If you smoke, stopping is the single biggest lever you control here.

Every option, graded

OptionEvidenceKindWhat the evidence says
NSAIDs Strong Conventional medicine Ordinary anti-inflammatory painkillers - ibuprofen, aspirin, diclofenac - genuinely help a migraine attack, and the trial evidence for that is solid.
Triptans Strong Conventional medicine Triptans are prescription medicines you take when a migraine attack has already started - not every day.
Amitriptyline and other tricyclic antidepressants Moderate Conventional medicine Amitriptyline is an old antidepressant used at much lower doses as a nightly tablet to make migraines less frequent - it does nothing for an attack in progress.
Important: Do not stop abruptly. The dose used for migraine prevention is far lower than the antidepressant dose - do not read one across to the other.
Beta blockers Moderate Conventional medicine Propranolol is an old, cheap blood-pressure tablet that has been used for decades to make migraines less frequent.
Important: Never stop a beta blocker suddenly. Rebound withdrawal can trigger angina, an abnormal heart rhythm or a heart attack, and it must be tapered with a prescriber.
CGRP monoclonal antibodies and gepants Moderate Conventional medicine These are the newest migraine drugs, and they work by blocking a nerve signalling molecule called CGRP.
Topiramate Moderate Conventional medicine Topiramate is a daily tablet taken to make migraines less frequent - it does nothing for an attack that has already started.
Butterbur Moderate Herb Butterbur, taken every day, cut migraine attacks roughly in half in its biggest study — genuinely one of the better results for any plant in this condition.
Feverfew Moderate Herb Feverfew is a daisy-family plant people have taken for headaches for a very long time.
Magnesium Moderate Nutrient Taking magnesium every day may cut down how often migraines come, though the studies genuinely disagree with each other and the effect is modest rather than dramatic.

Ginger Early Herb The honest answer on ginger is that nobody yet knows.
Coenzyme Q10 Early Nutrient CoQ10 taken daily may reduce how many migraines you get each month, but it does not appear to make the attacks you do get shorter or less painful, and the evidence is thinner than it looks.
Mindfulness meditation Early Practice Mindfulness training has been studied for migraine, and the results are genuinely mixed rather than clear-cut.
