Before anything else — when to stop and get care

emergencyFatigue with chest pain, breathlessness at rest or on mild exertion, fainting, or a racing/pounding heartStop here and get emergency care now. If your tiredness comes with chest pain or pressure, breathlessness when you are resting or doing something light, fainting or nearly fainting, or a racing or pounding heartbeat, call 911 (or your local emergency number) or go to an emergency department. These can be signs of a heart problem or of severe anaemia. Both need treating today, and neither is something a supplement can touch.
emergencyFatigue alongside hopelessness or thoughts of suicideIf you are feeling hopeless, or having thoughts of hurting yourself or of ending your life, please stop reading and reach a person now. In the US, call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Veterans: call 988 and press 1. You can also go to any emergency department. Deep exhaustion and despair travel together far more often than people realise — that is a reason to get support, not evidence that something is wrong with you.
urgentFatigue with unexplained weight loss, drenching night sweats, recurrent fevers, or a new lumpBook an urgent appointment — this week, not next month. Weight you did not try to lose, night sweats that soak your bedding or nightclothes, fevers that keep coming back, or a new lump anywhere need to be looked at by a clinician alongside your tiredness. Most of the time it will not be cancer. But this is exactly the combination clinicians are trained to investigate quickly, and being seen sooner costs you nothing except an appointment.
urgentFatigue with unusual bruising or bleeding, or looking very paleAsk for a full blood count within the next few days. If you are bruising without knocking into anything, your gums bleed, you are getting nosebleeds that will not settle, or people are telling you that you look very pale, contact your clinician and say you would like a full blood count soon. A problem with how your blood is being made is found on a simple, cheap, widely available test — not by trying another supplement.
urgentNew fatigue with excessive thirst and passing a lot of urineGet your blood sugar checked this week. New tiredness together with constant thirst and needing to pee far more than usual should prompt a blood glucose or HbA1c test — ask for it directly. If you also have vomiting, stomach pain, fast deep breathing, or breath that smells sweet or fruity, go to an emergency department now rather than waiting for an appointment.
urgentLoud snoring, witnessed pauses in breathing, or falling asleep during the dayAsk about a sleep apnea assessment before you try anything else. If you snore loudly, if someone has seen you stop breathing during sleep, or if you fall asleep during the day when you did not mean to, that pattern needs a proper sleep evaluation. No supplement, vitamin, herb or adaptogen treats sleep apnea. Please also be careful about driving while you are this sleepy, and tell your clinician if you drive for work.
routineHeavy periods with fatigue — common, very treatable, and routinely dismissedIf your periods are heavy and you are exhausted, ask specifically for a ferritin test, not just a haemoglobin. Soaking a pad or tampon every hour or two, passing clots bigger than a coin, or bleeding for more than about a week counts as heavy — even if it is what you have always had. Heavy periods are the single most common reason people run low on iron, low iron is very treatable, and this gets brushed off constantly. Ask anyway, and ask again if you are told your bloods are 'normal' without a ferritin being run.
routineCORE MESSAGE — persistent fatigue deserves basic blood tests before any supplementBefore you buy a single supplement, get the basic blood tests. Persistent tiredness deserves a full blood count, ferritin or iron studies, thyroid function, glucose or HbA1c, vitamin B12, and a coeliac screen if your clinician thinks it fits your picture. The most common treatable causes of fatigue show up on that panel. Supplements taken blind can shift your numbers just enough to hide the answer — and six months later you will have spent the money and still be tired. Get the tests first. Then come back and read.
routineTaking iron without a confirmed deficiency is not harmlessDo not start iron 'just in case'. Ask for a ferritin test first. There is evidence that iron helps tiredness in people who are genuinely low on it — and no good evidence that it does anything for tiredness in people who are not. Iron that your body does not need accumulates in the liver, heart and pancreas over years and causes real organ damage. And if you do turn out to be low, the important next question is why you are low, because in men and in women past menopause that often means bleeding somewhere that needs finding.
routineFatigue that began after an infection and worsens after exertion (post-exertional malaise)If your fatigue started after an infection and gets dramatically worse for a day or more after you exert yourself — physically or mentally — that pattern has a name: post-exertional malaise. Do not try to push through it with exercise. Look for a clinician who works with ME/CFS or long COVID, and read about pacing before you start any activity or training plan. Advice to 'just build up your fitness gradually' is the specific thing that can set you back, and it is no longer what the guidelines say.

Every option, graded

OptionEvidenceKindWhat the evidence says
Iron Strong Nutrient If your tiredness is coming from low iron, replacing it can make a real difference - but only if you actually are low.
Important: Get ferritin tested before supplementing - and keep iron tablets away from children, where overdose is a leading cause of poisoning death.
Treating the underlying cause Moderate Conventional medicine Tiredness that lasts is a symptom, not a diagnosis, so the first useful step is finding out what is driving it.
Coenzyme Q10 Moderate Nutrient CoQ10 is a substance your cells use to make energy, and when many small studies are pooled it looks like it may take some edge off fatigue.
Cognitive behavioural therapy Moderate Practice This is talking therapy aimed at the habits, thoughts and activity patterns that can keep exhaustion going, not at telling you the tiredness is imaginary.
Mindfulness meditation Moderate Practice Mindfulness means practising deliberate, non-judgemental attention to what is happening right now, usually taught as a group course with daily home practice.
Regular exercise and physical activity Moderate Practice Moving your body more can lift energy for some people, but where the evidence is strongest matters a lot.
Sleep hygiene and stimulus control Moderate Practice There is a real difference between generic sleep tips and the specific behavioural methods sleep specialists use.
Caffeine Early Conventional medicine Caffeine is genuinely good at making you feel more awake for a few hours, and there is decent trial evidence for that in shift workers and people short on sleep.
Ashwagandha Early Herb Ashwagandha is widely sold for energy and stress, and some small studies in healthy or athletic people report less mental fatigue and better exercise tolerance.

Vitamin D Early Nutrient Genuine vitamin D deficiency can leave you weak and tired, and it is cheap to test and easy to correct.
Pacing and activity management Early Practice Pacing means learning where your energy limit sits and deliberately staying inside it, so you do not trigger a crash a day or two later.
Modafinil and stimulants Not supported Conventional medicine Wakefulness drugs like modafinil, and stimulants like methylphenidate, are sometimes prescribed off-label to help people push through fatigue.
Important: If one of these was prescribed for you, don't stop or change it because of this grade, which is about fatigue, not the conditions they are prescribed for, such as narcolepsy. Talk to your prescriber.
Guarana and caffeine-containing herbs Not supported Herb Guarana is essentially a concentrated natural source of caffeine, and it has been tested mainly in people exhausted by cancer treatment.
