L-theanine

Early evidence · for anxiety & stress Early evidence · for insomnia

A grade belongs to a connection, not to a substance — the evidence can be decent for one condition and absent for another.

For anxiety & stress

L-theanine is a calming compound found naturally in green tea. Small, short studies in healthy people suggest a dose may soften a stressful moment, and a few weeks of use may modestly lower everyday anxiety, though those studies were small and some were run with involvement from the ingredient's manufacturer. In the one careful study of people with a diagnosed anxiety disorder it worked no better than a dummy pill, so think of it as promising for ordinary stress and unproven for clinical anxiety.

Safety

Well tolerated in the trials reviewed at 200-900 mg/day, with no serious adverse events reported, including in the 8-week trial at up to 900 mg/day in generalized anxiety disorder patients who were also taking antidepressants. Trials have run at most about 8-10 weeks, so nothing is established about safety beyond a couple of months of continuous use.

Who should be careful: Safety has not been established in pregnancy, in breastfeeding, or in children, and none of the trials above enrolled those groups. People with a diagnosed anxiety disorder should not substitute L-theanine for evaluated treatment: the only placebo-controlled trial in that population found no benefit over placebo on the primary anxiety measure.

Interactions: L-theanine was combined with stable antidepressants for 8 weeks in the generalized anxiety disorder trial without reported safety problems, so co-administration is not known to be hazardous, but anyone taking psychiatric medication, sedatives or blood-pressure medication should tell their prescriber before adding it. Interactions with caffeine, with which L-theanine is naturally co-consumed in tea, and any additive blood-pressure-lowering effect, are not well characterized in humans and should not be assumed either way.

What the evidence says

A 2020 systematic review (Williams et al.) of 9 peer-reviewed placebo-controlled RCTs of pure oral L-theanine concluded that 200-400 mg/day may assist in reducing stress and anxiety in people exposed to stressful conditions, while explicitly stating that longer-term and larger studies are needed before clinical use is justified. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 30 healthy adults, State-Trait Anxiety Inventory-trait, depression and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores decreased after four weeks of 200 mg/day L-theanine (p=0.006, 0.019 and 0.013), though the paper reports these as changes following L-theanine administration rather than as a demonstrated contrast against placebo, and two authors are employed by a commercial L-theanine producer. In a double-blind crossover trial of 34 healthy adults, an L-theanine-based nutrient drink reduced the subjective stress response to a multitasking stressor 1 hour post-dose and the salivary cortisol response 3 hours post-dose versus placebo, with no cognitive performance changes; because the product contained other ingredients, the effect cannot be attributed to L-theanine alone. Against this, an 8-week double-blind RCT in 46 patients with DSM-5 generalized anxiety disorder found adjunctive L-theanine 450-900 mg/day did not outperform placebo on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (p=0.73) or on insomnia severity (p=0.35). A 2024 RCT in 80 adults found a single 200 mg dose did not alter salivary alpha-amylase, secretory IgA, heart rate or state anxiety during a virtual-reality stress challenge. The evidence is therefore confined to small, short trials, positive mainly for acute or subclinical stress in healthy adults and null where anxiety was clinically diagnosed.

Doses studied: 200-400 mg/day of pure L-theanine in most trials: 200 mg/day for 4 weeks in the main chronic trial and single 200 mg doses in the acute-stress studies. The null generalized anxiety disorder trial used 450-900 mg/day adjunctively for 8 weeks. No trial reviewed ran longer than about 10 weeks.

Why this grade: A 2020 systematic review found 9 small placebo-controlled RCTs suggesting 200-400 mg/day may assist in reducing stress and anxiety in people exposed to stressful conditions, with the review's own authors stating that longer and larger studies are needed before clinical use is justified. The most-cited chronic trial (n=30) reports its anxiety result as a within-condition change after L-theanine rather than a demonstrated contrast against placebo, and two of its authors work for a commercial L-theanine producer; another supportive trial tested a multi-ingredient drink rather than pure L-theanine. Against this, the only placebo-controlled trial in a diagnosed clinical anxiety population was null on its primary anxiety outcome (HAMA p=0.73), and a 2024 acute-stress RCT found no effect on stress markers or state anxiety. Small, short trials with mixed results and null findings in the populations that matter most: early.

Sources (5)
  1. The Effects of Green Tea Amino Acid L-Theanine Consumption on the Ability to Manage Stress and Anxiety Levels: a Systematic Review
    Williams JL, Everett JM, et al. (2020) · PMID 31758301 · positive

    Across 9 peer-reviewed randomised controlled trials of pure oral L-theanine versus control, the authors found that supplementation of 200-400 mg/day may assist in the reduction of stress and anxiety in people exposed to stressful conditions, while stating that longer-term and larger cohort studies are needed to clinically justify its use as a therapeutic agent.

  2. Effects of L-Theanine Administration on Stress-Related Symptoms and Cognitive Functions in Healthy Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial
    Hidese S, Ogawa S, et al. (2019) · PMID 31623400 · positive

    In a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind crossover trial of 30 healthy adults, Self-rating Depression Scale, State-Trait Anxiety Inventory-trait and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores decreased after four weeks of L-theanine 200 mg/day (p=0.019, 0.006 and 0.013 respectively); the paper frames these as changes after L-theanine administration, and reserves explicit 'compared to the placebo administration' wording for the PSQI subscales and the verbal-fluency and executive-function measures. Two authors are employed by a commercial L-theanine manufacturer.

  3. Anti-Stress, Behavioural and Magnetoencephalography Effects of an L-Theanine-Based Nutrient Drink: A Randomised, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Crossover Trial
    White DJ, de Klerk S, et al. (2016) · PMID 26797633 · positive

    In 34 healthy adults, an L-theanine-based nutrient drink significantly reduced the subjective stress response to a multitasking cognitive stressor 1 hour post-dose and reduced the salivary cortisol response 3 hours post-dose versus placebo; no treatment-related cognitive performance changes were observed. The product was a multi-ingredient drink, so the effect cannot be attributed to L-theanine in isolation.

  4. L-theanine in the adjunctive treatment of generalized anxiety disorder: A double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial
    Sarris J, Byrne GJ, et al. (2019) · PMID 30580081 · null

    In 46 participants with a DSM-5 diagnosis of generalized anxiety disorder on stable antidepressants, 8 weeks of adjunctive L-theanine 450-900 mg/day did not outperform placebo for anxiety reduction on the HAMA (p=0.73) nor for insomnia severity on the Insomnia Severity Index (p=0.35); a self-reported sleep-satisfaction item favoured L-theanine (p=0.015) and a separation was seen on the ISI in those with non-clinical insomnia levels (p=0.007); no significant cognitive effects were found.

  5. Impact of L-theanine and L-tyrosine on markers of stress and cognitive performance in response to a virtual reality based active shooter training drill
    McAllister MJ, Martaindale MH, et al. (2024) · PMID 38975711 · null

    In 80 adults randomized to L-theanine 200 mg (n=25), L-tyrosine 2000 mg (n=28) or placebo (n=27) before a virtual-reality active-shooter and cognitive stress challenge, the challenge significantly raised salivary alpha-amylase, secretory IgA, heart rate and state anxiety, but ingestion of L-theanine did not impact any of these stress markers; only L-tyrosine affected a cognitive measure.

For insomnia

L-theanine is a calming amino acid from green tea that doesn't knock you out like a sleeping pill — the idea is it takes the edge off stress so sleep comes easier. A few small studies show promise, with people reporting better sleep quality, but the most rigorous trial found it didn't significantly reduce insomnia overall, so the evidence is genuinely early-stage. It is very well tolerated, which makes it a low-risk thing to try, just with modest expectations.

Safety

L-theanine is consistently well tolerated in trials: no significant adverse events were reported at 200 mg/day in healthy adults, at 200 mg in cancer patients, at 450-900 mg/day as an adjunct in GAD, or at 400 mg/day for 6 weeks in a pediatric ADHD trial. It is a normal constituent of tea. Long-term safety data at supplemental doses are limited.

Who should be careful: Insufficient data in pregnancy and breastfeeding — avoid supplemental doses without clinician advice. People with low blood pressure should use caution, as theanine may modestly lower blood pressure.

Interactions: May add to the effect of antihypertensive medicines and sedatives, and may blunt the effects of stimulant medications; people on stimulants (e.g., for ADHD) or blood-pressure treatment should check with their prescriber. Note that theanine taken as tea comes with caffeine, which itself disrupts sleep.

What the evidence says

Evidence for L-theanine in sleep is limited to small trials with mixed results. In a randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 30 healthy adults, 4 weeks of L-theanine 200 mg/day improved Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores (p=0.013), including sleep latency and sleep disturbance subscales (p<0.05). In contrast, an 8-week double-blind RCT of adjunctive L-theanine 450-900 mg/day in 46 adults with generalized anxiety disorder found no significant advantage over placebo on Insomnia Severity Index (p=0.35) or anxiety (p=0.73), with benefit limited to a single sleep-satisfaction item (p=0.015) and a subgroup with non-clinical insomnia symptoms. In a randomized, double-blind, 3-arm study of 120 cancer patients with insomnia, L-theanine 200 mg reduced Athens Insomnia Scale scores versus placebo over 14 days (mean AIS 9.55 vs 13.05 at day 14) but was significantly inferior to melatonin 3 mg (AIS 5.00). A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of 50 RCTs of tea, theanine, or theanine plus caffeine found small-to-moderate benefits for cognition and mood outcomes but noted that confidence intervals frequently highlighted uncertainty about the direction and magnitude of those differences, and it reported no pooled sleep effect estimates. A multi-ingredient presleep formula containing theanine also showed no significant improvement in polysomnographic or subjective sleep in 17 trained males.

Doses studied: 200 mg once daily (healthy adults; cancer patients) up to 450-900 mg/day (adjunctive dosing in GAD), typically for 2-8 weeks; commonly taken in the evening.

Why this grade: Only small, short human trials exist, and results are mixed: a 30-person crossover RCT in healthy adults improved PSQI scores, and a 3-arm trial in cancer patients beat placebo but was clearly inferior to melatonin, while the methodologically strongest trial (Sarris 2019, GAD adjunct) found no significant effect on its Insomnia Severity Index outcome. A 2025 systematic review of tea/theanine RCTs judged the evidence uncertain and reported no pooled sleep effect estimates. Small, few, and mixed trials place this at early; the positive trials also frequently involve manufacturer-affiliated authors.

  1. Effects of L-Theanine Administration on Stress-Related Symptoms and Cognitive Functions in Healthy Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial.
    Hidese et al., 2019 · PMID 31623400 · positive

    In a placebo-controlled crossover RCT of 30 healthy adults, 4 weeks of L-theanine 200 mg/day reduced Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores (p=0.013), with improvements in sleep latency, sleep disturbance, and use-of-sleep-medication subscales versus placebo (all p<0.05); two authors are employees of the theanine manufacturer (Taiyo Kagaku).

  2. L-theanine in the adjunctive treatment of generalized anxiety disorder: A double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial.
    Sarris et al., 2019 · PMID 30580081 · null

    In 46 adults with GAD on stable antidepressants, 8 weeks of adjunctive L-theanine 450-900 mg/day did not outperform placebo on insomnia severity (ISI, p=0.35) or anxiety (HAMA, p=0.73); only a single self-reported sleep-satisfaction item favored theanine (p=0.015), and an ISI separation appeared only in the subgroup with non-clinical insomnia symptoms (p=0.007).

  3. Comparison Between Efficacy of Oral Melatonin and Oral L-theanine in Improving Sleep in Cancer Patients Suffering From Insomnia: A Randomised Double-blinded Placebo-controlled Study.
    Kurdi, As, Ladhad, Mitragotri & Baiju, 2024 · PMID 38846134 · mixed

    In 120 cancer patients with insomnia, L-theanine 200 mg for 14 days improved Athens Insomnia Scale scores significantly more than placebo (mean AIS 9.55 vs 13.05 at day 14) but was significantly inferior to melatonin 3 mg (AIS 5.00 at day 14).

  4. Effects of Tea (Camellia sinensis) or its Bioactive Compounds l-Theanine or l-Theanine plus Caffeine on Cognition, Sleep, and Mood in Healthy Participants: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.
    Payne, Aceves-Martins, Dubost, Greyling & de Roos, 2025 · PMID 40314930 · mixed

    Across 50 RCTs in healthy participants (15 meta-analyzable), small-to-moderate benefits appeared for cognition and mood, with the authors noting that confidence intervals 'frequently highlighted the uncertainty surrounding the direction and magnitude' of those differences; no pooled sleep effect estimates are reported, leaving any sleep benefit unestablished; industry (tea-sector) co-authors are declared.

  5. Minimal Influence of Formulated Nutritional Interventions on Sleep and Next-Morning Physical Performance, Cognitive Function, and Postural Sway in Adult Males: A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled, Crossover Study.
    Morrison et al., 2025 · PMID 39983739 · null

    A presleep drink containing theanine (with carbohydrate, whey, tryptophan, and 5'AMP) produced no significant improvements versus placebo in any objective (polysomnography) or subjective sleep parameter in 17 trained adult males; a multi-ingredient design, so theanine-specific effects cannot be isolated.
