Conventional medicine

Calcium channel blockers

Moderate evidence · for high blood pressure

For high blood pressure

Calcium channel blockers are one of several accepted first choices for high blood pressure, and their best single result is stroke: against placebo in older adults, a regimen starting with one of these cut strokes by 42 percent. Against a cheap water pill, in the 24,303 people randomised to one or the other, they came out level on heart attacks and on death rather than ahead, and they led to more heart failure, the clearest of the ways a water pill does better. Swollen ankles are the common trade-off and depend on the dose: about 17 in 100 people on amlodipine against 6 in 100 on placebo, and fewer at lower doses. Which drug to start, switch or stop is a prescriber's call, not a website's.

Safety

These are prescription drugs; class choice, dose and monitoring belong with a prescriber, and no one should start, change or stop them from a website. The dihydropyridines - amlodipine, felodipine, nifedipine, nitrendipine, lercanidipine - and the non-dihydropyridines, verapamil and diltiazem, are not interchangeable, and the second pair slow the heart. Ankle and leg swelling is the signature dihydropyridine effect and is dose-dependent: across 22 placebo-controlled trials oedema occurred in 16.6% on amlodipine versus 6.2% on placebo (RR 2.9, 2.50-3.36), with lower risk at 2.5-5 mg/day than at 10 mg/day (PMID 31107359). Flushing, dizziness, palpitations, constipation and gum overgrowth are also common. This class does not disturb blood sugar (PMID 34774144) and causes neither the dry cough nor the potassium rise that ACE inhibitors and ARBs can. Its consistent disadvantage is heart failure, higher than with a thiazide: 10.2% versus 7.7% over six years in ALLHAT (RR 1.38, 1.25-1.52) (PMID 12479763), the trial whose estimate the two Cochrane head-to-head reviews largely reproduce rather than confirm independently (PMID 35000192, PMID 37439548). In a randomised verapamil trial non-stroke haemorrhage was more common than on atenolol or hydrochlorothiazide (HR 1.54, 1.16-2.04) (PMID 12709465). Seek urgent care for fainting, a very slow pulse, breathlessness, new swelling with rapid weight gain, confusion or severe weakness.

Who should be careful: Verapamil and diltiazem are avoided in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, in significant bradycardia, in second- or third-degree heart block without a pacemaker and in sick sinus syndrome, and are not combined with a beta-blocker outside specialist supervision, because together they can cause severe bradycardia or complete heart block. Dihydropyridines are avoided in severe aortic stenosis and in unstable angina or the days after a myocardial infarction, and short-acting immediate-release nifedipine is not used for blood pressure at all. This class is not a first choice where heart failure is present or likely, given a consistently higher heart-failure rate than with a thiazide diuretic. Use caution in significant liver impairment, since most of these drugs are cleared hepatically. Anyone with resistant, secondary or pulmonary hypertension, or who is pregnant, needs an individualised assessment this page does not cover.

Interactions: The cross-link that matters most on this page is beta blockers, graded here separately: verapamil or diltiazem with a beta-blocker can slow the heart dangerously or cause complete heart block, and needs specialist supervision. Magnesium, graded moderate on this page, is itself a calcium antagonist and at supplement doses can add to the pressure drop. Potassium, graded strong here, is not the hazard it is with an ACE inhibitor or ARB, because calcium channel blockers do not raise potassium. Grapefruit juice raises blood levels of several dihydropyridines, notably felodipine and nifedipine, and can cause an exaggerated pressure drop. Verapamil and diltiazem inhibit CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein and raise levels of simvastatin and atorvastatin (dose caps apply), ciclosporin, tacrolimus, carbamazepine, digoxin and several direct oral anticoagulants. Strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampicin, phenytoin and St John's wort can strip a dihydropyridine of most of its effect. NSAIDs blunt the blood-pressure effect, while decongestants such as pseudoephedrine, oral corticosteroids and stimulant medications raise pressure and work against treatment. Sodium reduction, the DASH diet, weight loss, regular exercise, beetroot or dietary nitrate, hibiscus, garlic and cocoa flavanols are all graded on this page and all lower blood pressure, so they stack with medication; so do alcohol, alpha-blockers, nitrates and PDE5 inhibitors. Disclose every supplement to the prescriber.

What the evidence says

Positive on stroke against placebo, not on death. Syst-Eur gave 4,695 patients over 60 with isolated systolic hypertension a nitrendipine-based regimen or placebo: total stroke fell from 13.7 to 7.9 endpoints per 1000 patient-years (42%, p=0.003), with all-cause mortality unchanged (-14%, p=0.22) (PMID 9297994). The 2018 Cochrane first-line review reports that same result, not a second one - stroke RR 0.58 (0.41-0.84) is 7.9/13.7 and mortality RR 0.86 (0.68-1.09) is that -14% - alongside total cardiovascular events RR 0.71 (0.57-0.87) and coronary heart disease RR 0.77 (0.55-1.09), all LOW quality and applicable to moderate to severe primary hypertension, not any raised reading (PMID 29667175). As thiazide versus calcium channel blocker, the 2023 Cochrane review found little to no difference in total mortality (RR 1.02, 0.96-1.08; 7 trials, 35,417; moderate certainty) and, in 6 trials with 35,217 participants at moderate certainty, none in stroke (RR 1.06, 0.95-1.18) or coronary heart disease (RR 1.00, 0.93-1.08) - but heart failure in 4.4% on the calcium channel blocker against 3.2% on the thiazide (RR 0.74, 0.66-0.82) and total cardiovascular events 14.3% against 13.3% (RR 0.93, 0.89-0.98), with withdrawals for adverse effects 7.6% against 6.2% (RR 0.81, 0.75-0.88; 7 trials, 33,908; low) (PMID 37439548). ALLHAT's 24,303-person amlodipine-versus-chlorthalidone contrast was level on the primary coronary outcome (RR 0.98, 0.90-1.07) and on all-cause mortality, and its heart-failure estimate (RR 1.38, 1.25-1.52), the 2022 review's (RR 1.37, 1.25-1.51) and the inverse of that 0.74 are one estimate to within rounding: three readings of largely the same randomised data, not three confirmations (PMID 12479763). That 2022 review found all-cause mortality no different from any other class, more major cardiovascular events than with diuretics (RR 1.05, 1.00-1.09), and more heart failure than with ACE inhibitors or ARBs (PMID 35000192). Its advantage over beta blockers reads largely the same head-to-head trials as the beta-blocker review this page grades that class on, from the other side - one comparison, not two. The honest negative is historical: at equal pressure control in 2000, acute myocardial infarction ran at OR 1.26 (1.11-1.43) and heart failure at 1.25 (1.07-1.46) against other classes, and the class was judged unsuitable first-line (PMID 11130522) - a coronary excess the modern pool does not reproduce. CONVINCE matched the pressure fall in 16,602 patients on verapamil versus atenolol or hydrochlorothiazide and did NOT show equivalence (HR 1.02, 0.88-1.18) (PMID 12709465). SURROGATE ONLY: this is the only first-line class with no material effect on new-onset type 2 diabetes (RR 1.02, 0.92-1.13), a threshold-defined diagnosis, not a hard event (PMID 34774144). Shared backdrop, reported by no class: across 48 trials and 344,716 participants each 5 mm Hg systolic reduction cut major cardiovascular events by about 10% (PMID 33933205).

Doses studied: Standard licensed doses titrated to a blood-pressure target. ALLHAT used amlodipine 2.5-10 mg/day against chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/day (PMID 12479763). Syst-Eur started nitrendipine 10-40 mg/day, adding enalapril 5-20 mg/day and hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-25.0 mg/day as needed, so its stroke result belongs to a nitrendipine-based regimen rather than to nitrendipine alone (PMID 9297994). CONVINCE began controlled-onset extended-release verapamil at 180 mg (PMID 12709465). Dose matters for tolerability rather than for outcomes: amlodipine 2.5-5 mg/day carried a lower oedema risk than 10 mg/day (RR 2.01, 1.41-2.88 versus 3.08, 2.62-3.60; p for interaction 0.03) (PMID 31107359).

Why this grade: Scoped to calcium channel blockers as a first-choice drug for established high blood pressure; whether to treat at all, and how low to aim, are graded separately on this page. The background to a 4.9-million-patient analysis records that guidelines recommend any primary agent among the first-line classes, naming both dihydropyridine and non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers alongside thiazides, ACE inhibitors and ARBs in the absence of comorbid indications (PMID 31668726) - among several acceptable classes, not above them. In the Cochrane first-line review the placebo-controlled evidence is null on all-cause mortality (RR 0.86, 0.68-1.09) and coronary heart disease (RR 0.77, 0.55-1.09) and rated LOW quality, while the beta-blocker evidence there, null on the same two (RR 0.96, 0.86-1.07 and 0.90, 0.78-1.03; this page grades beta blockers on a separate review), is rated LOW-TO-MODERATE (PMID 29667175). So this class cannot carry strong while its placebo evidence is graded below that of a class graded not_supported here; when torn, the lower grade wins. What separates the two is effect size (stroke RR 0.58 against 0.83, total cardiovascular events 0.71 against 0.89) and the review's verdict: calcium channel blockers 'may be similarly effective' to low-dose thiazides on lower-quality evidence, beta-blockers 'were inferior'. There is one positive placebo-controlled hard-outcome result here, not two: Syst-Eur cut total stroke from 13.7 to 7.9 endpoints per 1000 patient-years in 4,695 adults over 60 on a nitrendipine-based regimen (PMID 9297994), and 7.9/13.7 is 0.58 while that trial's non-significant 14% mortality reduction is 0.86 - precisely the review's two calcium-channel-blocker figures. Syst-Eur dominates that pool, so those are one result rather than two legs. Everything else is parity, and parity is not a positive result: ALLHAT's amlodipine and chlorthalidone arms (9,048 and 15,255, so 24,303 of a 33,357 three-arm trial) were level on the primary coronary outcome and on all-cause mortality (PMID 12479763), and head-to-head mortality is no different from any other class (PMID 35000192). Against a thiazide two debits sit at moderate certainty - congestive heart failure and total cardiovascular events - with more withdrawals for adverse effects besides (PMID 37439548). The head-to-head advantage over beta blockers separates them too, and the class is not uniform: a randomised verapamil trial failed to show equivalence (PMID 12709465) and non-dihydropyridines came out inferior to the other four classes observationally (PMID 31668726).

Sources (12)
  1. Randomised double-blind comparison of placebo and active treatment for older patients with isolated systolic hypertension. The Systolic Hypertension in Europe (Syst-Eur) Trial Investigators.
    Randomised trialStaessen JA, Fagard R, Thijs L, et al., Lancet 1997 · PMID 9297994 · positive

    4,695 patients over 60 whose average sitting systolic pressure at three run-in visits was 160-219 mm Hg with diastolic below 95 mm Hg were randomised to nitrendipine 10-40 mg daily, with enalapril 5-20 mg and hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-25.0 mg added as needed (n=2,398), or matching placebos (n=2,297). At a median two years the between-group difference was systolic 10.1 mm Hg (95% CI 8.8-11.4) and diastolic 4.5 mm Hg (3.9-5.1). Active treatment cut total stroke from 13.7 to 7.9 endpoints per 1000 patient-years (42% reduction, p=0.003), non-fatal stroke by 44% (p=0.007), all fatal and non-fatal cardiac endpoints including sudden death by 26% (p=0.03) and all fatal and non-fatal cardiovascular endpoints by 31% (p<0.001). Cardiovascular mortality fell 27% (p=0.07) and all-cause mortality was NOT influenced (-14%, p=0.22). The result belongs to a nitrendipine-BASED regimen, not to nitrendipine alone. The stroke rate ratio 7.9/13.7 is 0.58 and the mortality reduction is 0.86, which are the two figures the 2018 Cochrane first-line review reports for this class.

  2. First-line drugs for hypertension.
    Meta-analysisWright JM, Musini VM, Gill R, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2018 · PMID 29667175 · mixed

    A review of 24 placebo-controlled or no-treatment trials with 28 active treatment arms and 58,040 patients across four drug classes (the review sought six and found no trials for ARBs or alpha-blockers), mean age 56, follow-up three to five years; the abstract gives no separate trial count or participant total for the calcium-channel-blocker comparison. Results are stated to be mostly applicable to adults with moderate to severe primary hypertension. First-line calcium channel blockers reduced stroke from 3.4% to 1.9% (RR 0.58, 95% CI 0.41-0.84) and total cardiovascular events from 8.0% to 5.7% (RR 0.71, 0.57-0.87) but did NOT reduce coronary heart disease (3.1% versus 2.4%; RR 0.77, 0.55-1.09) or all-cause mortality (6.0% versus 5.1%; RR 0.86, 0.68-1.09), all on LOW-quality evidence, and no withdrawal-for-adverse-effect data were available for this class. Beta-blocker evidence is rated LOW-TO-MODERATE and is null on the same two outcomes (mortality RR 0.96, 0.86-1.07; coronary heart disease RR 0.90, 0.78-1.03), while first-line low-dose thiazides are rated high quality on all four. Syst-Eur dominates the calcium-channel-blocker pool: its own stroke ratio is 0.58 and its own mortality reduction 0.86, so these two estimates are not independent of that trial.

  3. Calcium channel blockers versus other classes of drugs for hypertension.
    Systematic reviewZhu J, Chen N, Zhou M, et al., Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2022 · PMID 35000192 · mixed

    23 randomised trials (18 dihydropyridine, 4 non-dihydropyridine, 1 not specified) with 153,849 hypertensive participants, comparing first-line calcium channel blockers with other classes over at least two years. All-cause mortality was NOT different from any other antihypertensive class, and the abstract gives no figure for it. Versus diuretics, calcium channel blockers probably increased major cardiovascular events (RR 1.05, 95% CI 1.00-1.09, P=0.03) and increased congestive heart failure (RR 1.37, 1.25-1.51, moderate certainty). Versus beta-blockers they reduced major cardiovascular events (RR 0.84, 0.77-0.92), stroke (RR 0.77, 0.67-0.88, moderate certainty) and cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.90, 0.81-0.99, low certainty). Versus ACE inhibitors they reduced stroke (RR 0.90, 0.81-0.99, low certainty) and increased congestive heart failure (RR 1.16, 1.06-1.28, low certainty). Versus ARBs they reduced myocardial infarction (RR 0.82, 0.72-0.94, moderate certainty) and increased congestive heart failure (RR 1.20, 1.06-1.36, low certainty). The review authors conclude that diuretics reduce major cardiovascular events and congestive heart failure more than calcium channel blockers at moderate certainty, and that many of the differences found are not robust; the background records that this class is often recommended as a first-line drug. The beta-blocker comparison reads largely the same head-to-head trials as the 2017 Cochrane beta-blocker review graded on this page - the four trials, 44,825 participants, that review pools for this comparison - from the opposite side.

  4. First-line diuretics versus other classes of antihypertensive drugs for hypertension.
    Narrative reviewReinhart M, Puil L, Salzwedel DM, Wright JM, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2023 · PMID 37439548 · mixed

    20 trials with 26 comparator arms randomising over 90,000 participants, eight of those trials comparing a first-line thiazide or thiazide-like diuretic with a first-line calcium channel blocker; the review states its findings are relevant to older hypertensive patients aged 50 to 75 with multiple co-morbidities including type 2 diabetes. Every estimate below is stated as thiazide versus calcium channel blocker, and the review gives the percentages calcium channel blocker first. Thiazides probably make little to no difference to total mortality (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.96-1.08; 7 trials, 35,417 participants; moderate certainty), stroke (RR 1.06, 0.95-1.18) or coronary heart disease (RR 1.00, 0.93-1.08; both 6 trials, 35,217; moderate certainty), and may make little to no difference to serious adverse events (RR 1.09, 0.97-1.24; 2 trials, 7,204; low certainty). They probably reduce heart failure, 4.4% on a calcium channel blocker versus 3.2% on a thiazide (RR 0.74, 0.66-0.82; 6 trials, 35,217; absolute risk reduction 1.2%; moderate certainty), and total cardiovascular events, 14.3% versus 13.3% (RR 0.93, 0.89-0.98; 6 trials, 35,217; absolute risk reduction 1.0%; moderate certainty), and may reduce withdrawals due to adverse effects, 7.6% versus 6.2% (RR 0.81, 0.75-0.88; 7 trials, 33,908; absolute risk reduction 1.4%; low certainty). No antihypertensive drug class demonstrated any clinically important advantages over first-line thiazides. This review and the 2022 calcium-channel-blocker review read overlapping head-to-head trials from opposite sides: 1 divided by 0.74 is 1.35, against that review's 1.37 for the same comparison.

  5. Major outcomes in high-risk hypertensive patients randomized to angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or calcium channel blocker vs diuretic: The Antihypertensive and Lipid-Lowering Treatment to Prevent Heart Attack Trial (ALLHAT).
    Randomised trialALLHAT Collaborative Research Group, JAMA 2002 · PMID 12479763 · mixed

    33,357 participants aged 55 or older with hypertension and at least one other coronary risk factor, from 623 North American centres, randomised to chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/d (n=15,255), amlodipine 2.5-10 mg/d (n=9,048) or lisinopril 10-40 mg/d (n=9,054), mean follow-up 4.9 years. The amlodipine-versus-chlorthalidone contrast is therefore 24,303 people, not 33,357. The primary outcome of combined fatal coronary heart disease or non-fatal myocardial infarction did NOT differ: versus chlorthalidone (six-year rate 11.5%), RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.90-1.07) for amlodipine (11.3%) and 0.99 (0.91-1.08) for lisinopril (11.4%). All-cause mortality did not differ either. Five-year systolic pressure was significantly higher on amlodipine (0.8 mm Hg, P=.03) and on lisinopril (2 mm Hg, P<.001) than on chlorthalidone, while five-year diastolic pressure was significantly LOWER on amlodipine (0.8 mm Hg, P<.001). For amlodipine versus chlorthalidone secondary outcomes were similar except a higher six-year heart-failure rate (10.2% versus 7.7%; RR 1.38, 1.25-1.52). For lisinopril versus chlorthalidone, combined cardiovascular disease (33.3% versus 30.9%; RR 1.10, 1.05-1.16), stroke (6.3% versus 5.6%; RR 1.15, 1.02-1.30) and heart failure (8.7% versus 7.7%; RR 1.19, 1.07-1.31) were higher. JAMA published two corrections to this article (2003;289:178 and 2004;291:2196); neither has its own PubMed record, and the figures above are as published in the original report.

  6. Health outcomes associated with calcium antagonists compared with other first-line antihypertensive therapies: a meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.
    Meta-analysisPahor M, Psaty BM, Alderman MH, et al., Lancet 2000 · PMID 11130522 · negative

    Nine trials with 27,743 participants followed at least two years, in which calcium antagonists and comparators achieved similar control of both systolic and diastolic pressure. Compared with patients assigned diuretics, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors or clonidine (n=15,044), those assigned calcium antagonists (n=12,699) had a significantly higher risk of acute myocardial infarction (odds ratio 1.26, 95% CI 1.11-1.43, p=0.0003), congestive heart failure (1.25, 1.07-1.46, p=0.005) and major cardiovascular events (1.10, 1.02-1.18, p=0.018), while stroke (0.90, 0.80-1.02, p=0.10) and all-cause mortality (1.03, 0.94-1.13, p=0.54) were within the play of chance. The authors concluded that the longer-acting calcium antagonists cannot be recommended as first-line therapy for hypertension. The grade survives this because the modern head-to-head pool is far larger and does not reproduce the coronary excess - coronary heart disease versus a thiazide is RR 1.00 (0.93-1.08) at moderate certainty in the 2023 Cochrane review - while the heart-failure signal persists, as does a smaller excess of major cardiovascular events against diuretics (RR 1.05, 1.00-1.09, in the 2022 Cochrane review).

  7. Principal results of the Controlled Onset Verapamil Investigation of Cardiovascular End Points (CONVINCE) trial.
    Randomised trialBlack HR, Elliott WJ, Grandits G, et al.; CONVINCE Research Group, JAMA 2003 · PMID 12709465 · null

    A double-blind randomised trial at 661 centres in 15 countries in 16,602 hypertensive patients with one or more additional cardiovascular risk factors: 8,241 received controlled-onset extended-release verapamil 180 mg and 8,361 received either atenolol 50 mg or hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg, with other drugs added in a specified sequence if needed. Blood pressure fell 13.6/7.8 mm Hg on verapamil and 13.5/7.1 mm Hg on the comparator, so the pressure reduction was matched. There were 364 primary cardiovascular events on verapamil versus 365 on atenolol or hydrochlorothiazide (hazard ratio 1.02, 95% CI 0.88-1.18; P=.77); stroke 1.15 (0.90-1.48), myocardial infarction 0.82 (0.65-1.03), cardiovascular death 1.09 (0.87-1.37), any prespecified cardiovascular event 1.05 (0.95-1.16) and all-cause mortality 1.08 (0.93-1.26). Non-stroke haemorrhage was MORE common on verapamil, 118 versus 79 patients (hazard ratio 1.54, 1.16-2.04; P=.003). After a mean of three years of follow-up the sponsor closed the study before unblinding the results, and the trial did not demonstrate equivalence.

  8. Angiotensin II receptor blockers and myocardial infarction: an updated analysis of randomized clinical trials.
    Meta-analysisVolpe M, Tocci G, Sciarretta S, Verdecchia P, Trimarco B, Mancia G, J Hypertens 2009 · PMID 19381108 · null

    A meta-analysis of 20 major randomised trials (108,909 patients, mean age 66.5 plus or minus 4.1 years) published by 31 August 2008 that compared ARB-based treatment with other drugs or placebo and reported myocardial infarction. Over a mean 3.3 plus or minus 1.1 years there were 2,374 of 53,208 myocardial infarctions in ARB-based groups and 2,354 of 53,153 in comparator arms (odds ratio 1.008, 95% CI 0.950-1.069). Risk did not differ between ARBs and calcium channel blockers (OR 1.112, 0.971-1.272), placebo (0.944, 0.841-1.060), beta-blockers and diuretics (0.970, 0.804-1.170) or ACE inhibitors (1.008, 0.926-1.099), and did not differ across clinical settings including hypertension. Being a 2009 analysis it cannot include the later trials in the 2022 Cochrane pool, but it draws on overlapping trials and does not show the calcium-channel-blocker advantage over ARBs for myocardial infarction (RR 0.82) that the Cochrane review reports.

  9. Blood pressure lowering and risk of new-onset type 2 diabetes: an individual participant data meta-analysis.
    Network meta-analysisNazarzadeh M, Bidel Z, Canoy D, et al.; Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, Lancet 2021 · PMID 34774144 · null

    An individual participant data analysis from the Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration: 145,939 participants from 19 randomised trials in the pooled analysis and 22 trials in the network meta-analysis, with 9,883 new diagnoses of type 2 diabetes over a median 4.5 years and people with diabetes at baseline excluded. Compared with placebo, calcium channel blockers had NO material effect on new-onset type 2 diabetes (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.92-1.13), while ACE inhibitors (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.93) and ARBs (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.92) reduced it and thiazide diuretics (RR 1.20, 1.07-1.35) and beta blockers (RR 1.48, 1.27-1.72) increased it. Blood-pressure lowering itself was protective: a 5 mm Hg systolic reduction cut type 2 diabetes risk by 11% (hazard ratio 0.89, 0.84-0.95). SURROGATE ONLY - new-onset type 2 diabetes is a threshold-defined diagnosis, not a hard cardiovascular event. This is the same collaboration's dataset as the 48-trial cardiovascular analysis, read for a different question.

  10. Peripheral edema and headache associated with amlodipine treatment: a meta-analysis of randomized, placebo-controlled trials.
    Meta-analysisVukadinović D, Scholz SS, Messerli FH, Weber MA, Williams B, Böhm M, Mahfoud F, J Hypertens 2019 · PMID 31107359 · negative

    A meta-analysis of 22 randomised placebo-controlled trials (7,226 patients) reporting oedema and headache with amlodipine in arterial hypertension and stable coronary artery disease. Peripheral oedema occurred in 16.6% on amlodipine versus 6.2% on placebo (risk ratio 2.9, 95% CI 2.50-3.36, P<0.0001). The placebo-adjusted rate was 63%, so about 37% of oedema cases on amlodipine were unrelated to it. Oedema risk was lower at low and medium doses of 2.5-5 mg (RR 2.01, 1.41-2.88) than at 10 mg (RR 3.08, 2.62-3.60; P for interaction 0.03). Headache was LESS common on amlodipine than on placebo (7.9% versus 10.9%; RR 0.77, 0.65-0.90; P=0.002), driven by the low and medium doses (RR 0.52, 0.40-0.69) rather than the 10 mg dose (RR 0.92, 0.74-1.15). This is a harms and tolerability finding about one drug in the class, not evidence about blood-pressure outcomes.

  11. Comprehensive comparative effectiveness and safety of first-line antihypertensive drug classes: a systematic, multinational, large-scale analysis.
    Meta-analysisSuchard MA, Schuemie MJ, Krumholz HM, et al., Lancet 2019 · PMID 31668726 · mixed

    A new-user cohort analysis of 4.9 million patients across six administrative claims and three electronic health record databases, generating 22,000 calibrated, propensity-score-adjusted hazard ratios across all first-line classes. Most estimates revealed NO effectiveness difference between classes, supporting equivalence for initiating monotherapy, with two exceptions: thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics showed better primary effectiveness than ACE inhibitors (acute myocardial infarction HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.75-0.95; hospitalisation for heart failure 0.83, 0.74-0.95; stroke 0.83, 0.74-0.95), and the NON-DIHYDROPYRIDINE calcium channel blockers were significantly inferior to the other four classes. The background states that current guidelines recommend any primary agent among the first-line drug classes - thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers and non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers - in the absence of comorbid indications. This is OBSERVATIONAL, not randomised. Funding included Janssen Research & Development and IQVIA alongside public funders in the USA, South Korea and Australia.

  12. Pharmacological blood pressure lowering for primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease across different levels of blood pressure: an individual participant-level data meta-analysis.
    Meta-analysisBlood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, Lancet 2021 · PMID 33933205 · positive

    Individual participant data for 344,716 participants from 48 randomised trials of blood-pressure-lowering drugs versus placebo, versus other classes, or more versus less intensive regimens, assembled by the Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration. After a median 4.15 years (Q1-Q3 2.97-4.96), 42,324 participants (12.3%) had at least one major cardiovascular event. A 5 mm Hg systolic reduction lowered major cardiovascular events by about 10%: hazard ratio 0.91 (95% CI 0.89-0.94) in the 186,988 people without prior cardiovascular disease (baseline 157/89 mm Hg) and 0.89 (0.86-0.92) in the 157,728 with it (baseline 146/84 mm Hg). Event rates fell from 31.9 to 25.9 per 1000 person-years without prior cardiovascular disease and from 39.7 to 36.0 with it, with no reliable heterogeneity by prior cardiovascular disease status or across baseline systolic categories. The abstract reports NOTHING by drug class and tests heterogeneity only by those two variables, so this is a class-collective result about the millimetres removed and not evidence about calcium channel blockers. Its pool mixes placebo-controlled trials with head-to-head comparisons between classes, so it overlaps the individual trials and class reviews of blood-pressure drugs rather than corroborating them independently. The Lancet's published correction (PMID 34022983) updates only the collaboration's group list; no estimate changed.
