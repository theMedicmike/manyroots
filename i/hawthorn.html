Herbs

Hawthorn (Crataegus)

Early evidence · for high blood pressure

For high blood pressure

Hawthorn has a long history of use for heart complaints, and short trials suggest it can ease heart failure symptoms such as breathlessness and fatigue, though the one large, long trial found it did not reduce cardiac events or deaths. For blood pressure specifically the picture is weaker still: the handful of small studies that looked at it mostly found no meaningful change, and the one that did found it only in the lower number. Worth knowing before you buy: some products sold as hawthorn are combined with camphor and are actually intended to RAISE low blood pressure, so check the label carefully if your pressure is high.

Safety

Hawthorn is generally well tolerated. The 2681-patient SPICE trial found adverse events comparable between hawthorn 900 mg/day and placebo over 24 months in people with heart failure on optimal medication, which is the strongest tolerability evidence available for any herb in this node. In the Cochrane heart failure review, reported adverse events were infrequent, mild and transient, and included nausea, dizziness, and cardiac and gastrointestinal complaints; one trial in that review recorded three deaths on active treatment versus one on control, without further detail. The 16-week diabetes trial specifically looked for and found no herb-drug interaction. Dizziness is the most relevant symptom to watch for, particularly in anyone already on blood pressure or cardiac medication. Because hawthorn is used for heart conditions, self-treating cardiac symptoms with it rather than seeking assessment is the main practical risk.

Who should be careful: Check the label before buying: some commercial hawthorn preparations are fixed combinations with camphor, sold specifically to RAISE low blood pressure, and a meta-analysis of four trials confirmed they significantly increase both systolic and diastolic pressure. Anyone with high blood pressure should avoid those products. Anyone with heart failure, an arrhythmia, or known cardiac disease should use hawthorn only under clinical supervision, since it has cardioactive properties and self-treatment may delay proper assessment. Avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding due to insufficient safety data. Stop before surgery. Not a substitute for prescribed antihypertensive or heart failure therapy - the largest hawthorn trial found no reduction in cardiac events or mortality when added to optimal heart failure medication.

Interactions: Potential additive effects with cardiac glycosides such as digoxin, and with beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, nitrates and other cardiovascular drugs; supervision is advised for anyone on these. Additive blood-pressure-lowering effect with antihypertensive medication, and conversely an opposing, pressure-raising effect if the product is a hawthorn-camphor combination. The one trial that formally assessed herb-drug interaction, in patients taking a mean of 4.4 hypoglycaemic and/or hypotensive drugs, found none over 16 weeks, which is reassuring but not definitive.

What the evidence says

The hypertension evidence base is small and largely negative. A randomised crossover dose-ranging trial in 21 prehypertensive or mildly hypertensive adults tested 1000, 1500 and 2500 mg of standardised hawthorn extract and found no dose-response effect on flow-mediated dilation (the primary outcome) or on blood pressure (a secondary outcome); each dosing period lasted only 3.5 days, which limits what the null can rule out. A randomised double-blind pilot in 36 mildly hypertensive subjects taking 500 mg for 10 weeks found blood pressure declined in all groups including placebo, with ANOVA providing no evidence of difference between treatments; only a non-significant trend toward diastolic reduction (P=0.081) was noted. The most cited positive trial randomised 79 patients with type 2 diabetes to 1200 mg hawthorn or placebo for 16 weeks and found a significant group difference in diastolic reduction (P=0.035, hawthorn falling 85.6 to 83.0 mmHg while placebo rose 84.5 to 85.0 mmHg) but NO significant systolic difference (3.6 vs 0.8 mmHg, P=0.329). An older 92-participant trial of Crataegus curvisepala reported significant systolic and diastolic decreases after three months but did not report effect sizes. Running against all of this, a meta-analysis of four RCTs (221 patients) of a fixed hawthorn-and-camphor combination found it significantly INCREASED both systolic and diastolic blood pressure versus placebo and is used therapeutically for hypotension. In chronic heart failure, a different condition, a Cochrane review of 14 trials found improvements in maximal workload and symptoms, but the largest trial ever run on hawthorn, in 2681 heart failure patients over 24 months, found no significant effect on time to first cardiac event or on cardiac mortality.

Doses studied: 1200 mg/day standardised hawthorn extract for 16 weeks (the trial showing a diastolic-only effect); 500 mg/day for 10 weeks (null); 1000, 1500 and 2500 mg/day standardised to 50 mg oligomeric procyanidin per 250 mg extract, taken twice daily for only 3.5 days per arm (null, no dose-response). The SPICE heart failure trial used 900 mg/day of the standardised leaf-and-flower extract WS 1442 for 24 months as an add-on to optimal medication. Heart failure trials generally used standardised leaf-and-flower extracts as adjuncts to conventional therapy.

Why this grade: Human trials of hawthorn for blood pressure are few, small (21 to 92 participants, roughly 228 in total) and predominantly null or borderline. The two best-designed trials found no effect: a crossover dose-ranging study found no dose-response on blood pressure, and a pilot RCT found no difference between treatments by ANOVA. The one trial reporting benefit found it for diastolic pressure only, with no systolic difference. Hawthorn's substantive evidence base lies in chronic heart failure symptoms, a different condition, and even there the largest trial ever conducted (2681 patients over 24 months) found no significant effect on cardiac events or mortality. A meta-analysis of a hawthorn-camphor combination product found it significantly RAISES blood pressure. The grade is 'early' rather than 'not_supported' only because the hypertension evidence base is too small to establish a null with confidence, not because the existing signal is encouraging.

Sources (8)
  1. The efficacy and safety of Crataegus extract WS 1442 in patients with heart failure: the SPICE trial.
    Holubarsch CJF, Colucci WS, Meinertz T, Gaus W, Tendera M, 2008 · PMID 19019730 · null

    The largest hawthorn trial ever conducted: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2681 adults with NYHA class II-III chronic heart failure and LVEF <=35% given 900 mg/day WS 1442 or placebo for 24 months on top of optimal medication. Average time to first cardiac event was 620 days for WS 1442 versus 606 days for placebo (event rates 27.9% and 28.9%; HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.82 to 1.10; p=0.476) - NO significant effect on the primary endpoint. The cardiac mortality trend (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.73 to 1.09) was not statistically significant (p=0.269). A reduction in sudden cardiac death appeared only in a post-hoc subgroup with LVEF >=25% (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.37 to 0.94, p=0.025). Adverse events were comparable in both groups.

  2. The combination of hawthorn extract and camphor significantly increases blood pressure: A meta-analysis and systematic review.
    Csupor D, Viczian R, Lantos T, Kiss T, Hegyi P, Toth B et al., 2019 · PMID 31255877 · negative

    Meta-analysis of four randomised placebo-controlled trials (221 patients) of a fixed combination of hawthorn extract and camphor used for decades in the therapy of HYPOTENSION. The combination significantly INCREASED both systolic and diastolic blood pressure versus placebo (p=0.017 and 0.049 respectively). Authors concluded the blood-pressure-raising effect is confirmed although the supporting evidence is very limited and the optimum dose and duration remain unclear. The camphor component may drive the effect, but the result means hawthorn-containing combination products cannot be assumed to lower blood pressure.

  3. Effect of hawthorn standardized extract on flow mediated dilation in prehypertensive and mildly hypertensive adults: a randomized, controlled cross-over trial.
    Asher GN, Viera AJ, Weaver MA, Dominik R, Caughey M, Hinderliter AL, 2012 · PMID 22458601 · null

    Four-period crossover trial in 21 prehypertensive or mildly hypertensive adults comparing placebo against 1000, 1500 and 2500 mg hawthorn extract standardised to 50 mg oligomeric procyanidin per 250 mg. There was no evidence of a dose-response effect for the primary outcome (flow-mediated dilation percent) or for any secondary outcome including blood pressure. Important limitation: each dose was taken twice daily for only 3.5 days before measurement, with a 4-day washout, and blood pressure was a secondary not primary endpoint - so this null cannot exclude an effect emerging over weeks. Authors concluded that if hawthorn has a blood pressure lowering effect it is likely mediated by a nitric-oxide-independent mechanism.

  4. Promising hypotensive effect of hawthorn extract: a randomized double-blind pilot study of mild, essential hypertension.
    Walker AF, Marakis G, Morris AP, Robinson PA, 2002 · PMID 11807965 · null

    Randomised double-blind pilot in 36 mildly hypertensive subjects assigned to 600 mg magnesium, 500 mg hawthorn extract, both, or placebo for 10 weeks. Systolic and diastolic pressure declined in all treatment groups INCLUDING placebo, and ANOVA provided no evidence of difference between treatments. Only a factorial contrast analysis showed a non-significant trend toward reduced resting diastolic pressure at week 10 in the 19 subjects taking hawthorn (p=0.081), alongside a non-significant trend toward reduced anxiety (p=0.094).

  5. Hypotensive effects of hawthorn for patients with diabetes taking prescription drugs: a randomised controlled trial.
    Walker AF, Marakis G, Simpson E, Hope JL, Robinson PA, Hassanein M, Simpson HCR, 2006 · PMID 16762125 · mixed

    79 patients with type 2 diabetes randomised to 1200 mg/day hawthorn extract (n=39) or placebo (n=40) for 16 weeks; 71% were taking hypotensive drugs, with a mean intake of 4.4 hypoglycaemic and/or hypotensive drugs. There was a significant group difference in mean diastolic reduction (P=0.035): hawthorn fell from 85.6 to 83.0 mmHg while placebo rose from 84.5 to 85.0 mmHg. However there was NO group difference in systolic reduction (3.6 mmHg hawthorn vs 0.8 mmHg placebo, P=0.329). No herb-drug interaction was found and minor health complaints were reduced from baseline in both groups.

  6. Antihypertensive effect of Iranian Crataegus curvisepala Lind.: a randomized, double-blind study.
    Asgary S, Naderi GH, Sadeghi M, Kelishadi R, Amiri M, 2004 · PMID 15700749 · positive

    Double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 92 men and women aged 40-60 with primary mild hypertension, given hydroalcoholic extract of Crataegus curvisepala leaves and flowers three times daily for more than 4 months. Reported a significant decrease in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure after 3 months (p<0.05), described as a time-dependent antihypertensive effect. Effect sizes in mmHg were NOT reported in the abstract, so the magnitude cannot be assessed; the analysis used Student's t-test only.

  7. Hawthorn extract for treating chronic heart failure.
    Pittler MH, Guo R, Ernst E (Cochrane), 2008 · PMID 18254076 · positive

    Cochrane review of 14 double-blind RCTs (10 pooled, 855 patients with NYHA class I-III chronic heart failure, NOT hypertension) found hawthorn superior to placebo for maximal workload (WMD 5.35 Watts, 95% CI 0.71 to 10.00, n=380), exercise tolerance, the pressure-heart rate product, and symptoms such as breathlessness and fatigue (WMD -5.47, 95% CI -8.68 to -2.26, n=239). No mortality or cardiac event data were reported apart from one trial that recorded deaths (three on active treatment, one on control) without further detail. This establishes hawthorn's evidence base in heart failure symptoms rather than blood pressure lowering; reported adverse events were infrequent, mild and transient, including nausea, dizziness, and cardiac and gastrointestinal complaints.

  8. Effect of vitamin C and hawthorn beverage formula on blood pressure and oxidative stress in heat-exposed workers: a cluster-randomized controlled trial.
    Du W, Zhang S, Yang J, Fan H, 2024 · PMID 39209360 · mixed

    Cluster-randomised trial in heat-exposed workers reported systolic reduction of 7.41 mmHg and diastolic of 7.93 mmHg versus control over 41 days. However the intervention combined a 500 mL hawthorn beverage (278.7 mg flavonoids) WITH a 130 mg vitamin C tablet against slightly salted water, so the hawthorn contribution cannot be isolated; the comparator was not an inert placebo, only four shift-teams were randomised, and the setting was occupational heat exposure rather than established hypertension.
