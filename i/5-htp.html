Minerals & nutrients

5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan)

Early evidence · for insomnia

For insomnia

5-HTP is a building block your body uses to make serotonin, and from there melatonin, so the idea that it might help sleep makes biological sense. But the actual human testing is thin: one small 12-week study in older adults found the clearest benefit in the people who were sleeping badly to begin with, and that study had no placebo group. It may turn out to help, but nobody has yet run the kind of trial that would tell us for sure, and there is an unresolved question about contaminants in commercial products.

Safety

Generally well tolerated in the short trials conducted at the doses studied. Nausea and other gastrointestinal upset are the most commonly reported effects. In a 28-day randomised double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial of 100 mg/day of a Griffonia simplicifolia extract (whose main constituent is 5-HTP) in 18 healthy volunteers, mild transient adverse events occurred in two participants (11%); that same trial found a significant increase in the distance of mechanical allodynia on active treatment (beta = 0.82, 95% CI 0.05-1.59, p = 0.038), which the authors interpret as possible serotonergic facilitation of pain pathways — a signal worth knowing about even though it was not a sleep study. Long-term safety beyond about 12 weeks has not been established, and 5-HTP has not been studied in pregnancy or breastfeeding. A genuine unresolved product-quality question hangs over 5-HTP: a contaminant designated 'Peak X' was chemically identified as 4,5-tryptophan-dione, a putative neurotoxin, and detected in six over-the-counter samples tested in one analysis, though a later review by authors at a commercial laboratory argued this was an analytical artefact. Because supplements are not pre-approved for purity, product sourcing matters.

Who should be careful: Do not use alongside serotonergic medication without a clinician's supervision. Avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding (unstudied). People with Parkinson's disease taking carbidopa-levodopa, and anyone with a serotonin-related condition such as carcinoid syndrome, should not use 5-HTP without specialist advice. Persistent insomnia should be evaluated for underlying causes rather than self-treated.

Interactions: The following reflect established pharmacology rather than findings from the trials cited above. 5-HTP raises serotonin and should not be combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, triptans, tramadol, lithium or St John's wort without medical supervision, because of the risk of serotonin syndrome. It is decarboxylated outside the brain, so it interacts with carbidopa and other peripheral decarboxylase inhibitors used in Parkinson's disease. Additive sedation is possible with alcohol and other sedatives. Formal interaction studies are lacking.

What the evidence says

Evidence for 5-HTP in insomnia and poor sleep in adults is preliminary. The most directly relevant trial (Sutanto 2024) was a 12-week single-blind randomised study in 30 older adults (66 +/- 3 years) comparing 100 mg/day 5-HTP against no supplement, with no placebo arm; the authors report an overall favourable effect on certain sleep-quality components and a rise in serum serotonin, but the significant improvement in subjective global sleep score was confined to participants classified as poor sleepers (reported change -2.80 +/- 1.10, p = 0.005), who also showed greater gut microbiota diversity (Simpson 0.037 +/- 0.032 vs -0.007 +/- 0.022, p = 0.013). Bruni 2004 gave L-5-HTP 2 mg/kg/day for 20 days in an open, unblinded trial in children with sleep terrors: 29/31 (93.5%) responded at one month versus 4/14 (28.6%) untreated, and 26/31 (83.9%) were sleep-terror-free at six months — but sleep terrors are a childhood parasomnia, not adult insomnia. In a double-blind crossover trial in 18 people with Parkinson's disease and REM sleep behaviour disorder (Meloni 2022), 50 mg/day increased the percentage of stage REM sleep but produced only a marginal, non-significant reduction in arousal index and wake after sleep onset. The one trial reporting a clean sleep-latency benefit (Shell 2010: 32.3 to 19.1 minutes, p = 0.01; between-group p = 0.02) tested a combination product containing both GABA and 5-HTP in 18 people and was authored from the manufacturer, so it cannot be attributed to 5-HTP alone. Against these, a 2026 randomised pilot comparing melatonin plus 5-HTP against melatonin plus L-tryptophan in children with neurodevelopmental disorders found no statistically significant between-group differences in any sleep parameter (13 of 26 enrolled completed). A dedicated PubMed search conducted for this entry located no adequately powered double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 5-HTP by itself for adult insomnia.

Doses studied: 100 mg/day for 12 weeks (adults, sleep quality); 50 mg/day for 4 weeks (Parkinson's REM sleep behaviour disorder); 2 mg/kg/day at bedtime for 20 days (children, sleep terrors); 50 mg twice daily within a GABA/5-HTP combination product; 100 mg/day for 28 days of a Griffonia simplicifolia extract in a healthy-volunteer pain trial

Why this grade: Human sleep trials exist but are few, small and methodologically weak. The only trial testing 5-HTP against adult sleep quality was single-blind with no placebo arm (n=30), and its significant global sleep-score gain was confined to a poor-sleeper subgroup. The other supporting studies are an open-label paediatric study of sleep terrors (a different condition and population), a small crossover in Parkinson's REM sleep behaviour disorder, and an industry-authored combination product that also contained GABA. A 2026 randomised paediatric trial of a melatonin/5-HTP combination found no significant between-group difference. A fresh PubMed search performed for this review (38 records) located no adequately powered double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 5-HTP alone for adult insomnia. That is the definition of preliminary, so 'early' rather than 'moderate'. 'Traditional' was considered and rejected because 5-HTP is a modern isolated compound rather than a documented historical remedy, and a randomised adult sleep-quality trial does exist.

Sources (8)
  1. The impact of 5-hydroxytryptophan supplementation on sleep quality and gut microbiota composition in older adults: A randomized controlled trial.
    Sutanto CN, Xia X, Heng CW, et al., 2024 · PMID 38309227 · mixed

    12-week single-blind randomised trial, 30 older adults (66 +/- 3 y), 100 mg/day 5-HTP vs no supplement (no placebo). Authors report an overall favourable effect on certain sleep-quality components and increased serum serotonin, but the significant improvement in subjective global sleep score was confined to poor sleepers (reported change -2.80 +/- 1.10, p = 0.005); good sleepers did not show this. Poor sleepers also showed increased microbiota diversity (Simpson 0.037 +/- 0.032 vs -0.007 +/- 0.022, p = 0.013).

  2. Effect of melatonin enriched with L-Tryptophan and 5-Hydroxytryptophan on sleep parameters in children with neurodevelopmental disorders.
    Cruz-Sanabria F, Cenerini G, Bruno S, Ferri R, Fiori S, Faraguna U, 2026 · PMID 41643230 · null

    Single-centre randomised pilot comparative trial; 51 children screened, 26 enrolled, 13 completed (melatonin+5-HTP: 9; melatonin+L-tryptophan: 4). NO statistically significant between-group differences in change scores were observed on actigraphy. A within-group reduction in Sleep Movement Index occurred in the melatonin+5-HTP completers (6.55 to 1.25, p = 0.006), which the authors call hypothesis-generating given small sample size, high attrition and limited power.

  3. Preliminary finding of a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 5-hydroxytryptophan on REM sleep behavior disorder in Parkinson's disease.
    Meloni M, Figorilli M, Carta M, et al., 2022 · PMID 34403081 · mixed

    Double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial, 18 patients with Parkinson's disease and REM sleep behaviour disorder, 50 mg/day 5-HTP over 4 weeks. 5-HTP increased the total percentage of stage REM sleep but produced only a marginal, non-significant reduction in arousal index and wake after sleep onset. Different condition and population from adult insomnia.

  4. L-5-Hydroxytryptophan treatment of sleep terrors in children.
    Bruni O, Ferri R, Miano S, Verrillo E, 2004 · PMID 15146330 · positive

    Open (unblinded) pharmacological trial in 45 children aged 3.2-10.6 years; 31 received L-5-HTP 2 mg/kg/day at bedtime for 20 days. 29/31 (93.5%) responded at 1 month vs 4/14 (28.6%) in the untreated comparison group; 26/31 (83.9%) were sleep-terror-free at 6 months. Sleep terrors are a childhood parasomnia, not adult insomnia, and the design was open-label.

  5. A randomized, placebo-controlled trial of an amino acid preparation on timing and quality of sleep.
    Shell W, Bullias D, Charuvastra E, May LA, Silver DS, 2010 · PMID 19417589 · positive

    Double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 18 participants (9 active, 9 placebo), of a combination amino acid formula containing both GABA and 5-hydroxytryptophan. Sleep latency fell from 32.3 to 19.1 minutes on active treatment (p = 0.01) vs 34.8 to 33.1 on placebo (between-group p = 0.02); sleep duration rose from 5.0 to 6.83 hours. The effect cannot be attributed to 5-HTP alone, and the first author's affiliation is the company that makes the product.

  6. The Effect of Griffonia simplicifolia on Pain Intensity, Central and Peripheral Sensitization, and Pain Modulation in Healthy Volunteers-A Randomized, Double-Blinded, Placebo-Controlled Crossover Trial.
    Schlemmer AJ, Hammer S, Fandler-Hofler S, Lang-Illievich K, Bornemann-Cimenti H, 2026 · PMID 42197069 · mixed

    Randomised double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial, 18 healthy volunteers (17 completers), 100 mg/day of a Griffonia simplicifolia extract (5-HTP is its main constituent) for 28 days. No significant effect on acute pain perception. The one significant difference was an INCREASED distance of mechanical allodynia on active treatment (beta = 0.82, 95% CI 0.05-1.59, p = 0.038), which the authors suggest may reflect serotonergic facilitation of pronociceptive pathways and enhanced sensitization. Mild, transient adverse events occurred in two participants (11%). Note: PubMed's plain-text record strips the italicised species name from the title.

  7. Structural characterization of a case-implicated contaminant, "Peak X," in commercial preparations of 5-hydroxytryptophan.
    Klarskov K, Johnson KL, Benson LM, et al., 2003 · PMID 12508395 · negative

    The contaminant 'Peak X' found in eosinophilia-myalgia-syndrome case-implicated 5-hydroxytryptophan was identified by mass spectrometry as 4,5-tryptophan-dione, a putative neurotoxin. It was also detected in 6 over-the-counter 5-HTP samples tested, at 0.5% to 10.3% of the amount present in the case-implicated material. The authors state this gives some cause for concern about the safety of commercial preparations.

  8. Safety of 5-hydroxy-L-tryptophan.
    Das YT, Bagchi M, Bagchi D, Preuss HG, 2004 · PMID 15068828 · mixed

    Safety review concluding that no definitive cases of 5-HTP toxicity emerged over roughly 20 years of worldwide use, with the possible exception of one unresolved Canadian case, and arguing that the 'Peak X' signal reflects chromatographic artefact at negligible concentrations. Directly contradicts the Klarskov analysis, so the contamination question remains unsettled. The lead author's affiliation is a commercial laboratory (ISSI Laboratories Inc.), which readers should weigh.
