Conventional medicine

Thiazide diuretics

Strong evidence · for high blood pressure

For high blood pressure

ImportantOn a thiazide, sodium reduction (graded strong here) adds to a low-sodium risk concentrated in the first 30 days, and with an ACE inhibitor or ARB, potassium supplements or salt substitutes can push potassium dangerously high. Agree these with the prescriber.

Thiazide diuretics are water pills, and for clearly raised pressure they are the best-tested blood pressure drugs there are: against placebo they cut deaths, strokes, heart attacks and overall heart events - all four, on the highest certainty Cochrane awards. Those trials enrolled moderate to severe hypertension; in milder, lower-risk hypertension only a possible stroke reduction has been shown, which this page grades separately. No class has beaten them head to head on deaths, though one trial beat a thiazide combination. The cost is real: roughly twice as many stop it for side effects as a placebo, it pushes sodium and potassium down, and it makes a type 2 diabetes diagnosis more likely.

Safety

These are prescription drugs, and no one should start, change or stop them from a website. Tolerability is the weak point: withdrawals rose from 5.0% on control to 11.3% on low-dose thiazides (RR 2.38) and from 2.2% to 9.8% on high dose (RR 4.48) (PMID 29667175), and in largely-thiazide trials in adults 60 or older 16% withdrew on treatment versus 5.4% on control (RR 2.91; low-certainty) (PMID 41065416). Increased urination, low potassium, low sodium, raised uric acid and gout, dizziness and orthostatic hypotension are the common effects, so electrolytes and kidney function are normally rechecked after a dose change and during vomiting, diarrhoea or heat. Low sodium is commoner than labels suggest: two-year cumulative incidence of sodium below 130 mmol/L was 3.83% on bendroflumethiazide and 3.51% on a hydrochlorothiazide combination - excesses of 1.35% and 1.38% over non-thiazide comparators, concentrated in the first 30 days and larger with age and comorbidity (PMID 38109740). Chlorthalidone caused more hypokalaemia than hydrochlorothiazide (6.0% versus 4.4%) (PMID 36516076). Beyond the heart: new-onset type 2 diabetes diagnoses rose (RR 1.20) (PMID 34774144), and hydrochlorothiazide specifically was linked to cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma against other thiazides (HR 1.50, 1.06-2.11), rising with duration and dose (PMID 33104975) - sun protection and skin checks are the response. Seek urgent care for fainting, confusion, severe weakness or cramps with palpitations.

Who should be careful: Avoided in significant hyponatraemia or hypokalaemia until corrected, in symptomatic hyperuricaemia or active gout, in Addison's disease, and in anyone with a severe sulfonamide-derived drug allergy. Low kidney function is a nuance, not a stop sign: in 160 people with stage 4 chronic kidney disease, mean eGFR 23.2, three-fifths already on a loop diuretic, chlorthalidone lowered 24-hour systolic pressure by 10.5 mm Hg more than placebo over 12 weeks - SURROGATE ONLY, no events measured - with more hypokalaemia, reversible creatinine rise, raised blood sugar, dizziness and raised uric acid (PMID 34739197), so a thiazide can still work at that level of function, but dosing there is a specialist decision and no outcome benefit has been shown. Use caution in diabetes and prediabetes given the documented rise in new-onset type 2 diabetes, in older people and those with more comorbidity, who carry the largest excess risk of low sodium, in anyone volume-depleted from vomiting, diarrhoea or heat, and in pregnancy, where they are not a preferred agent. Chlorthalidone and hydrochlorothiazide are not interchangeable milligram for milligram, and a randomised switch between them produced no outcome gain and more hypokalaemia. Anyone with resistant, secondary or pulmonary hypertension, or who is pregnant, needs an individualised assessment this page does not cover.

Interactions: Three interventions graded on this same page interact with a thiazide directly. Potassium (graded strong here) is often the answer to thiazide-induced low potassium, but potassium supplements and potassium-based salt substitutes overshoot into dangerous hyperkalaemia when a thiazide is combined with an ACE inhibitor or ARB, so potassium is replaced to a measured level rather than taken freely. Magnesium (moderate here) is depleted by thiazides too, and low magnesium makes low potassium refractory to replacement. Sodium reduction (strong here) added to a thiazide is the everyday route to low sodium and volume depletion, especially in hot weather or illness. Beyond the page: NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen blunt the blood-pressure effect and, with a thiazide plus an ACE inhibitor or ARB, raise the risk of acute kidney injury. Thiazides raise lithium levels, sometimes to toxicity. Low potassium increases digoxin toxicity and the risk of dangerous rhythms with QT-prolonging drugs such as sotalol and amiodarone; corticosteroids and stimulant laxatives deepen the loss. Carbamazepine, SSRIs and desmopressin add to the risk of low sodium. Thiazides reduce calcium excretion and raise serum calcium, which matters alongside calcium or high-dose vitamin D. Alcohol, alpha-blockers, nitrates and PDE5 inhibitors such as sildenafil add to the hypotensive effect. Licorice root both raises blood pressure and drives potassium down. Disclose every supplement to the prescriber.

What the evidence says

Hard clinical events throughout this summary, except the diabetes harm - a threshold diagnosis, SURROGATE ONLY. PLACEBO LIMB: the 2018 Cochrane first-line review pooled 24 trials with 28 active treatment arms and 58,040 patients ACROSS FOUR CLASSES - none were found for ARBs or alpha-blockers; the thiazide estimates come from its low-dose arms, not the whole pool, and apply to moderate to severe hypertension. Those arms reduced mortality (11.0% control versus 9.8% treatment; RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.82-0.97), total cardiovascular events (12.9% versus 9.0%; RR 0.70, 0.64-0.76), stroke (6.2% versus 4.2%; RR 0.68, 0.60-0.77) and coronary heart disease (3.9% versus 2.8%; RR 0.72, 0.61-0.84) on HIGH-quality evidence, while high-dose arms cut stroke and cardiovascular events but not mortality (RR 0.90, 0.76-1.05) or coronary heart disease (RR 1.01, 0.85-1.20) (PMID 29667175). SHEP is of the kind pooled there, not independent of it: five-year stroke fell from 8.2 to 5.2 per 100 in 4,736 adults 60 or older on a chlorthalidone-BASED regimen with an atenolol step (RR 0.64) (PMID 2046107). The 2025 Cochrane review in adults 60 or older, most of whose trials used a first-line thiazide, found all-cause mortality RR 0.91 (0.85-0.97) on high-certainty evidence from 13 studies and 25,932 participants - of 16 trials and 26,795 included - possibly confined to ages 60-79 (RR 0.86, 0.79-0.95) rather than 80 or older (RR 0.97, 0.87-1.10) (PMID 41065416). Both reviews select first-line placebo-or-no-treatment trials and share authors, so they are not independent. HEAD-TO-HEAD LIMB, comparator figure first: versus calcium channel blockers thiazides gave mortality parity (RR 1.02, 0.96-1.08), fewer total cardiovascular events (14.3% versus 13.3%; RR 0.93, 0.89-0.98) and less heart failure (4.4% versus 3.2%; RR 0.74, 0.66-0.82) but no advantage on stroke (RR 1.06, 0.95-1.18) or coronary heart disease (RR 1.00, 0.93-1.08); versus ACE inhibitors, mortality parity (RR 1.00, 0.95-1.07) and slightly less stroke (4.7% versus 4.1%; RR 0.89, 0.80-0.99) but LITTLE TO NO DIFFERENCE in total cardiovascular events (RR 0.97, 0.92-1.02) (PMID 37439548). A different team reading from the calcium-channel-blocker side agreed: versus diuretics they probably increased major cardiovascular events (RR 1.05, 1.00-1.09) and heart failure (RR 1.37, 1.25-1.51) (PMID 35000192). In ALLHAT chlorthalidone matched amlodipine and lisinopril on the primary coronary outcome and on death, but beat amlodipine on heart failure (10.2% versus 7.7%) and lisinopril on combined cardiovascular disease, stroke and heart failure (PMID 12479763). The debits: a thiazide combination LOST to an amlodipine one (PMID 19052124); switching between thiazides changed nothing (PMID 36516076); in untreated MILD hypertension drug therapy has not been shown to reduce death, total cardiovascular events or coronary disease, though stroke may fall (PMID 41925078); and new-onset type 2 diabetes diagnoses rose (PMID 34774144).

Doses studied: Low dose is the dose the outcome evidence supports. ALLHAT used chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/day (PMID 12479763). SHEP used chlorthalidone 12.5 then 25 mg/day with atenolol 25-50 mg/day as step two, so its result belongs to a regimen, not to chlorthalidone alone (PMID 2046107). The Diuretic Comparison Project ran chlorthalidone 12.5 or 25 mg/day against hydrochlorothiazide 25 or 50 mg/day, with no outcome difference (PMID 36516076). In stage 4 chronic kidney disease it was titrated from 12.5 to 50 mg/day for 12 weeks - SURROGATE ONLY (PMID 34739197). Against placebo, high-dose thiazides did not reduce mortality or coronary heart disease - in outcome terms a different drug (PMID 29667175).

Why this grade: Scoped to thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics at LOW dose as a first-choice drug, and the placebo evidence is for MODERATE TO SEVERE primary hypertension - Cochrane's own wording, with the over-60 pool averaging 182/95 mm Hg. Whether a milder reading should be treated at all, and how low to aim, are graded separately on this page. Inside that scope the grade is earned on two limbs. Against placebo or no treatment, the low-dose thiazide arms reduced all-cause mortality (11.0% on control versus 9.8% on treatment; RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.82-0.97), stroke (6.2% versus 4.2%; RR 0.68, 0.60-0.77), coronary heart disease (3.9% versus 2.8%; RR 0.72, 0.61-0.84) and total cardiovascular events (12.9% versus 9.0%; RR 0.70, 0.64-0.76) - all four, on HIGH-quality evidence, which no other class achieves (PMID 29667175) - and in adults 60 or older, where most trials used a first-line thiazide, all-cause mortality fell again on high-certainty evidence (RR 0.91, 0.85-0.97) (PMID 41065416). Head to head across 20 active-comparator trials in over 90,000 participants, no class showed any clinically important advantage over first-line thiazides: mortality parity against calcium channel blockers (RR 1.02, 0.96-1.08) and ACE inhibitors (RR 1.00, 0.95-1.07), fewer total cardiovascular events than calcium channel blockers (RR 0.93, 0.89-0.98) and alpha-blockers (RR 0.74, 0.69-0.80) but LITTLE TO NO DIFFERENCE against ACE inhibitors (RR 0.97, 0.92-1.02), and fewer withdrawals for adverse effects than all four classes tested (PMID 37439548). Guidelines place thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics among the accepted first-line classes rather than above them (PMID 31668726). The debits sit inside the grade. Dose is not a detail: high-dose thiazides reduced stroke and cardiovascular events but not mortality (RR 0.90, 0.76-1.05) or coronary heart disease (RR 1.01, 0.85-1.20). Tolerability is the cost - withdrawals rose from 5.0% to 11.3% at low dose (RR 2.38, 2.06-2.75) and from 2.2% to 9.8% at high dose (RR 4.48, 3.83-5.24). The class raises new-onset type 2 diabetes diagnoses (RR 1.20, 1.07-1.35) where ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower them (PMID 34774144). And one randomised trial beat a thiazide combination outright (HR 0.80, 0.72-0.90), on an ACE-inhibitor background and stopped early (PMID 19052124). Two caveats span the grade: four of the five Cochrane reviews cited here share authors from the Cochrane Hypertension group, the calcium-channel-blocker review being the exception, and the finding that benefit tracks the millimetres removed is class-collective rather than a thiazide result.

  1. First-line drugs for hypertension.
    Meta-analysisWright JM, Musini VM, Gill R, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2018 · PMID 29667175 · positive

    The review pooled 24 trials with 28 active treatment arms and 58,040 patients ACROSS FOUR DRUG CLASSES (the review sought six and found no trials for ARBs or alpha-blockers), comparing a first-line class with placebo or no treatment; no randomised trials were found for ARBs or alpha-blockers. Mean age was 56 and follow-up three to five years, and the reviewers state the results are mostly applicable to adults with moderate to severe primary hypertension. The low-dose thiazide arms reduced all four outcomes on HIGH-quality evidence: mortality 11.0% with control versus 9.8% with treatment (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.82-0.97), total cardiovascular events 12.9% versus 9.0% (RR 0.70, 0.64-0.76), stroke 6.2% versus 4.2% (RR 0.68, 0.60-0.77) and coronary heart disease 3.9% versus 2.8% (RR 0.72, 0.61-0.84). High-dose thiazide arms reduced stroke (1.9% versus 0.9%; RR 0.47, 0.37-0.61) and total cardiovascular events (5.1% versus 3.7%; RR 0.72, 0.63-0.82) but did NOT reduce mortality (3.1% versus 2.8%; RR 0.90, 0.76-1.05) or coronary heart disease (2.7% versus 2.7%; RR 1.01, 0.85-1.20). Withdrawals due to adverse effects rose from 5.0% with control to 11.3% with low-dose treatment (RR 2.38, 2.06-2.75) and from 2.2% with control to 9.8% with high-dose treatment (RR 4.48, 3.83-5.24), both on low-quality evidence. First-line ACE inhibitors also reduced all four outcomes but on low- to moderate-quality evidence (mortality RR 0.83, 0.72-0.95), and the reviewers conclude high-dose thiazides and beta-blockers were inferior to first-line low-dose thiazides.

  2. Pharmacotherapy for hypertension in adults 60 years or older.
    Meta-analysisMusini VM, Tejani AM, Bassett K, Puil L, Thompson W, Wright JM, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2025 · PMID 41065416 · positive

    16 randomised trials (26,795 participants) of antihypertensive drug therapy against placebo or no treatment in healthy ambulatory adults 60 years or older (mean age 73.8) with moderate to severe hypertension averaging 182/95 mm Hg; MOST of these trials evaluated first-line thiazide diuretic therapy, mean treatment 3.8 years. Treatment reduced all-cause mortality (10% with treatment versus 11% with control; RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.85-0.97; 13 studies, 25,932 participants; HIGH-certainty evidence) and probably reduced cardiovascular morbidity and mortality (10% versus 14%; RR 0.72, 0.68-0.77; 15 studies), cerebrovascular morbidity and mortality (3.4% versus 5.2%; RR 0.66, 0.59-0.74; 13 studies) and coronary morbidity and mortality (3.7% versus 4.8%; RR 0.78, 0.69-0.88; 11 studies). Withdrawals due to adverse effects may have increased (16% with treatment versus 5.4% with control; RR 2.91, 2.56-3.30; 4 studies, 11,310 participants; low-certainty). The mortality reduction may be largely confined to ages 60-79 (RR 0.86, 0.79-0.95) rather than 80 or older (RR 0.97, 0.87-1.10), though the subgroup test showed no evidence of a difference. A sensitivity analysis restricted to the three trials in people with isolated systolic hypertension found similar benefits; the abstract does not name those trials. No new or ongoing trials were identified and the review is closed. Like the 2018 first-line review it selects trials of first-line therapy against placebo or no treatment and shares authors with it, so the two are not independent replications.

  3. First-line diuretics versus other classes of antihypertensive drugs for hypertension.
    Narrative reviewReinhart M, Puil L, Salzwedel DM, Wright JM, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2023 · PMID 37439548 · mixed

    20 randomised active-comparator trials with 26 comparator arms randomising over 90,000 participants; the reviewers state the findings are relevant to older male and female hypertensive patients aged 50 to 75 with multiple comorbidities including type 2 diabetes, which is an applicability statement rather than the randomised age range. Versus CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS: little to no difference in total mortality (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.96-1.08; 7 trials, 35,417; moderate-certainty); thiazides probably reduced total cardiovascular events (14.3% on a calcium channel blocker versus 13.3% on a thiazide; RR 0.93, 0.89-0.98; absolute risk reduction 1.0%) and heart failure (4.4% versus 3.2%; RR 0.74, 0.66-0.82; ARR 1.2%); little to no difference in stroke (RR 1.06, 0.95-1.18), coronary heart disease (RR 1.00, 0.93-1.08) or serious adverse events (RR 1.09, 0.97-1.24; low-certainty); fewer withdrawals for adverse effects (7.6% versus 6.2%; RR 0.81, 0.75-0.88). Versus ACE INHIBITORS: little to no difference in total mortality (RR 1.00, 0.95-1.07; 3 trials, 30,961), in TOTAL CARDIOVASCULAR EVENTS (RR 0.97, 0.92-1.02; 3 trials, 30,900; low-certainty, Cochrane's wording 'may result in little to no difference'), in coronary heart disease (RR 1.03, 0.96-1.12) or heart failure (RR 0.94, 0.84-1.04); stroke probably reduced slightly (4.7% versus 4.1%; RR 0.89, 0.80-0.99); fewer withdrawals (3.9% versus 2.9%; RR 0.73, 0.64-0.84). Versus BETA-BLOCKERS: mortality RR 0.96 (0.84-1.10), total cardiovascular events RR 0.88 (0.78-1.00, interval touching unity), fewer withdrawals (10.1% versus 7.9%; RR 0.78, 0.71-0.85). Versus ALPHA-BLOCKERS: total cardiovascular events 12.1% versus 9.0% (RR 0.74, 0.69-0.80), heart failure 5.4% versus 2.8% (RR 0.51, 0.45-0.58), fewer withdrawals (RR 0.70, 0.54-0.89). Data were insufficient for the other classes, and only three studies reported total serious adverse events. The reviewers conclude that no antihypertensive drug class demonstrated any clinically important advantages over first-line thiazides. Percentages are given comparator-first, as the review reports them.

  4. Calcium channel blockers versus other classes of drugs for hypertension.
    Systematic reviewZhu J, Chen N, Zhou M, Guo J, Zhu C, Zhou J, Ma M, He L, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2022 · PMID 35000192 · mixed

    23 randomised trials (18 dihydropyridine, 4 non-dihydropyridine, 1 unspecified) with 153,849 hypertensive participants, each comparing a first-line calcium channel blocker with another class over at least two years. All-cause mortality did not differ between first-line calcium channel blockers and any other class. Compared with DIURETICS, calcium channel blockers probably increased major cardiovascular events (RR 1.05, 95% CI 1.00-1.09, P=0.03) and increased congestive heart failure (RR 1.37, 1.25-1.51), both moderate-certainty, and the reviewers conclude there is moderate-certainty evidence that diuretics reduce major cardiovascular events and congestive heart failure more than calcium channel blockers. They also caution that many of the differences found are not robust and further trials might change the conclusions. This is a different author team from the first-line diuretic review, so it is an independent check on direction, but both reviews select randomised first-line head-to-head trials of multi-year duration, so they read one body of trials from opposite sides rather than replicating each other - and their estimates agree: the 2023 diuretic review's RR 0.93 for total cardiovascular events on thiazides against calcium channel blockers (PMID 37439548) inverts to 1/0.93 = 1.075, against this review's 1.05 for major cardiovascular events on calcium channel blockers against diuretics, two related but not identical composites.

  5. Major outcomes in high-risk hypertensive patients randomized to angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or calcium channel blocker vs diuretic: The Antihypertensive and Lipid-Lowering Treatment to Prevent Heart Attack Trial (ALLHAT).
    Randomised trialALLHAT Officers and Coordinators for the ALLHAT Collaborative Research Group, JAMA 2002 · PMID 12479763 · mixed

    33,357 participants aged 55 or older with hypertension and at least one other coronary risk factor, from 623 North American centres, randomly assigned to chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/d (n=15,255), amlodipine 2.5-10 mg/d (n=9,048) or lisinopril 10-40 mg/d (n=9,054), mean follow-up 4.9 years. The 33,357 is the three-arm total; the chlorthalidone-versus-amlodipine contrast is 24,303 people and the chlorthalidone-versus-lisinopril contrast 24,309. The primary outcome of combined fatal coronary heart disease or non-fatal myocardial infarction did NOT differ: versus chlorthalidone (six-year rate 11.5%), RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.90-1.07) for amlodipine (11.3%) and 0.99 (0.91-1.08) for lisinopril (11.4%). All-cause mortality also did not differ. For amlodipine versus chlorthalidone, secondary outcomes were SIMILAR EXCEPT for a higher six-year rate of heart failure with amlodipine (10.2% versus 7.7%; RR 1.38, 1.25-1.52). For lisinopril versus chlorthalidone, lisinopril had higher six-year rates of combined cardiovascular disease (33.3% versus 30.9%; RR 1.10, 1.05-1.16), stroke (6.3% versus 5.6%; RR 1.15, 1.02-1.30) and heart failure (8.7% versus 7.7%; RR 1.19, 1.07-1.31). The authors conclude thiazide-type diuretics are superior in preventing one or more major forms of cardiovascular disease, are less expensive, and should be preferred for first-step therapy. Because the 2023 Cochrane head-to-head review rests on only three trials and 30,900 participants for its ACE-inhibitor comparison and six trials and 35,217 for its calcium-channel-blocker comparison, while ALLHAT's own two-arm contrasts number 24,309 and 24,303, that review and this trial are not independent evidence. JAMA published two corrections to this article (2003;289:178 and 2004;291:2196); neither has its own PubMed record, and the figures above are as published in the original report.

  6. Prevention of stroke by antihypertensive drug treatment in older persons with isolated systolic hypertension. Final results of the Systolic Hypertension in the Elderly Program (SHEP). SHEP Cooperative Research Group.
    Randomised trialSHEP Cooperative Research Group, JAMA 1991 · PMID 2046107 · positive

    4,736 people aged 60 or older with systolic pressure 160-219 mm Hg and diastolic below 90 mm Hg (average 170/77 mm Hg, mean age 72, 57% women, 14% black) were randomised to chlorthalidone 12.5 mg/d then 25 mg/d as step one, with atenolol 25-50 mg/d as step two, or matching placebo, and followed an average 4.5 years. Five-year total stroke incidence was 5.2 per 100 participants on treatment versus 8.2 per 100 on placebo: relative risk 0.64 (P=.0003), a 36% reduction with a five-year absolute benefit of 30 events per 1000. Secondary relative risks, which the published report gives without confidence intervals, were 0.73 for non-fatal myocardial infarction plus coronary death, 0.68 for major cardiovascular events (absolute benefit 55 per 1000 over five years) and 0.87 for death from all causes. The regimen was chlorthalidone-BASED with a beta-blocker second step, not chlorthalidone alone, and this trial is of the kind pooled in the first-line and over-60 reviews rather than independent of them.

  7. Benazepril plus amlodipine or hydrochlorothiazide for hypertension in high-risk patients.
    Randomised trialJamerson K, Weber MA, Bakris GL, et al.; ACCOMPLISH Trial Investigators, N Engl J Med 2008 · PMID 19052124 · negative

    11,506 patients with hypertension at high cardiovascular risk were randomised double-blind to benazepril plus amlodipine or benazepril plus hydrochlorothiazide. The trial was TERMINATED EARLY after a mean 36 months when the prespecified stopping boundary was exceeded, which tends to overstate effect size. Achieved pressures were close (131.6/73.3 versus 132.5/74.4 mm Hg). The primary composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke, hospitalisation for angina, resuscitation after sudden cardiac arrest and coronary revascularisation occurred in 552 patients (9.6%) on benazepril-amlodipine versus 679 (11.8%) on benazepril-hydrochlorothiazide: absolute risk reduction 2.2%, relative risk reduction 19.6%, hazard ratio 0.80 (95% CI 0.72-0.90; P<0.001). The secondary composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction and non-fatal stroke gave a hazard ratio of 0.79 (0.67-0.92; P=0.002). This is the clearest randomised result against a thiazide in this entry's evidence base, and its scope is narrow: both arms contained an ACE inhibitor, so it compares combinations rather than first-line classes, and it therefore falls outside a head-to-head review requiring a first-line diuretic arm against another first-line class. The abstract reports no hydrochlorothiazide dose.

  8. Pharmacotherapy for mild hypertension.
    Meta-analysisWang D, Wright JM, Adams SP, Cundiff DK, Gueyffier F, Grenet G, Ambasta A, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2026 · PMID 41925078 · null

    Individual patient data from five randomised trials (9,124 participants; 4,593 on antihypertensives, 4,531 on placebo or no treatment) in adults with UNTREATED MILD hypertension - systolic 140-159 or diastolic 90-99 mm Hg, or both - and no pre-existing cardiovascular disease. There may be little to no reduction in all-cause mortality (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.64-1.14; 5 trials, 9,124), total cardiovascular events (RR 0.93, 0.69-1.24; 4 trials, 7,292) or coronary heart disease (RR 1.12, 0.80-1.57; 3 trials, 7,080), all low-certainty. There may be a decreased risk of stroke (RR 0.41, 0.20-0.84; 4 trials, 7,292; low-certainty) and an increase in withdrawals due to adverse effects (RR 4.80, 4.14-5.57; 1 trial, 17,354 participants, aggregate trial-level data; low-certainty). The abstract does not name the drug classes used, so this is CLASS-COLLECTIVE evidence rather than a thiazide result; it is the current version of the review, which supersedes the 2012 one. It marks the scope limit of the placebo evidence for this class, which is for moderate to severe hypertension: in this milder population nothing has been shown to be prevented except possibly stroke.

  9. Chlorthalidone vs. Hydrochlorothiazide for Hypertension-Cardiovascular Events.
    Randomised trialIshani A, Cushman WC, Leatherman SM, et al.; Diuretic Comparison Project Writing Group, N Engl J Med 2022 · PMID 36516076 · null

    A pragmatic randomised trial in 13,523 Department of Veterans Affairs patients aged 65 or older (mean age 72, mean baseline systolic 139 mm Hg) who had been receiving hydrochlorothiazide 25 or 50 mg/day - 94.5% on 25 mg - assigned either to CONTINUE hydrochlorothiazide or to switch to chlorthalidone 12.5 or 25 mg/day, so roughly half the cohort did not switch. At a median 2.4 years the primary composite of non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, heart-failure hospitalisation, urgent coronary revascularisation for unstable angina and non-cancer-related death occurred in 702 patients (10.4%) on chlorthalidone versus 675 (10.0%) on hydrochlorothiazide - hazard ratio 1.04 (95% CI 0.94-1.16; P=0.45) - with NO between-group difference in any component. Hypokalaemia was more common on chlorthalidone (6.0% versus 4.4%, P<0.001). Funded by the Veterans Affairs Cooperative Studies Program. This is a within-class comparison: it tests which thiazide, not whether a thiazide.

  10. Blood pressure lowering and risk of new-onset type 2 diabetes: an individual participant data meta-analysis.
    Network meta-analysisNazarzadeh M, Bidel Z, Canoy D, et al.; Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, Lancet 2021 · PMID 34774144 · negative

    An individual participant data analysis of trials collected by the Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration: 145,939 participants from 19 trials in the one-stage pool and 22 trials in the network meta-analysis, 9,883 new diagnoses of type 2 diabetes over a median 4.5 years, with people known to have diabetes at baseline excluded. Versus placebo, thiazide diuretics INCREASED new-onset type 2 diabetes (RR 1.20, 95% CI 1.07-1.35), as did beta blockers (RR 1.48, 1.27-1.72), while ACE inhibitors (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.93) and ARBs (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.92) reduced it and calcium channel blockers had no material effect (RR 1.02, 0.92-1.13). Blood-pressure lowering itself was protective: a 5 mm Hg systolic reduction cut type 2 diabetes risk by 11% (hazard ratio 0.89, 0.84-0.95), so the thiazide excess is an off-target drug effect working against the pressure effect rather than a consequence of lowering pressure. New-onset type 2 diabetes is a threshold-defined diagnosis, not a hard cardiovascular event. This analysis and the collaboration's cardiovascular analysis draw on the same collection of trials read for two different questions, so they are not two independent bodies of evidence.

  11. Use of Hydrochlorothiazide and Risk of Melanoma and Nonmelanoma Skin Cancer.
    Journal articleRouette J, Yin H, Pottegård A, Nirantharakumar K, Azoulay L, Drug Saf 2021 · PMID 33104975 · negative

    A propensity-score-matched population-based cohort in the UK Clinical Practice Research Datalink: 20,513 new users of hydrochlorothiazide matched 1:1 to new users of OTHER thiazide diuretics between January 1988 and March 2018, median follow-up 8.6 years. Hydrochlorothiazide was associated with an increased risk of cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (hazard ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.06-2.11), rising with cumulative duration (5-10 years HR 2.10, 1.20-3.67; over 10 years HR 3.70, 1.77-7.73) and cumulative dose (100,000 mg or more, HR 4.96, 2.51-9.81). There was NO increased risk of basal cell carcinoma (HR 1.01, 0.91-1.13) or melanoma (HR 0.82, 0.63-1.08), with no duration or dose-response for either. Because the comparator is other thiazide diuretics, this is a hydrochlorothiazide-specific signal rather than a thiazide-class effect. OBSERVATIONAL, not randomised, and about skin cancer rather than blood-pressure outcomes.

  12. Cumulative Incidence of Thiazide-Induced Hyponatremia : A Population-Based Cohort Study.
    Journal articleAndersson NW, Wohlfahrt J, Feenstra B, Hviid A, Melbye M, Lund M, Ann Intern Med 2024 · PMID 38109740 · negative

    A Danish register-based cohort study using target trial emulation in people aged 40 or older with no recent antihypertensive prescription and no previous hyponatraemia, 2014 to 2018. Two emulated trials compared 37,786 new users of bendroflumethiazide with 44,963 new users of a calcium channel blocker, and 11,943 new users of a hydrochlorothiazide plus renin-angiotensin-system-inhibitor combination pill with 85,784 new users of a renin-angiotensin system inhibitor alone. Two-year cumulative incidences of sodium below 130 mmol/L were 3.83% and 3.51% on the thiazide regimens; the risk differences against the non-thiazide comparators were 1.35% (95% CI 1.04-1.66) and 1.38% (1.01-1.75), and were HIGHER with older age and greater comorbidity burden. Hazard ratios were 3.56 (2.76-4.60) and 4.25 (3.23-5.59) during the first 30 days after starting treatment, falling to 1.26 (1.09-1.46) and 1.29 (1.05-1.58) after one year. The authors conclude the excess risk is more substantial, particularly in the first months, than drug labelling indicates. OBSERVATIONAL; the authors note residual confounding is likely and that filled prescriptions were assumed to equal drug use.

  13. Chlorthalidone for Hypertension in Advanced Chronic Kidney Disease.
    Randomised trialAgarwal R, Sinha AD, Cramer AE, Balmes-Fenwick M, Dickinson JH, Ouyang F, Tu W, N Engl J Med 2021 · PMID 34739197 · positive

    160 patients with stage 4 chronic kidney disease and poorly controlled hypertension confirmed by 24-hour ambulatory monitoring were randomised 1:1 to chlorthalidone, 12.5 mg/day titrated every four weeks up to a maximum 50 mg/day, or placebo, for 12 weeks; 121 (76%) had diabetes, 96 (60%) were already receiving loop diuretics, mean eGFR was 23.2 plus or minus 4.2 mL/min/1.73 m2 and patients were on a mean 3.4 antihypertensive medications. The adjusted change in 24-hour systolic pressure was -11.0 mm Hg (95% CI -13.9 to -8.1) with chlorthalidone versus -0.5 mm Hg (-3.5 to 2.5) with placebo, a between-group difference of -10.5 mm Hg (-14.6 to -6.4; P<0.001), and the urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio fell by 50 percentage points more (37 to 60). Hypokalaemia, reversible increases in serum creatinine, hyperglycaemia, dizziness and hyperuricaemia were all more frequent with chlorthalidone. SURROGATE ONLY: the outcomes are blood pressure and albuminuria over 12 weeks in 160 people, not events, so a thiazide can still lower pressure at this level of kidney function without any demonstration that it prevents events there.

  14. Comprehensive comparative effectiveness and safety of first-line antihypertensive drug classes: a systematic, multinational, large-scale analysis.
    Meta-analysisSuchard MA, Schuemie MJ, Krumholz HM, et al., Lancet 2019 · PMID 31668726 · mixed

    A new-user cohort analysis of 4.9 million patients across six administrative claims and three electronic health record databases generated 22,000 calibrated, propensity-score-adjusted hazard ratios across all first-line classes. MOST estimates revealed no effectiveness difference between classes, but thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics showed better primary effectiveness than ACE inhibitors for acute myocardial infarction (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.75-0.95), hospitalisation for heart failure (0.83, 0.74-0.95) and stroke (0.83, 0.74-0.95) while on initial treatment, with safety profiles also favouring diuretics; non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers were significantly inferior to the other four classes. Randomisation reproduces part of this and not all of it: the heart-failure and stroke advantages over an ACE inhibitor are echoed in ALLHAT (heart failure RR 1.19, 1.07-1.31 and stroke RR 1.15, 1.02-1.30 for lisinopril versus chlorthalidone) and in the Cochrane head-to-head stroke result (RR 0.89, 0.80-0.99), while the myocardial-infarction advantage is not (ALLHAT primary RR 0.99, 0.91-1.08; Cochrane coronary heart disease versus ACE inhibitors RR 1.03, 0.96-1.12). This is OBSERVATIONAL. The paper's background also states the guideline position: current guidelines recommend any primary agent among the first-line classes - thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, and dihydropyridine and non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers - in the absence of comorbid indications, and randomised trials have not further refined the choice. Funding included Janssen Research & Development and IQVIA alongside public funders in the USA, South Korea and Australia.

  15. Pharmacological blood pressure lowering for primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease across different levels of blood pressure: an individual participant-level data meta-analysis.
    Meta-analysisBlood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, Lancet 2021 · PMID 33933205 · positive

    Individual participant data from 48 randomised trials (344,716 participants; median 4.15 years' follow-up) of blood-pressure-lowering drugs versus placebo, versus other classes, or more versus less intensive regimens. A 5 mm Hg systolic reduction lowered major cardiovascular events by about 10%: hazard ratio 0.91 (95% CI 0.89-0.94) in the 186,988 people without prior cardiovascular disease (baseline 157/89 mm Hg) and 0.89 (0.86-0.92) in the 157,728 with it (baseline 146/84 mm Hg). Primary-prevention event rates fell from 31.9 to 25.9 per 1000 person-years, with no reliable heterogeneity across baseline systolic categories from under 120 to 170 mm Hg or more. This is a CLASS-COLLECTIVE result about the millimetres removed, not about thiazide diuretics: the stratified analyses test prior cardiovascular disease and baseline systolic category only, and the paper reports no comparison between drug classes, so nothing here supports a claim that the effect holds whichever class produces it. Because the pooled trials are themselves placebo-controlled and head-to-head trials of the same generation this entry cites individually, the estimate is not independent of them. The Lancet's published correction (PMID 34022983) updates only the collaboration's group list; no estimate changed.
