SAMe (S-adenosyl methionine)

Early evidence · for depression & low mood

For depression & low mood

SAMe is a compound the body makes naturally, and it has been used for mood in parts of Europe for decades. The research on it is genuinely split: pooling the older trials suggests it can help on its own, but the newer and more carefully run studies have not been able to repeat that — including one where a standard prescription antidepressant also failed to beat a dummy pill, and two published reviews in the same year reached opposite conclusions from much the same evidence. It is also one of the few things here that carries a real psychiatric risk, so it should not be started without a clinician who knows your history.

Safety

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal: in the largest modern trial, 19% reported stomach discomfort and 20% diarrhea. Insomnia, restlessness, anxiety, headache and sweating are also reported, and SAMe is usually taken in the morning to limit sleep disruption. The serious concern is mood switching: the Cochrane review recorded two cases of mania or hypomania among 441 SAMe-treated participants, and a 2018 adjunctive trial reported one case of serotonin syndrome-like symptoms when SAMe was added to an antidepressant. Dropout rates across pooled trials did not differ from placebo or from antidepressants, so overall tolerability is reasonable. SAMe is not a replacement for prescribed antidepressant treatment or psychotherapy.

Who should be careful: Bipolar disorder is the key exclusion: SAMe can precipitate mania or hypomania and should not be used in bipolar depression outside specialist psychiatric supervision. Anyone with a personal or family history of bipolar disorder, or with unexplained periods of elevated mood, reduced need for sleep, or impulsivity, should be evaluated before starting. Also speak to your neurologist first if you have Parkinson's disease treated with levodopa.

Interactions: Combining SAMe with SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics, MAO inhibitors, tramadol, triptans, dextromethorphan or St John's wort raises the risk of serotonin syndrome — a serotonin syndrome-like case was reported in at least one trial participant — so do not combine without clinician oversight. SAMe may interact with levodopa. Enteric-coated products are typically used because plain SAMe is poorly absorbed, and product quality varies widely between brands.

What the evidence says

The strongest single positive signal comes from a 73-participant 6-week double-blind trial in serotonin reuptake inhibitor non-responders, where adjunctive SAMe at 800 mg twice daily gave HDRS response of 36.1% versus 17.6% on placebo and remission of 25.8% versus 11.7%, with numbers needed to treat of roughly 6 and 7; that result has not replicated. A 2024 meta-analysis of 23 trials (N=2,183) found SAMe monotherapy superior to placebo at moderate certainty (SMD -0.58, 95% CI -0.93 to -0.23, I-squared 68%) but no significant difference when added to antidepressants (SMD -0.22, 95% CI -0.63 to 0.19) and none against antidepressants alone (SMD 0.06, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.18), while a second 2024 meta-analysis of 14 trials (1,522 subjects, doses 200-3,200 mg/day) found no significant difference from placebo in any comparison. The 2016 Cochrane review reached no firm conclusion: its placebo-controlled monotherapy estimate rested on 2 studies and 142 participants (SMD -0.54, 95% CI -1.54 to 0.46, P = 0.29, very low quality), SAMe was similar to imipramine (SMD -0.04, 95% CI -0.34 to 0.27, 619 participants) and to escitalopram (MD 0.12, 95% CI -2.75 to 2.99), and the only positive comparison was as an SSRI add-on (MD -3.90, 95% CI -6.93 to -0.87) from that same single 73-participant study. The modern trials are consistently null: a 189-participant 12-week three-arm trial found SAMe 1,600-3,200 mg/day, escitalopram 10-20 mg/day and placebo all improved HDRS-17 by about 5-6 points with no significant between-group difference (response 36% / 34% / 30%; remission 28% / 28% / 17%); a 107-participant 8-week adjunctive RCT found no difference at 800 mg/day (p = 0.51; response 54.3% vs 50.0%; remission 43.5% vs 38.3%) with no moderation by COMT or other genotypes; and a 49-participant monotherapy pilot found a 3.76-point MADRS advantage that was not statistically significant (p = 0.13) against a 53% placebo response rate.

Doses studied: 800-1,600 mg/day orally as adjunct to a serotonin reuptake inhibitor (Papakostas 2010, positive); 800 mg/day adjunctive (Sarris 2018, null) and 800 mg/day as monotherapy (Sarris 2019, null); 1,600-3,200 mg/day as monotherapy (Mischoulon 2014, null). Across the 14-trial 2024 meta-analysis, daily doses ranged from 200 to 3,200 mg and study duration from 2 to 12 weeks. Trials typically ran 6-12 weeks.

Why this grade: The evidence is genuinely contradictory and the best-controlled modern trials are null. Two 2024 meta-analyses of largely overlapping literature reach opposite conclusions: one (23 trials, N=2,183) found SAMe monotherapy superior to placebo at moderate certainty (SMD -0.58, 95% CI -0.93 to -0.23, I-squared 68%) but no benefit as an adjunct (SMD -0.22, 95% CI -0.63 to 0.19); the other (14 trials, 1,522 subjects) found no significant difference from placebo as monotherapy, as an adjunct, or against imipramine or escitalopram. Cochrane's placebo-controlled monotherapy comparison rested on only 2 studies and 142 participants (SMD -0.54, 95% CI -1.54 to 0.46) at very low quality, and its single positive add-on result comes from one 73-participant study. The three modern adequately conducted trials retrieved here are all null: n=189 (a failed trial in which the active comparator escitalopram also did not beat placebo), n=107 adjunctive, and n=49 monotherapy. Torn between moderate and early, graded to early: the positive pooled signal rests on older, smaller, lower-quality trials and has not been reproduced by the modern rigorous ones. Not graded not_supported, because a moderate-certainty positive meta-analysis exists and Cochrane concluded further investigation is warranted rather than that SAMe is ineffective.

Sources (7)
  1. Efficacy and acceptability of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAMe) for depressed patients: A systematic review and meta-analysis.
    Limveeraprajak N, Nakhawatchana S, Visukamol A, et al., 2024 (Prog Neuropsychopharmacol Biol Psychiatry) · PMID 38423354 · positive

    Across 23 trials (N = 2,183), SAMe monotherapy was significantly superior to placebo (SMD -0.58, 95% CI -0.93 to -0.23, I-squared 68%); in conjunction with antidepressants SAMe did not differ from placebo plus antidepressants (SMD -0.22, 95% CI -0.63 to 0.19, I-squared 76%); and SAMe did not differ from antidepressants alone (SMD 0.06, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.18). No significant differences in dropout rates were observed in any comparison. The authors rated the monotherapy finding moderate-certainty and noted efficacy as an adjunct and against antidepressants remains unresolved.

  2. S-Adenosylmethionine (SAMe) as an adjuvant therapy for patients with depression: An updated systematic review and meta-analysis.
    Peng TR, Cheng HY, Wu TW, 2024 (Gen Hosp Psychiatry) · PMID 38199136 · null

    Fourteen trials with 1,522 subjects, daily doses of 200-3,200 mg and durations of 2-12 weeks, showed no significant difference in depression for SAMe versus placebo as monotherapy, SAMe versus imipramine or escitalopram as monotherapy, or SAMe versus placebo as adjunctive therapy. The authors caution that results should be interpreted carefully given the small number of studies and wide dose range, and recommend patients discuss options with their doctor before taking SAMe.

  3. S-adenosyl methionine (SAMe) for depression in adults.
    Galizia I, Oldani L, Macritchie K, et al., 2016 (Cochrane Database Syst Rev) · PMID 27727432 · mixed

    The review covered 8 trials and 934 adults across all comparisons. There was no strong evidence of benefit for SAMe over placebo as monotherapy (SMD -0.54, 95% CI -1.54 to 0.46, P = 0.29) — an estimate drawn from just 2 studies and 142 participants at very low quality. SAMe was similar to imipramine (SMD -0.04, 95% CI -0.34 to 0.27, 619 participants) and to escitalopram (MD 0.12, 95% CI -2.75 to 2.99); only add-on to SSRIs favoured SAMe (MD -3.90, 95% CI -6.93 to -0.87), from a single 73-participant study at low quality. Two reports of mania or hypomania occurred among 441 SAMe participants.

  4. S-adenosyl methionine (SAMe) augmentation of serotonin reuptake inhibitors for antidepressant nonresponders with major depressive disorder: a double-blind, randomized clinical trial.
    Papakostas GI, Mischoulon D, Shyu I, Alpert JE, Fava M, 2010 (Am J Psychiatry) · PMID 20595412 · positive

    In 73 serotonin reuptake inhibitor non-responders with MDD, 6 weeks of adjunctive SAMe (target 800 mg twice daily) produced HDRS response in 36.1% versus 17.6% on placebo and remission in 25.8% versus 11.7%, with numbers needed to treat of approximately 6 for response and 7 for remission; discontinuation rates did not differ significantly between groups. The authors described the results as preliminary and warranting replication.

  5. A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAMe) versus escitalopram in major depressive disorder.
    Mischoulon D, Price LH, Carpenter LL, et al., 2014 (J Clin Psychiatry) · PMID 24500245 · null

    189 outpatients with DSM-IV MDD were randomized for 12 weeks to SAMe 1,600-3,200 mg/day, escitalopram 10-20 mg/day, or placebo; all three arms improved by about 5-6 HDRS-17 points with no significant between-group differences (response 36% / 34% / 30%; remission 28% / 28% / 17%). The results failed to support an advantage over placebo for either SAMe or the standard antidepressant. Stomach discomfort (19%) and diarrhea (20%) were the most common SAMe side effects.

  6. Adjunctive S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe) in treating non-remittent major depressive disorder: An 8-week double-blind, randomized, controlled trial.
    Sarris J, Byrne GJ, Bousman C, et al., 2018 (Eur Neuropsychopharmacol) · PMID 30115553 · null

    In 107 treatment non-remittent MDD outpatients, 8 weeks of SAMe 800 mg/day added to antidepressants produced no significant between-group difference in MADRS versus placebo (p = 0.51); response was 54.3% versus 50.0% and remission 43.5% versus 38.3%, with no effect on any secondary outcome and no moderation by COMT or other genotypes. One participant developed serotonin syndrome-like symptoms. The authors concluded 800 mg/day SAMe is not an effective adjunctive treatment in MDD.

  7. S-Adenosylmethionine (SAMe) monotherapy for depression: an 8-week double-blind, randomised, controlled trial.
    Sarris J, Murphy J, Stough C, et al., 2019 (Psychopharmacology) · PMID 31712971 · null

    In 49 unmedicated adults with MDD and MADRS scores of 14-25, SAMe 800 mg/day monotherapy produced a 3.76-point greater MADRS reduction than placebo over 8 weeks, but this was not statistically significant (p = 0.13) despite a medium-to-large effect size of 0.72, against a 53% placebo response rate; only an exploratory subgroup with milder symptoms (MADRS 22 or below) reached significance (p = 0.045). The treatment was safe and well tolerated.
