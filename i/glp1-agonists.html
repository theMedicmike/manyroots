Conventional medicine

GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide) and the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist tirzepatide

Strong evidence · for blood sugar & type 2 diabetes

For blood sugar & type 2 diabetes

These weekly or daily injections copy a gut hormone that prompts insulin release when you eat and makes you feel full sooner, so blood sugar comes down substantially and most people lose a lot of weight. What sets the best-studied ones apart is that they have been shown to reduce actual events, not just numbers: fewer heart attacks, strokes, heart-failure admissions and kidney failures, and fewer deaths, in very large trials of people with type 2 diabetes. The catches are real and worth knowing: nausea and gut side effects are the rule rather than the exception, gallbladder problems go up, about twice as many people stop because of side effects, most of the weight returns within a year of stopping, there is an eye-nerve signal still being sorted out, and not every drug in this family has shown the heart benefits. This is prescription treatment; nothing here is a reason to change or stop what you are already taking.

Safety

Gastrointestinal effects are the rule, not the exception: nausea 20-24% versus 8% on placebo in SUSTAIN 1 (PMID 28110911); across 21 trials and 99,599 patients, gastrointestinal disorders rose 63% and gallbladder disorders 26%, with no difference in stroke, pancreatitis or neoplasms (PMID 40892610); severe gastrointestinal events were most raised with tirzepatide (OR 4.21, 1.87-9.49; moderate certainty) in the 2025 BMJ living network meta-analysis of 869 trials (PMID 40813122). That normalisation is itself a hazard, because it is how the pain that is not ordinary gets explained away. Severe, constant upper abdominal pain that bores through to your back, does not come in waves, is worse lying flat and better leaning forward, and does not settle between doses is not dose-escalation nausea. Stop the injection, do not take the next weekly dose, and get urgent assessment for pancreatitis - say 'I am on a GLP-1 medication, please check a lipase.' Product labelling instructs prompt discontinuation while pancreatitis is being ruled out, and no restart if it is confirmed. Know the difference between ordinary nausea and a surgical emergency: do not ride it out, get medical care the same day, and say that you take a GLP-1 receptor agonist. The randomised evidence on pancreatitis is not one-way reassurance - pooled cardiovascular and kidney outcome trials found no difference (PMID 40892610, 40156846), but a 2025 meta-analysis of 62 randomised trials and 66,232 patients found pancreatitis 44% more common on GLP-1 receptor agonists (RR 1.44, 1.09-1.89), an excess that was no longer significant once trials were stratified by background medication (PMID 40988099). The gallbladder finding is more consistent: gallbladder disorders 26% more common across 99,599 trial patients (PMID 40892610), and across 76 randomised trials and 103,371 patients gallbladder or biliary disease rose 37% (RR 1.37, 1.23-1.52), cholelithiasis 27%, cholecystitis 36%, and 2.29-fold in weight-loss trials specifically, with higher risk at higher doses and longer duration (PMID 35344001). Gallstone disease and alcohol are the leading causes of acute pancreatitis (PMID 41010887), so the route from a gallstone to a surgical emergency is real. Yellowing of the eyes or skin, pale stools, dark urine, or fever with right-sided pain under the ribs also need same-day assessment. A normal ketone or glucose reading does not rule any of this out - the pain is the signal, not the number. About twice as many people stop for adverse effects (16.6% versus 8.2% in SELECT; PMID 37952131). An optic-nerve signal is unresolved: one matched cohort found substantially higher NAION incidence on semaglutide (PMID 38958939), while a 2025 systematic review of nine studies found the literature inconsistent, absolute risk low, and caution warranted mainly in people with crowded optic nerve heads or optic disc drusen (PMID 40926946); report sudden painless vision loss urgently. On the widely discussed suicidality question, a systematic review and meta-analysis found no significant association with suicidal ideation or behaviour (RR 0.568, 95% CI 0.077-4.205), though heterogeneity was very high (I-squared 98%) and the authors still advise monitoring people with psychiatric conditions (PMID 39945396). Benefits are treatment-dependent: two-thirds of weight loss returned within a year of stopping (PMID 35441470) and diabetes diagnoses rose after 17 weeks off tirzepatide (PMID 39536238). These trials tested regulated pharmaceutical products; compounded or unregulated versions were not studied and their contents are not what was trialled. Nothing here is a reason to stop, reduce or substitute anything for a prescribed glucose-lowering medicine.

Who should be careful: Product labelling contraindicates use in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2; a French nested case-control study in the national health insurance database (2,562 thyroid cancer cases, 45,184 matched controls) found 1-3 years of use associated with all thyroid cancer (adjusted HR 1.58, 1.27-1.95) and medullary thyroid cancer (1.78, 1.04-3.05) (PMID 36356111), although randomised trials have not shown an excess (PMID 28910237, 40892610). Not a treatment for type 1 diabetes, and not for use in pregnancy per labelling. People with existing diabetic retinopathy need eye monitoring: semaglutide raised retinopathy complications 76% in SUSTAIN-6, a signal generally attributed to rapid glucose lowering (PMID 27633186). Caution with a history of pancreatitis or gallstones, and with severe gastrointestinal disease including gastroparesis, since these drugs slow stomach emptying further.

Interactions: Adding a GLP-1 receptor agonist to insulin or a sulphonylurea increases hypoglycaemia risk and the other drug's dose usually needs lowering by the prescriber; on its own, hypoglycaemia below 54 mg/dL occurred in 0.2-1.7% in SURPASS-2 (PMID 34170647). Because these drugs slow stomach emptying they can change how quickly other oral medicines are absorbed. Tell any surgical or anaesthesia team that you take one, because delayed gastric emptying affects fasting and airway safety planning.

What the evidence says

Separating surrogates from outcomes matters here. On SURROGATES the effect is large: semaglutide 0.5 mg and 1.0 mg weekly lowered HbA1c by 1.43 and 1.53 percentage points more than placebo over 30 weeks from a baseline of 8.05% (PMID 28110911); in the head-to-head SURPASS-2 trial (1,879 patients on metformin, baseline 8.28%) tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg lowered HbA1c by 2.01/2.24/2.30 points versus 1.86 with semaglutide 1 mg, with 1.9-5.5 kg more weight loss (PMID 34170647); across 38 placebo-controlled trials the class range ran from -0.63 percentage points (lixisenatide) to -1.79 (tirzepatide 15 mg) (PMID 40212008). On progression to a DIAGNOSIS, among SURMOUNT-1 participants with obesity and prediabetes, 176 weeks of tirzepatide reduced new type 2 diabetes to 1.3% versus 13.3% on placebo (HR 0.07) with 12.3-19.7% weight loss, though after 17 weeks off drug the rates were 2.4% vs 13.7% (PMID 39536238). On HARD OUTCOMES - death, heart attack, stroke, kidney failure - the evidence is established for some agents and not others: semaglutide reduced the cardiovascular death/MI/stroke composite in SUSTAIN-6 (HR 0.74, 0.58-0.95) but significantly increased retinopathy complications (HR 1.76, 1.11-2.78) (PMID 27633186); liraglutide reduced MACE (HR 0.87), cardiovascular death (0.78) and all-cause death (0.85) in LEADER (PMID 27295427); semaglutide 1 mg cut major kidney disease events 24% (HR 0.76) and all-cause death 20% in FLOW (PMID 38785209); whereas once-weekly exenatide (HR 0.91, 0.83-1.00, P=0.06 for superiority) (PMID 28910237) and lixisenatide (HR 1.02, 0.89-1.17) (PMID 26630143) did not beat placebo, and tirzepatide was noninferior but not superior to dulaglutide in its only outcome trial (HR 0.92, P=0.09 for superiority) (PMID 41406444). Pooled across 10 outcome trials (71,351 people with T2D), long-acting agents reduced MACE 14%, heart-failure hospitalisation 14%, the composite kidney outcome 17% and all-cause death 12%, with no excess severe hypoglycaemia, retinopathy or pancreatic events at class level (PMID 40156846); semaglutide 2.4 mg also reduced MACE 20% (HR 0.80) in 17,604 people with obesity and cardiovascular disease but no diabetes (PMID 37952131). The trade-offs: gastrointestinal disorders +63% and gallbladder disorders +26% across 99,599 trial patients (PMID 40892610), permanent discontinuation 16.6% vs 8.2% in SELECT (PMID 37952131), a French nested case-control study linking 1-3 years of use to all thyroid cancer (HR 1.58) and medullary thyroid cancer (HR 1.78) (PMID 36356111), an unresolved optic-nerve signal (PMID 38958939, 40926946), and two-thirds of lost weight regained within a year of stopping semaglutide 2.4 mg, with cardiometabolic markers reverting toward baseline (PMID 35441470).

Doses studied: Semaglutide subcutaneous 0.5 or 1.0 mg once weekly (SUSTAIN 1, SUSTAIN-6; PMID 28110911, 27633186), 1.0 mg weekly (FLOW; PMID 38785209), 2.4 mg weekly for obesity and cardiovascular prevention (STEP 1 extension, SELECT; PMID 35441470, 37952131). Tirzepatide 5, 10 or 15 mg once weekly (SURPASS-2, SURMOUNT-1, SURPASS-CVOT; PMID 34170647, 39536238, 41406444). Dulaglutide 1.5 mg weekly as the active comparator in SURPASS-CVOT (PMID 41406444). Exenatide extended-release 2 mg once weekly (EXSCEL; PMID 28910237). Doses are escalated over weeks by a prescriber to limit nausea.

Why this grade: Graded strong on HARD OUTCOMES, not on glucose numbers: across ten placebo-controlled cardiovascular and kidney outcome trials in 71,351 people with type 2 diabetes, long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonists reduced major adverse cardiovascular events 14%, heart-failure hospitalisation 14%, a composite kidney outcome 17% and all-cause mortality 12% (PMID 40156846), with the same direction confirmed across 21 trials and 99,599 patients (PMID 40892610). The grade applies to the long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonists with placebo-controlled outcome trials - semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide - and not uniformly to every agent: once-weekly exenatide (PMID 28910237) and lixisenatide (PMID 26630143) did not beat placebo on cardiovascular outcomes. Tirzepatide is included in the entry because its surrogate and diabetes-prevention data are the largest in the class, but its hard-outcome evidence is weaker than its reputation: it has no placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcome trial, and in SURPASS-CVOT it was noninferior but not superior to dulaglutide (HR 0.92, 95.3% CI 0.83-1.01; P=0.09 for superiority) (PMID 41406444). Benefits are treatment-dependent and reverse after stopping, gastrointestinal and gallbladder harms are real, and semaglutide carries a retinopathy signal.

  1. Cardiovascular and Kidney Outcomes and Mortality With Long-Acting Injectable and Oral Glucagon-Like Peptide 1 Receptor Agonists in Individuals With Type 2 Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomized Trials.
    Meta-analysisLee MMY, Sattar N et al., 2025 · PMID 40156846 · positive

    HARD OUTCOMES. 10 randomised placebo-controlled cardiovascular and kidney outcome trials, 71,351 people with T2D: MACE reduced 14% (HR 0.86, 0.81-0.90), heart-failure hospitalisation 14% (0.86, 0.79-0.93), composite kidney outcome 17% (0.83, 0.75-0.92) and all-cause mortality 12% (0.88, 0.82-0.93), with a consistent 14% reduction across all MACE components. No significant heterogeneity by subcutaneous versus oral route. No increased risk of severe hypoglycaemia, retinopathy or pancreatic events at class level. Authors note trial-level meta-analysis precludes subgroup detail and may introduce ecological bias.

  2. Tirzepatide versus Semaglutide Once Weekly in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes.
    Randomised trialFrias JP et al. (SURPASS-2), 2021 · PMID 34170647 · positive

    SURROGATE endpoints. Established T2D on metformin, n=1,879, baseline HbA1c 8.28%, 40 weeks, open-label: HbA1c fell 2.01, 2.24 and 2.30 percentage points with tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg versus 1.86 with semaglutide 1 mg (differences -0.15, -0.39 and -0.45 points; all significant). Weight loss was 1.9-5.5 kg greater with tirzepatide. Nausea 17-22% vs 18%, vomiting 6-10% vs 8%, diarrhoea 13-16% vs 12%; hypoglycaemia below 54 mg/dL 0.2-1.7% vs 0.4%; serious adverse events 5-7% vs 3%. No cardiovascular outcomes measured.

  3. Tirzepatide for Obesity Treatment and Diabetes Prevention.
    Randomised trialJastreboff AM et al. (SURMOUNT-1), 2024 · PMID 39536238 · positive

    1,032 participants with obesity AND prediabetes within a 2,539-patient trial, 176 weeks: weight change -12.3%, -18.7% and -19.7% with tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg versus -1.3% with placebo. Type 2 diabetes diagnosed in 1.3% versus 13.3% (HR 0.07, 95% CI 0.0-0.1). After 17 weeks off treatment, 2.4% versus 13.7% had diabetes (HR 0.12, 0.1-0.2), showing the effect is treatment-dependent. Adverse events mostly gastrointestinal, mild to moderate, concentrated in the first 20 weeks of dose escalation. No cardiovascular outcomes measured.

  4. Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes.
    Randomised trialMarso SP et al. (SUSTAIN-6), 2016 · PMID 27633186 · mixed

    3,297 people with T2D (83.0% with established cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease or both), 104 weeks, semaglutide 0.5 or 1.0 mg weekly. Designed as a noninferiority safety trial: primary composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke occurred in 6.6% versus 8.9% (HR 0.74, 0.58-0.95; P<0.001 for noninferiority). Nonfatal stroke HR 0.61 (0.38-0.99); nonfatal MI 0.74 (0.51-1.08, P=0.12); cardiovascular death rates similar between groups. Retinopathy complications (vitreous haemorrhage, blindness, need for intravitreal treatment or photocoagulation) were significantly HIGHER on semaglutide (HR 1.76, 1.11-2.78; P=0.02). More participants discontinued for gastrointestinal events.

  5. Cardiovascular Outcomes with Tirzepatide versus Dulaglutide in Type 2 Diabetes.
    Phase 3 trialNicholls SJ et al. (SURPASS-CVOT), 2025 · PMID 41406444 · mixed

    HARD OUTCOME, NO SUPERIORITY. 13,299 randomised (13,165 analysed) patients with T2D and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, mean HbA1c 8.4%, tirzepatide up to 15 mg weekly versus dulaglutide 1.5 mg - an active comparator, not placebo. Primary composite of cardiovascular death, MI or stroke occurred in 12.2% versus 13.1% (HR 0.92, 95.3% CI 0.83-1.01; P=0.003 for noninferiority; P=0.09 for SUPERIORITY). More gastrointestinal adverse events with tirzepatide. Tirzepatide has no placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcome trial.

  6. Effects of Once-Weekly Exenatide on Cardiovascular Outcomes in Type 2 Diabetes.
    Randomised trialHolman RR et al. (EXSCEL), 2017 · PMID 28910237 · null

    HARD OUTCOME, NULL. 14,752 people with T2D (73.1% with previous cardiovascular disease), median 3.2 years, exenatide extended-release 2 mg weekly: primary composite 11.4% versus 12.2% (HR 0.91, 0.83-1.00) - noninferior for safety but NOT superior to placebo (P=0.06 for superiority). No significant difference in cardiovascular death, MI, stroke, heart-failure hospitalisation, acute pancreatitis, pancreatic cancer, medullary thyroid carcinoma or serious adverse events.

  7. Evaluating the Rates of Pancreatitis and Pancreatic Cancer Among GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials.
    Meta-analysisWen J et al., 2025 · PMID 40988099 · negative

    HARM SIGNAL, randomised. 62 RCTs of dulaglutide, exenatide, liraglutide, semaglutide, beinaglutide, retatrutide or tirzepatide, 66,232 patients, mean age 58.3 years, mean follow-up 43.5 weeks. Pancreatitis was significantly MORE common on GLP-1 receptor agonists (RR 1.44, 95% CI 1.09-1.89; P=0.009); the excess was no longer significant when trials were stratified by background medication (with: RR 1.28, 0.87-1.87; without: RR 1.37, 0.91-2.05). Pancreatic cancer overall was not increased (RR 1.30, 0.86-1.97), though it was in the with-background-medication stratum (RR 1.85, 1.05-3.26). Authors note many trials with zero events in both arms were excluded. This is the reason the entry does not tell readers that randomised trials rule out a pancreatitis excess, and why it gives an explicit red-flag rule instead.

  8. Association of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonist Use With Risk of Gallbladder and Biliary Diseases: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomized Clinical Trials.
    Meta-analysisHe L et al., 2022 · PMID 35344001 · negative

    HARM, randomised. 76 RCTs, 103,371 patients (mean age 57.8 years, 40.5% women): gallbladder or biliary disease RR 1.37 (95% CI 1.23-1.52); cholelithiasis 1.27 (1.10-1.47), cholecystitis 1.36 (1.14-1.62), biliary disease 1.55 (1.08-2.22). Risk was substantially higher in weight-loss trials (RR 2.29, 1.64-3.18) than in type 2 diabetes or other trials (1.27, 1.14-1.43; P<.001 for interaction), higher at higher doses (1.56 vs 0.99) and with longer duration of use (1.40 vs 0.79).

  9. Weight regain and cardiometabolic effects after withdrawal of semaglutide: The STEP 1 trial extension.
    Randomised trialWilding JPH et al., 2022 · PMID 35441470 · negative

    NEGATIVE / benefits not durable. 327 adults with obesity and no diabetes followed one year after stopping semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly plus lifestyle intervention: on-treatment weight loss was 17.3% versus 2.0% on placebo; after withdrawal participants regained 11.6 percentage points, leaving a net 5.6% loss at week 120 (roughly two-thirds of the loss regained). Cardiometabolic improvements reverted toward baseline for most variables. Authors conclude ongoing treatment is required to maintain the improvements.

  10. Risk of Nonarteritic Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy in Patients Prescribed Semaglutide.
    Journal articleHathaway JT et al., 2024 · PMID 38958939 · negative

    HARM SIGNAL, observational. Retrospective matched cohort from one academic neuro-ophthalmology registry, 16,827 patients with no history of NAION. Among 710 patients with T2D, 36-month cumulative NAION incidence was 8.9% on semaglutide versus 1.8% on non-GLP-1 RA antidiabetes drugs (HR 4.28, 95% CI 1.62-11.29). Among 979 overweight or obese patients, 6.7% versus 0.8% (HR 7.64, 2.21-26.36). Authors state that as an observational study it cannot establish causality; a 2025 systematic review of nine studies found results inconsistent and absolute risk low (PMID 40926946).
