ACE inhibitors and ARBs

Strong evidence · for high blood pressure

For high blood pressure

ImportantThese must not be taken in pregnancy or when trying to conceive - they can fatally injure a developing baby's kidneys - and if a pregnancy is discovered, contact the prescriber urgently rather than simply stopping.

These two related drug families are a standard first choice for high blood pressure, and their trials counted strokes, heart attacks and deaths, not a cuff reading. ACE inhibitors have beaten placebo on four hard outcomes, including death and stroke; ARBs have no clean placebo-controlled trial as a first blood-pressure drug, so they stand on guideline first-line status and head-to-head trials matching ACE inhibitors on death, with far less cough. Neither beat a cheap water pill: in 24,309 people on chlorthalidone or lisinopril, heart attack and death were level and the ACE inhibitor did a bit worse on stroke and heart failure. Starting, switching or stopping one belongs with a prescriber.

Safety

These are prescription drugs; class choice, dose and monitoring belong with a prescriber, and no one should start, change or stop them from a website. Pregnancy is the hard line: these drugs can injure and kill a developing baby's kidneys, so they are not used by anyone pregnant or trying to conceive, and the switch is arranged before conception, not after a positive test; if a pregnancy is discovered, contact the prescriber urgently rather than simply stopping, so a replacement is arranged. Dry cough is the signature ACE-inhibitor effect - 4.2% on ramipril versus 1.1% on telmisartan, angioedema 0.3% versus 0.1% (PMID 18378520) - and is why ARBs caused fewer withdrawals for adverse effects across eight head-to-head trials (RR 0.83, absolute risk reduction 1.8%) (PMID 25148386). Angioedema - swelling of the tongue, lips or throat - is an emergency and can first appear months or years in, not just at the start. Kidney function and potassium are rechecked one to two weeks after a start or an increase: both drugs raise potassium and can reduce kidney function, especially with a diuretic or an NSAID. Dizziness on standing is common early. An ACE inhibitor plus an ARB is not a stronger version of either: in ONTARGET it added hypotensive symptoms (4.8% vs 1.7%), syncope and renal dysfunction (13.5% vs 10.2%) with no outcome gain (RR 0.99, 0.92-1.07) (PMID 18378520). Seek urgent care for a swollen tongue, lips or throat, fainting, confusion, severe weakness or a very low pressure.

Who should be careful: Contraindicated in pregnancy and in anyone trying to conceive, because of fetal kidney injury and death; contraindicated after any previous episode of angioedema on an ACE inhibitor or ARB, and in known bilateral renal artery stenosis. An ACE inhibitor and an ARB are not used together, and neither is combined with a direct renin inhibitor, because of hyperkalaemia and kidney injury. Used with caution and closer monitoring in advanced chronic kidney disease, in existing hyperkalaemia, in significant aortic stenosis, and in anyone volume-depleted from vomiting, diarrhoea, heat or high diuretic doses. Race modified the result in ALLHAT's prespecified subgroup analysis: lisinopril versus chlorthalidone differed by race for systolic pressure, for stroke and for combined cardiovascular disease, with stroke relative risk 1.40 (95% CI 1.17-1.68) in black participants against 1.00 (0.85-1.17) in non-black participants, and the authors concluded that thiazide-type diuretics remain the drugs of choice for initial therapy in both groups (PMID 15811979). Anyone with resistant, secondary or pulmonary hypertension, or who is pregnant, needs an individualised assessment this page does not cover.

Interactions: Potassium is the highest-consequence stacking hazard on this page: the potassium entry graded strong for high blood pressure covers potassium-enriched salt substitutes and potassium supplements, and both routes can cause dangerous hyperkalaemia with an ACE inhibitor or ARB - as can potassium-sparing diuretics (spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride) and trimethoprim. NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen blunt the blood-pressure effect and, combined with one of these drugs plus a diuretic, are a recognised route to acute kidney injury. ACE inhibitors raise lithium levels. Sacubitril-valsartan must not overlap with an ACE inhibitor because of angioedema risk, and a washout is required. Alcohol, alpha-blockers, nitrates, PDE5 inhibitors such as sildenafil and tadalafil, and any additional blood-pressure-lowering supplement add to the hypotensive effect. Licorice root, decongestants such as pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine, oral corticosteroids and stimulant medications raise blood pressure and work against treatment. Disclose every supplement to the prescriber; several interventions graded on this page are themselves mildly blood-pressure-lowering and stack with medication.

What the evidence says

The ACE half carries placebo-controlled evidence on hard events: in the 2018 Cochrane first-line review, ACE inhibitors reduced all-cause mortality (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.72-0.95), stroke (RR 0.65, 0.52-0.82), coronary heart disease (RR 0.81, 0.70-0.94) and total cardiovascular events (RR 0.76, 0.67-0.85) on low- to moderate-quality evidence mostly applicable to moderate to severe hypertension; the abstract gives no trial count or participant total for the ACE-inhibitor comparison, and the review found no first-line ARB trials to include (PMID 29667175). The ARB half rests on equivalence plus guideline first-line status (PMID 31668726). Nine head-to-head trials found no difference between the families in total mortality (RR 0.98, 0.88-1.10; 5 trials, moderate certainty), total cardiovascular events (RR 1.07, 0.96-1.19; 3 trials, low certainty) or cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.98, 0.85-1.13; 4 trials), with fewer withdrawals for adverse effects on ARBs (RR 0.83, 0.74-0.93), mainly dry cough (PMID 25148386). ONTARGET matched telmisartan 80 mg to ramipril 10 mg in 17,118 high-vascular-risk people, 16.7% versus 16.5% (RR 1.01, 0.94-1.09) (PMID 18378520); pooled against placebo, myocardial infarction was no different on ARBs (OR 0.944, 0.841-1.060), a subgroup whose size the abstract does not give (PMID 19381108). One significant divergence survives: heart-failure prevention or hospitalisation favours ACE inhibitors (RR 0.71, 0.54-0.93) (PMID 30518809); an indirect review's mortality edge for ACE inhibitors does not survive randomised head-to-head comparison (PMID 28862020). The ARB-versus-placebo nulls come from sicker populations than first-line hypertension: TRANSCEND randomised 5,926 ACE-intolerant people with vascular disease or diabetes with end-organ damage to telmisartan or placebo and missed its primary composite (HR 0.92, 0.81-1.05; p=0.216) (PMID 18757085); SCOPE randomised candesartan against placebo in 4,964 adults aged 70-89 but gave 84% of controls open-label treatment, and its primary outcome fell a non-significant 10.9% (P=0.19), with non-fatal stroke down 27.8% (P=0.04) (PMID 12714861). Against first-line thiazides the ACE inhibitor loses two outcomes in ALLHAT's 24,309-person contrast - stroke 6.3% versus 5.6% (RR 1.15, 1.02-1.30) and heart failure 8.7% versus 7.7% (RR 1.19, 1.07-1.31) - with the primary coronary outcome and death no different (PMID 12479763); the 2023 head-to-head review that trial dominates gives mortality RR 1.00 (0.95-1.07) and does not reproduce the heart-failure excess (PMID 37439548). SURROGATE ONLY: ACE inhibitors (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.93) and ARBs (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.92) reduced new-onset type 2 diabetes versus placebo where thiazides raised it, and a threshold-defined diagnosis is not a prevented cardiovascular event (PMID 34774144). Underneath sits a class-collective result: across 48 trials and 344,716 participants, each 5 mm Hg systolic reduction cut major cardiovascular events by about 10% (PMID 33933205).

Doses studied: Licensed doses titrated to a blood-pressure target rather than fixed. ALLHAT used lisinopril 10-40 mg/day (PMID 12479763); ONTARGET compared ramipril 10 mg/day with telmisartan 80 mg/day (PMID 18378520); TRANSCEND used telmisartan 80 mg/day (PMID 18757085); HOPE-3 used candesartan 16 mg/day plus hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg/day and found nothing on either co-primary outcome (PMID 27041480). Head-to-head trials titrated both arms to one target, testing class not dose. No ARB dose above comes from a placebo-controlled first-line hypertension trial: the 2018 Cochrane review found none, SCOPE gave 84% of controls open-label treatment, and TRANSCEND was not a first-line hypertension population.

Why this grade: Scoped to ACE inhibitors and ARBs as a first-choice drug for established high blood pressure; whether to treat at all, and how low to aim, are graded separately on this page. Strong rests on two halves of unequal strength, and the thinner half is load-bearing. The ACE half has placebo-controlled hard-outcome evidence: in the 2018 Cochrane first-line review, ACE inhibitors cut all-cause mortality (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.72-0.95), stroke (RR 0.65, 0.52-0.82), coronary heart disease (RR 0.81, 0.70-0.94) and total cardiovascular events (RR 0.76, 0.67-0.85) - four hard outcomes - and the same review found no first-line ARB trials at all (PMID 29667175). The ARB half therefore reaches this grade on the scale's other limb, guideline first-line status: current guidelines recommend any primary agent among thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and both calcium channel blocker types, in the absence of comorbid indications (PMID 31668726). Substituting one for the other rests on equivalence, not placebo trials - nine head-to-head trials in primary hypertension found no difference in total mortality (RR 0.98, 0.88-1.10, moderate certainty, 5 trials), total cardiovascular events (RR 1.07, 0.96-1.19, low certainty, 3 trials) or cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.98, 0.85-1.13, 4 trials), in data drawn mostly from people with existing clinical sequelae of hypertension (PMID 25148386), and ONTARGET matched telmisartan to ramipril across 17,118 people (RR 1.01, 0.94-1.09) (PMID 18378520). The asymmetry belongs in the open: thiazides carry high-quality placebo evidence on all four outcomes, ACE inhibitors low to moderate, ARBs none, so this entry sits below the thiazide entry despite sharing its badge. The debits are not only randomised: a 4.9-million-patient cohort analysis also favoured thiazides over ACE inhibitors on heart attack, heart-failure admission and stroke (PMID 31668726). ALLHAT's 24,309-person chlorthalidone-versus-lisinopril contrast gave more stroke (RR 1.15, 1.02-1.30) and more heart failure (RR 1.19, 1.07-1.31) on the ACE inhibitor, with the primary coronary outcome and death no different (PMID 12479763) - and it is also the whole thiazide comparison in the 2018 Cochrane review of this class (PMID 30480768), so those are one result, not two. The larger 2023 review does not reproduce that heart-failure excess as significant, and finds mortality identical (PMID 37439548). Between the two families, one significant hard-outcome difference favours ACE inhibitors, for heart failure (RR 0.71, 0.54-0.93) (PMID 30518809).

  1. First-line drugs for hypertension.
    Meta-analysisWright JM, Musini VM, Gill R, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2018 · PMID 29667175 · positive

    Twenty-four randomised trials with 28 active treatment arms and 58,040 patients across four drug classes (the review sought six and found no trials for ARBs or alpha-blockers), compared with placebo or no treatment; mean age 56, follow-up three to five years, mostly applicable to moderate to severe primary hypertension. The abstract reports no trial count and no participant total for any single class. First-line ACE inhibitors, on low- to moderate-quality evidence, reduced all-cause mortality from 13.6% to 11.3% (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.72-0.95), stroke from 6.0% to 3.9% (RR 0.65, 0.52-0.82), coronary heart disease from 13.5% to 11.0% (RR 0.81, 0.70-0.94) and total cardiovascular events from 20.1% to 15.3% (RR 0.76, 0.67-0.85). No randomised controlled trials of ARBs, or of alpha-blockers, as first-line therapy were found to include, and no withdrawal-for-adverse-effect data were available for ACE inhibitors. First-line low-dose thiazides reduced the same four outcomes on high-quality evidence, and the authors conclude that first-line ACE inhibitors and calcium channel blockers may be similarly effective but on lower-quality evidence. The 2017 updated search identified no new trials.

  2. Angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors versus angiotensin receptor blockers for primary hypertension.
    Meta-analysisLi EC, Heran BS, Wright JM, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014 · PMID 25148386 · null

    Nine head-to-head randomised trials of at least one year, 11,007 participants in total, comparing an ACE inhibitor directly with an ARB in primary hypertension. That total is not the denominator of each estimate: five studies reported total mortality, three reported total cardiovascular events, four reported cardiovascular mortality, eight contributed withdrawal data, and no study separately reported cardiovascular morbidity. There was no evidence of a difference in total mortality (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.88-1.10), total cardiovascular events (RR 1.07, 0.96-1.19) or cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.98, 0.85-1.13). Withdrawals due to adverse effects were slightly lower with ARBs on high-level evidence (RR 0.83, 0.74-0.93; absolute risk reduction 1.8%; number needed to treat 55 over 4.1 years), mainly from more dry cough on ACE inhibitors. GRADE was moderate for mortality and low for total cardiovascular events, limited by possible publication bias because several otherwise eligible trials had no extractable hypertension-subgroup data. The authors state that ACE inhibitors' placebo-controlled efficacy cannot be extrapolated to ARBs, which have not been studied in placebo-controlled trials for hypertension, and that their data mostly derive from participants with existing clinical sequelae of hypertension.

  3. Telmisartan, ramipril, or both in patients at high risk for vascular events.
    Randomised trialONTARGET Investigators (Yusuf S, Teo KK, Pogue J, et al.), N Engl J Med 2008 · PMID 18378520 · null

    8,576 patients were randomised to ramipril 10 mg/day, 8,542 to telmisartan 80 mg/day and 8,502 to both, in people with vascular disease or high-risk diabetes without heart failure - a high-vascular-risk population, not uncomplicated first-line hypertension. Mean blood pressure was 0.9/0.6 mm Hg lower on telmisartan than on ramipril. At a median 56 months the primary composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke or hospitalisation for heart failure occurred in 1,412 on ramipril (16.5%) and 1,423 on telmisartan (16.7%), relative risk 1.01 (95% CI 0.94-1.09). Telmisartan gave less cough (1.1% vs 4.2%, P<0.001) and less angioedema (0.1% vs 0.3%, P=0.01) but more hypotensive symptoms (2.6% vs 1.7%, P<0.001), with syncope equal at 0.2%. The combination arm gave relative risk 0.99 (0.92-1.07) with more hypotensive symptoms (4.8% vs 1.7%, P<0.001), syncope (0.3% vs 0.2%, P=0.03) and renal dysfunction (13.5% vs 10.2%, P<0.001).

  4. Effects of the angiotensin-receptor blocker telmisartan on cardiovascular events in high-risk patients intolerant to angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors: a randomised controlled trial.
    Randomised trialTRANSCEND Investigators (Yusuf S, Teo K, Anderson C, et al.), Lancet 2008 · PMID 18757085 · null

    5,926 patients intolerant to ACE inhibitors, with cardiovascular disease or diabetes with end-organ damage, randomised to telmisartan 80 mg/day (n=2,954) or placebo (n=2,972), many already receiving other proven therapies. Over a median 56 months (IQR 51-64) mean blood pressure was 4.0/2.2 mm Hg lower on telmisartan. The primary composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke or hospitalisation for heart failure occurred in 465 (15.7%) versus 504 (17.0%), hazard ratio 0.92 (95% CI 0.81-1.05, p=0.216). A secondary composite excluding heart failure occurred in 384 (13.0%) versus 440 (14.8%), 0.87 (0.76-1.00), p=0.048 unadjusted and p=0.068 after adjustment for multiplicity and overlap with the primary outcome. Cardiovascular hospitalisation fell from 980 (33.0%) to 894 (30.3%), relative risk 0.92 (0.85-0.99, p=0.025). Hypotensive symptoms were the commonest reason for permanent discontinuation (0.98% on telmisartan vs 0.54% on placebo). This is the cleanest ARB-versus-placebo trial with hard outcomes, but the population is established vascular disease or diabetes with end-organ damage, not first-line hypertension. Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim. An erratum is listed on this record (Lancet 2008;372(9647):1384); it carries no separate PubMed entry, so its contents are not retrievable there.

  5. Major outcomes in high-risk hypertensive patients randomized to angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or calcium channel blocker vs diuretic: The Antihypertensive and Lipid-Lowering Treatment to Prevent Heart Attack Trial (ALLHAT).
    Randomised trialALLHAT Collaborative Research Group, JAMA 2002 · PMID 12479763 · mixed

    33,357 participants aged 55 or older with hypertension and at least one other coronary risk factor, from 623 North American centres, randomly assigned to chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/day (n=15,255), amlodipine 2.5-10 mg/day (n=9,048) or lisinopril 10-40 mg/day (n=9,054); mean follow-up 4.9 years. The ACE-inhibitor comparison is the 24,309 people in the chlorthalidone and lisinopril arms, not the 33,357 of the whole three-arm trial. Against chlorthalidone's six-year rate of 11.5%, lisinopril's primary outcome of fatal coronary heart disease or non-fatal myocardial infarction was no different at 11.4% (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.91-1.08), and all-cause mortality did not differ. Lisinopril had higher six-year rates of combined cardiovascular disease (33.3% vs 30.9%; RR 1.10, 1.05-1.16), stroke (6.3% vs 5.6%; RR 1.15, 1.02-1.30) and heart failure (8.7% vs 7.7%; RR 1.19, 1.07-1.31). Five-year systolic pressure was 2 mm Hg higher on lisinopril than on chlorthalidone (P<.001). The authors conclude that thiazide-type diuretics are superior in preventing one or more major forms of cardiovascular disease, are less expensive, and should be preferred for first-step therapy. JAMA published two corrections to this article (2003;289:178 and 2004;291:2196); neither has its own PubMed record, and the figures above are as published in the original report.

  6. Outcomes in hypertensive black and nonblack patients treated with chlorthalidone, amlodipine, and lisinopril.
    Journal articleWright JT Jr, Dunn JK, Cutler JA, et al.; ALLHAT Collaborative Research Group, JAMA 2005 · PMID 15811979 · mixed

    The prespecified racial-subgroup analysis of ALLHAT - the same 33,357 randomised participants as the main report (PMID 12479763), split by subgroup, not a second trial, and 35% of them black. No significant difference was found between treatment groups for the primary coronary outcome in either racial subgroup. For lisinopril versus chlorthalidone, results differed by race for systolic blood pressure, which fell further on chlorthalidone in black participants, and for stroke and combined cardiovascular disease (P<.001, P=.01 and P=.04 respectively for interaction): stroke relative risk was 1.40 (95% CI 1.17-1.68) in black and 1.00 (0.85-1.17) in non-black participants, and combined cardiovascular disease 1.19 (1.09-1.30) and 1.06 (1.00-1.13). Heart-failure relative risks were 1.30 (1.10-1.54) and 1.13 (1.00-1.28), with no significant interaction by race. Adjusting for blood pressure over time did not significantly alter the lisinopril-versus-chlorthalidone differences in black participants. The authors conclude that thiazide-type diuretics remain the drugs of choice for initial therapy in both black and non-black hypertensive patients.

  7. First-line diuretics versus other classes of antihypertensive drugs for hypertension.
    Narrative reviewReinhart M, Puil L, Salzwedel DM, Wright JM, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2023 · PMID 37439548 · mixed

    Twenty active-comparator randomised trials with 26 comparator arms randomising over 90,000 participants. The authors state the findings are relevant to first-line use in older male and female hypertensive patients aged 50 to 75 with multiple co-morbidities including type 2 diabetes - an applicability statement, not the randomised age range. Compared with first-line ACE inhibitors, first-line thiazides probably produce little to no difference in total mortality (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.95-1.07; 3 trials, 30,961 participants; moderate certainty), may produce little to no difference in total cardiovascular events (RR 0.97, 0.92-1.02; 3 trials, 30,900; low certainty), and probably little to no difference in coronary heart disease (RR 1.03, 0.96-1.12; 3 trials, 30,900) or heart failure (RR 0.94, 0.84-1.04; 2 trials, 30,392); they probably reduce stroke slightly, 4.7% on ACE inhibitors versus 4.1% on thiazides (RR 0.89, 0.80-0.99; absolute risk reduction 0.6%), and withdrawals due to adverse effects, 3.9% versus 2.9% (RR 0.73, 0.64-0.84; 3 trials, 25,254). ARBs fall among the comparators for which data were insufficient, and the authors' conclusions name beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, ACE inhibitors and alpha-blockers only, so this review carries no ARB result. The reviewers state that no antihypertensive drug class demonstrated any clinically important advantages over first-line thiazides. The abstract names no included trial, but ALLHAT alone randomised 24,309 people to chlorthalidone or lisinopril (PMID 12479763), so it necessarily dominates a three-trial, 30,961-participant comparison.

  8. First-line drugs inhibiting the renin angiotensin system versus other first-line antihypertensive drug classes for hypertension.
    Meta-analysisChen YJ, Li LJ, Tang WL, et al.; Wright JM, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2018 · PMID 30480768 · mixed

    Forty-five randomised, active-controlled, double-blind trials, 66,625 participants, mean age 66, comparing first-line renin-angiotensin-system inhibitors - ACE inhibitors, ARBs and renin inhibitors - with other first-line classes. Versus first-line thiazides, renin-angiotensin-system inhibitors increased heart failure (RR 1.19, 95% CI 1.07-1.31, absolute risk increase 1.0%) and increased stroke (RR 1.14, 1.02-1.28, absolute risk increase 0.6%), with all-cause death no different (RR 1.00, 0.94-1.07); all three of those estimates come from a single 24,309-participant trial, and that is exactly the size of ALLHAT's chlorthalidone and lisinopril arms (15,255 plus 9,054) while the heart-failure figure matches ALLHAT's own RR 1.19 (1.07-1.31) digit for digit (PMID 12479763), so this review and that trial are one result and not two agreeing studies. Total cardiovascular events (RR 1.05, 1.00-1.11) and total myocardial infarction (RR 0.93, 0.86-1.01) rested on 2 trials and 24,379 participants and did not differ. Versus first-line calcium channel blockers, renin-angiotensin-system inhibitors decreased heart failure (RR 0.83, 0.77-0.90, absolute risk reduction 1.2%; 5 trials, 35,143) and increased stroke (RR 1.19, 1.08-1.32, absolute risk increase 0.7%; 4 trials, 34,673), with no difference in all-cause death, total cardiovascular events or total myocardial infarction, the authors noting that the heart-failure increase on calcium channel blockers exceeded their stroke decrease. Versus first-line beta-blockers, on low-certainty evidence, they decreased total cardiovascular events (RR 0.88, 0.80-0.98; 2 trials, 9,239) and stroke (RR 0.75, 0.63-0.88; 1 trial, 9,193). The authors conclude that first-line thiazides caused less heart failure and stroke than first-line renin-angiotensin-system inhibitors. PubMed marks this review superseded by the 2023 update (PMID 37439548), which pools two trials for the thiazide heart-failure comparison and does not reproduce the excess as significant.

  9. A systematic review and network meta-analysis of the comparative efficacy of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers in hypertension.
    Network meta-analysisDimou C, Antza C, Akrivos E, et al., Journal of Human Hypertension 2019 · PMID 30518809 · mixed

    A systematic review and network meta-analysis of placebo- and active-controlled double-blind randomised trials in essential hypertension: blood-pressure results from 30 studies with 7,370 participants, and mortality or morbidity results from 8 studies with 25,158 participants. The two classes were similar in lowering systolic (weighted mean difference 0.59, 95% CI -0.21 to 1.38) and diastolic pressure (0.62, -0.06 to 1.30), and similar for all-cause mortality (RR 0.96, 0.80-1.14), cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.87, 0.67-1.14), fatal and non-fatal myocardial infarction (RR 1.02, 0.75-1.37) and stroke (RR 1.13, 0.87-1.46). ACE inhibitors were more helpful than ARBs in the prevention and/or the hospitalisation for heart failure (RR 0.71, 0.54-0.93) - the one significant hard-outcome difference between the two families. This is a network rather than a purely head-to-head analysis, and its 8-study mortality and morbidity set overlaps the head-to-head trials cited separately here, so it is a re-analysis of largely the same data rather than independent confirmation. Two things bound how far it can be pushed: Cochrane's head-to-head pool reported no separately extractable cardiovascular morbidity (PMID 25148386), and ONTARGET's primary composite included hospitalisation for heart failure and was flat (PMID 18378520).

  10. Angiotensin II receptor blockers and myocardial infarction: an updated analysis of randomized clinical trials.
    Meta-analysisVolpe M, Tocci G, Sciarretta S, et al., Journal of Hypertension 2009 · PMID 19381108 · null

    A meta-analysis of 20 major randomised trials published by 31 August 2008, 108,909 patients, mean age 66.5, mean follow-up 3.3 years, comparing ARB-based treatment with other drugs or placebo and reporting myocardial infarction incidence. Myocardial infarction occurred in 2,374 of 53,208 in ARB-based groups and 2,354 of 53,153 in comparator arms, odds ratio 1.008 (95% CI 0.950-1.069). Against ACE inhibitors specifically the odds ratio was 1.008 (0.926-1.099) - the same point estimate on a different confidence interval - and against placebo 0.944 (0.841-1.060), against beta-blockers and diuretics 0.970 (0.804-1.170), against calcium channel blockers 1.112 (0.971-1.272), and for ARB-plus-ACE-inhibitor combination therapy 0.996 (0.896-1.107). Risks of myocardial infarction did not differ across clinical conditions including hypertension, high cardiovascular risk, stroke, coronary disease, renal disease and heart failure. Its ARB-versus-placebo estimate, a subgroup whose size the abstract does not give, is null and covers myocardial infarction only; the pooled trials overlap ONTARGET, TRANSCEND and SCOPE, so the analysis is not independent of them.

  11. Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors reduce mortality compared to angiotensin receptor blockers: Systematic review and meta-analysis.
    Meta-analysisSalvador GL, Marmentini VM, Cosmo WR, Junior EL, European Journal of Preventive Cardiology 2017 · PMID 28862020 · mixed

    Seventeen prospective studies, 73,761 participants, searched from 1 January 2000 to 31 December 2015, of which 12 studies randomised participants to ARB therapy (n=24,697) and five to ACE inhibitors (n=12,170) - two separate sets of trials against their own comparators rather than a head-to-head comparison, and the authors note that no prior review had compared either class with placebo in essential hypertension, because few studies exist with that design. ACE inhibitors were significant for reducing total deaths (OR 0.85, 95% CI 0.78-0.93) and cardiovascular deaths (OR 0.77, 0.69-0.87), while ARB therapy showed no reduction in total deaths (OR 1.02, 0.96-1.09) or cardiovascular deaths (OR 0.95, 0.86-1.06); for acute myocardial infarction, stroke and heart failure or hospitalisation, reductions were significant for both classes. The mortality contrast is therefore indirect, drawn across different trial sets with different comparators. Direct randomised comparison of the two classes found no mortality difference - RR 0.98 (0.88-1.10) in the five head-to-head trials reporting it (PMID 25148386) and RR 1.01 (0.94-1.09) in ONTARGET (PMID 18378520) - and that is the stronger design for this question.

  12. The Study on Cognition and Prognosis in the Elderly (SCOPE): principal results of a randomized double-blind intervention trial.
    Randomised trialLithell H, Hansson L, Skoog I, et al.; SCOPE Study Group, J Hypertens 2003 · PMID 12714861 · mixed

    4,964 patients aged 70-89 with systolic blood pressure 160-179 mmHg and/or diastolic 90-99 mmHg and a Mini Mental State Examination score of at least 24, at 527 centres in 15 countries, randomised to the angiotensin receptor blocker candesartan or placebo with open-label antihypertensive therapy added as needed - which meant active antihypertensive therapy was extensively used in the control group, 84% of patients, so this is not a clean placebo comparison. Over a mean 3.7 years blood pressure fell 21.7/10.8 mmHg on candesartan and 18.5/9.2 mmHg on control. A first major cardiovascular event - cardiovascular death, non-fatal stroke or non-fatal myocardial infarction - occurred in 242 candesartan patients versus 268 control patients, a non-significant risk reduction of 10.9% (95% CI -6.0 to 25.1, P=0.19). Non-fatal stroke fell 27.8% (1.3 to 47.2, P=0.04) and all stroke 23.6% (-0.7 to 42.1, P=0.056), with no significant differences in myocardial infarction or cardiovascular mortality. Mean Mini Mental State Examination score fell from 28.5 to 28.0 on candesartan and from 28.5 to 27.9 on control (P=0.20), and the proportions with significant cognitive decline or dementia did not differ.

  13. Blood-Pressure Lowering in Intermediate-Risk Persons without Cardiovascular Disease.
    Randomised trialLonn EM, Bosch J, Lopez-Jaramillo P, et al.; HOPE-3 Investigators, N Engl J Med 2016 · PMID 27041480 · null

    One comparison from a two-by-two factorial trial: 12,705 intermediate-risk adults without cardiovascular disease, mean baseline 138.1/81.9 mm Hg, randomised to candesartan 16 mg plus hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg daily or placebo for a median 5.6 years. Blood pressure fell a further 6.0/3.0 mm Hg on treatment, but the first co-primary outcome of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction or non-fatal stroke occurred in 260 participants (4.1%) versus 279 (4.4%), hazard ratio 0.93 (95% CI 0.79-1.10; P=0.40), and the second co-primary outcome in 312 (4.9%) versus 328 (5.2%), hazard ratio 0.95 (0.81-1.11; P=0.51). Benefit appeared only in the prespecified subgroup in the upper third of baseline systolic pressure, above 143.5 mm Hg, and was neutral in the middle and lower thirds. The randomised intervention was an ARB and a thiazide given together, so the null belongs to that regimen and to the question of whom to treat at this pressure level, not to ARBs on their own. Funded by the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and AstraZeneca.

  14. Blood pressure lowering and risk of new-onset type 2 diabetes: an individual participant data meta-analysis.
    Network meta-analysisNazarzadeh M, Bidel Z, Canoy D, et al.; Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, Lancet 2021 · PMID 34774144 · positive

    A one-stage individual participant data meta-analysis of 145,939 participants from 19 randomised trials, with 22 trials in the network meta-analysis; median follow-up 4.5 years; 9,883 new diagnoses of type 2 diabetes; people with a known diagnosis of diabetes at baseline excluded. A 5 mm Hg systolic reduction cut type 2 diabetes risk by 11% (hazard ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.84-0.95). By class versus placebo, ACE inhibitors (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.93) and ARBs (RR 0.84, 0.76-0.92) reduced new-onset type 2 diabetes, while beta blockers (RR 1.48, 1.27-1.72) and thiazide diuretics (RR 1.20, 1.07-1.35) increased it, and calcium channel blockers had no material effect (RR 1.02, 0.92-1.13). New-onset type 2 diabetes is a threshold-defined diagnosis, not a hard cardiovascular event.

  15. Comprehensive comparative effectiveness and safety of first-line antihypertensive drug classes: a systematic, multinational, large-scale analysis.
    Meta-analysisSuchard MA, Schuemie MJ, Krumholz HM, et al., Lancet 2019 · PMID 31668726 · mixed

    The Background states the guideline position this entry's second limb rests on: current guidelines recommend any primary agent among the first-line drug classes thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers and non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers, in the absence of comorbid indications, and randomised trials have not further refined this choice. A new-user cohort analysis of 4.9 million patients across six administrative claims and three electronic health record databases then generated 22,000 calibrated, propensity-score-adjusted hazard ratios comparing all first-line classes on three primary outcomes - acute myocardial infarction, hospitalisation for heart failure and stroke - plus six secondary effectiveness and 46 safety outcomes. Most estimates revealed no effectiveness difference between classes, supporting equivalence for initiating monotherapy, but thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics showed better primary effectiveness than ACE inhibitors for acute myocardial infarction (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.75-0.95), hospitalisation for heart failure (0.83, 0.74-0.95) and stroke (0.83, 0.74-0.95) while on initial treatment, with safety profiles also favouring diuretics over ACE inhibitors. Non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers were significantly inferior to the other four classes. This is observational, not randomised. Funding included Janssen Research and Development and IQVIA alongside public funders in the USA, South Korea and Australia.

  16. Pharmacological blood pressure lowering for primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease across different levels of blood pressure: an individual participant-level data meta-analysis.
    Meta-analysisBlood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, Lancet 2021 · PMID 33933205 · positive

    Individual participant data from 48 randomised trials (344,716 participants; median 4.15 years' follow-up) of blood-pressure-lowering drugs versus placebo, versus other classes, or more versus less intensive regimens. A 5 mm Hg systolic reduction lowered major cardiovascular events by about 10%: hazard ratio 0.91 (95% CI 0.89-0.94) in the 186,988 people without prior cardiovascular disease (baseline 157/89 mm Hg) and 0.89 (0.86-0.92) in the 157,728 with it (baseline 146/84 mm Hg). Primary-prevention event rates fell from 31.9 to 25.9 per 1000 person-years, with no reliable heterogeneity across baseline systolic categories from under 120 to 170 mm Hg or more. This is a class-collective result about the millimetres removed: the abstract stratifies only by prior cardiovascular disease and by baseline systolic category and reports nothing by drug class, so it says nothing specific about ACE inhibitors or ARBs. Its data come from 51 studies published between 1972 and 2013, comparing drugs with placebo, with other classes, or more against less intensive regimens - the same body of trials this entry cites individually - so it is not independent of them; the abstract names no included trial, so that overlap is structural rather than itemised. The Lancet's published correction (PMID 34022983) updates only the collaboration's group list; no estimate changed.
