Topiramate

Moderate evidence · for migraine & headache

For migraine & headache

Topiramate is a daily tablet taken to make migraines less frequent - it does nothing for an attack that has already started. On average it cuts about one attack per month compared with a dummy pill, and roughly one in four people who take it get a halving of their attacks that they wouldn't have got otherwise. The catch is side effects: pins and needles, brain fog, word-finding trouble and weight loss are common, nearly four in ten people stopped taking it in one head-to-head trial, and it can seriously harm a developing baby, so it needs reliable contraception.

Safety

Adverse effects are common and dose-related, with numbers-needed-to-harm as low as 3 at 100 mg/day and 2 at 200 mg/day. Frequent effects include paraesthesia (pins and needles, reported by about 30% in trials), taste disturbance, weight loss, fatigue, somnolence, dizziness and nausea. Cognitive effects - word-finding difficulty, slowed thinking, memory and concentration problems - are among the commonest reasons people stop; nearly 4 in 10 discontinued for adverse events in the HER-MES trial. Mood change and depression occur, and a suicide attempt was recorded in the topiramate arm of the CHAMP paediatric trial; antiepileptics as a class carry a suicidality warning. Less common but important: acute angle-closure glaucoma (sudden eye pain and blurred vision - urgent care), oligohidrosis with hyperthermia (especially in hot weather), metabolic acidosis, kidney stones, and hyperammonaemic encephalopathy when combined with valproate. Titrate up slowly and taper down rather than stopping abruptly.

Who should be careful: Pregnancy, and in people who could become pregnant without reliable contraception - first-trimester exposure was associated with a 2.5- to 5.4-fold higher birth prevalence of oral clefts, and topiramate is restricted or contraindicated for migraine prevention in pregnancy in several regulatory jurisdictions. Also avoid with a history of nephrolithiasis, narrow-angle glaucoma, or metabolic acidosis; use caution in significant renal or hepatic impairment and in people with a history of depression or suicidal ideation.

Interactions: Reduces the effectiveness of combined oral contraceptives at higher doses - use an alternative or additional contraceptive method. Additive risk of kidney stones and metabolic acidosis with carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, zonisamide). Raises the risk of hyperammonaemic encephalopathy with valproate. Levels are lowered by enzyme-inducing antiepileptics (carbamazepine, phenytoin). Additive CNS depression with alcohol and sedatives; increased heat-intolerance risk with anticholinergics.

What the evidence says

Topiramate is a daily PREVENTIVE; it has no acute role. A Cochrane meta-analysis of 17 trials found topiramate reduced headache frequency by about 1.2 attacks per 28 days versus placebo (MD -1.20, 95% CI -1.59 to -0.80; 9 trials, 1,737 participants) and roughly doubled the responder rate (RR 2.02, 1.57-2.60; NNT 4, 95% CI 3 to 6). Dose-response is flat above 100 mg/day - 200 mg was no more effective than 100 mg - while harms rose with dose, with numbers-needed-to-harm of 3 to 25 at 100 mg and 2 to 17 at 200 mg. The same review found no significant advantage over amitriptyline (1 study, 330 participants), flunarizine, propranolol (2 studies, 342 participants) or relaxation training, and relaxation improved migraine-specific quality of life more than topiramate did. Tolerability is the principal limitation: in the 777-patient double-dummy HER-MES trial, 38.9% of the topiramate group discontinued for adverse events versus 10.6% on erenumab, and 50%-or-greater responders were 31.2% on topiramate versus 55.4% on erenumab. In chronic migraine, topiramate reduced headache/migraine days by less than 1.5 days per month against 2.0-2.5 for CGRP monoclonal antibodies, and was the only drug in a chronic-migraine network meta-analysis that did not improve headache-related quality of life - though it remained the least costly and most cost-effective option at a 50,000 GBP per QALY threshold, and the same health technology assessment found no evidence the CGRP antibodies are more clinically and cost-effective than topiramate. The largest placebo-controlled paediatric trial (CHAMP, 328 children and adolescents) was stopped early for futility with no separation from placebo, which does not directly transfer to adults but should temper confidence. The 2025 ACP guideline includes antiseizure medications among the classes evaluated for initial preventive monotherapy, but rates all three of its recommendations conditional with low-certainty evidence.

Doses studied: 50, 100 or 200 mg per day orally, titrated slowly. Cochrane identifies 100 mg/day as effective and reasonably well tolerated; 200 mg/day was no more effective than 100 mg but produced more adverse events. Taken daily for PREVENTION, not during an attack.

Why this grade: A Cochrane meta-analysis of 17 trials shows a real but small preventive effect (about 1.2 fewer attacks per 28 days; responder NNT 4), and it is the ONLY oral preventive with trial data good enough to include in recent chronic-migraine evidence syntheses. Graded down from strong because the effect is modest, 200 mg is no better than 100 mg, tolerability is poor (38.9% stopped for adverse events in a head-to-head trial), it did not beat placebo in the largest paediatric trial, it did not outperform amitriptyline, propranolol or relaxation in head-to-head comparisons, it was the only drug in a chronic-migraine network meta-analysis that failed to improve headache-related quality of life, and the 2025 ACP guideline rates all preventive recommendations conditional with low-certainty evidence.

Sources (7)
  1. Topiramate for the prophylaxis of episodic migraine in adults.
    Meta-analysisLinde, Mulleners, Chronicle & McCrory, 2013 (Cochrane) · PMID 23797676 · positive

    20 papers describing 17 unique trials. Topiramate reduced headache frequency by about 1.2 attacks per 28 days versus placebo (MD -1.20, 95% CI -1.59 to -0.80; 9 trials, 1,737 participants) and doubled the responder rate (RR 2.02, 1.57-2.60; NNT 4, 95% CI 3 to 6; 9 trials, 1,190 participants). Meta-analysis of the three multi-dose studies suggests 200 mg is no more effective than 100 mg. No significant difference versus amitriptyline, flunarizine, propranolol or relaxation; relaxation improved migraine-specific quality of life more than topiramate. Adverse events at 100 mg gave NNHs of 3 to 25, and at 200 mg NNHs of 2 to 17.

  2. Erenumab versus topiramate for the prevention of migraine - a randomised, double-blind, active-controlled phase 4 trial.
    Randomised trialReuter et al., 2022 (Cephalalgia, HER-MES) · PMID 34743579 · negative

    777 patients, 24-week double-blind double-dummy head-to-head, 95.1% completed. 38.9% of the topiramate group (50-100 mg/day) discontinued because of an adverse event versus 10.6% on erenumab (erenumab vs topiramate OR 0.19, 95% CI 0.13-0.27, P<0.001). 50%-or-greater reduction in monthly migraine days: 31.2% on topiramate versus 55.4% on erenumab (OR 2.76, 2.06-3.71, P<0.001). Topiramate was clearly inferior on both tolerability and efficacy in this direct comparison.

  3. Trial of Amitriptyline, Topiramate, and Placebo for Pediatric Migraine.
    Randomised trialPowers et al., 2017 (N Engl J Med, CHAMP) · PMID 27788026 · negative

    NOTE: children and adolescents aged 8-17, not adults. 361 randomised, 328 analysed; trial concluded early for futility at a planned interim analysis. 50%-or-greater reduction in headache days: 55% topiramate, 52% amitriptyline, 61% placebo (topiramate vs placebo P=0.48). No differences in headache-related disability, headache days or trial completion. Topiramate caused more paraesthesia (31% vs 8%) and weight loss (8% vs 0%), and one patient in the topiramate group had a suicide attempt.

  4. Clinical effectiveness of pharmacological interventions for managing chronic migraine in adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis.
    Network meta-analysisNaghdi et al., 2023 (J Headache Pain) · PMID 38057728 · mixed

    12 RCTs in chronic migraine. Topiramate beat placebo on monthly headache and migraine days, but was the ONLY one of the six included drugs that did not improve headache-related quality of life, and ranked below the CGRP antibodies. No eligible studies existed for amitriptyline, candesartan or propranolol. Authors emphasise the absolute differences across all drugs are at best modest.

  5. Preventive drug treatments for adults with chronic migraine: a systematic review with economic modelling.
    Network meta-analysisMistry et al., 2024 (Health Technol Assess) · PMID 39365169 · mixed

    11 RCTs, 7,352 adults with chronic migraine. Topiramate reduced headache/migraine days by less than 1.5 days per month, versus 2.0-2.5 for CGRP monoclonal antibodies and just under 2 for onabotulinumtoxinA, and was very unlikely to be the best choice for headache days or quality of life against them. However the authors state 'Topiramate was the only oral drug for which we were able to include data', that they found no evidence the CGRP antibodies are more clinically and cost-effective than topiramate or Botox, and that topiramate was the least costly option and most cost-effective up to 50,000 GBP per QALY. Nervous-system and gastrointestinal adverse events were common with topiramate in the 40-trial, 25,891-participant adverse-events review.

  6. Prevention of Episodic Migraine Headache Using Pharmacologic Treatments in Outpatient Settings: A Clinical Guideline From the American College of Physicians.
    GuidelineQaseem et al., 2025 (Ann Intern Med) · PMID 39899861 · mixed

    Guideline for adults with 1-14 headache days per month. Antiseizure medications (valproate and topiramate) were among the eight drug classes ACP evaluated for initiating preventive monotherapy. All three ACP recommendations have conditional strength and low-certainty evidence - meaning no preventive drug, topiramate included, has high-certainty guideline support.

  7. Topiramate use in pregnancy and the birth prevalence of oral clefts.
    Journal articleMines et al., 2014 (Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf) · PMID 24692316 · negative

    Retrospective cohort across four US data sources. Birth prevalence of oral clefts was 0.36% (7/1,945) after first-trimester topiramate exposure, versus 0.14% (20/13,512) in formerly-exposed women and 0.07% (9/13,614) in women with similar medical profiles. Standardised prevalence ratios 2.5 (95% CI 1.0-6.0) and 5.4 (2.0-14.6); adjustment for covariates individually or by propensity score yielded similar results.
