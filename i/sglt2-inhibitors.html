Conventional medicine

SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin)

Strong evidence · for blood sugar & type 2 diabetes

For blood sugar & type 2 diabetes

These once-daily tablets make the kidneys pass extra sugar out in the urine, which brings blood sugar down modestly and takes some fluid and weight off. Their real strength is not the sugar number at all: in large trials they clearly reduced hospital admissions for heart failure and slowed kidney damage toward dialysis, and the kidney protection holds up even in people who do not have diabetes. One of these drugs, empagliflozin, also reduced deaths in a trial of people at high cardiovascular risk; two others in the class showed no difference in deaths, so survival benefit is something to check drug by drug rather than assume across the family. The main downsides are genital yeast infections, which are common, and a rare but dangerous form of ketoacidosis that can happen even when your blood sugar reading looks normal - which is why prescribers give sick-day rules about pausing the tablet when you are unwell, fasting or having surgery. Canagliflozin specifically was linked to more amputations in one large trial. This is prescription treatment, and nothing here is a reason to change what you are taking.

Safety

Euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis is the key warning: it can occur with near-normal glucose, so nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, breathlessness or unusual fatigue while taking one of these needs urgent ketone testing regardless of the glucose reading. Pooled randomised risk is roughly doubled (RR 2.32) (PMID 40070044) and the absolute rate was 0.3% versus 0.1% over a median 4.2 years in DECLARE (PMID 30415602). Because of this, prescribers issue sick-day rules for these tablets covering vomiting, fasting, acute illness and surgery - ask your own prescriber for yours, and follow theirs rather than any general advice. Ask your prescriber or pharmacist for blood ketone strips and a meter that reads them, and keep them at home - blood ketone testing is not always easy to get hold of, and that limited availability is one of the things that makes this complication hard to catch early (PMID 40926068). Euglycaemic ketoacidosis cannot be seen on a glucose reading, so a normal glucose number rules nothing out. Blood beta-hydroxybutyrate is the measurement clinicians use, and a urine ketone strip is a reasonable fallback if it is what you have: in 516 emergency-department patients with high glucose, of whom 54 had ketoacidosis, the urine dipstick and point-of-care beta-hydroxybutyrate were exactly as sensitive as each other (98.1% each), with beta-hydroxybutyrate the more specific of the two (78.6% versus 35.1%), meaning a positive urine strip is much more often a false alarm (PMID 21307381) - though that study enrolled people with high glucose, so it does not settle how urine strips perform in euglycaemic ketoacidosis specifically. Either way, ketones detected while you are unwell on one of these tablets is a reason to contact your prescriber or urgent care, not to wait and see. For planned surgery there is an actual figure to raise with your team: international guidance recommends withholding SGLT2 inhibitors 3-4 days beforehand, and the FDA advises at least 3 days, because surgical stress and prolonged fasting are among the main triggers of postoperative euglycaemic ketoacidosis (PMID 40926068, 39969891). Be aware the question is contested rather than settled - in 34,671 people with type 2 diabetes having emergency surgery, where the drug could not be stopped in advance, postoperative ketoacidosis was 3.8% with an SGLT2 inhibitor versus 3.5% without after adjustment, a difference of 0.2% (95% CI -1.7% to 2.2%) (PMID 39969891), and stopping the drug has been associated with worse outcomes in people with heart failure (PMID 40926068). That is why the decision is individualised: bring the 3-4 day figure to your surgeon and prescriber and let them decide, rather than stopping or continuing on your own. Genital yeast infections are the most common side effect (OR 3.29 to 5.06) and urinary tract infections are modestly increased (OR 1.42) (PMID 40813122, 24026259). The class also lowers body weight and blood pressure (PMID 24026259), and kidney benefit is consistent in people with and without diabetes (PMID 32970396, 36331190). Nothing here is a reason to stop, reduce or substitute a supplement or food for a prescribed glucose-lowering medicine.

Who should be careful: Type 1 diabetes: the ketoacidosis risk is why these drugs are not a substitute for insulin and are not used in type 1 diabetes outside specialist care. Also a prior episode of ketoacidosis, a very low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diet, heavy alcohol use, or kidney function below the trial entry thresholds (eGFR 20 to 30 depending on the agent; PMID 36331190, 30990260). Canagliflozin doubled amputations in CANVAS (HR 1.97, 1.41-2.75) (PMID 28605608) and the 2025 BMJ living network meta-analysis rates amputation a probable class harm (OR 1.27, 1.01-1.61) (PMID 40813122), so a history of foot ulcers, amputation or peripheral arterial disease is a reason to discuss another agent - although a 13-trial meta-analysis of 84,581 participants found no significant amputation difference (P=0.07) (PMID 41767694). Product labels carry a warning for Fournier's gangrene (necrotising perineal infection); a rapid review found no study reporting a significant association, including a meta-analysis of 84 studies (PMID 33882584), so this is rare but severe. A 2013 review also noted an imbalance in bladder and breast cancer incidence with dapagliflozin versus control (PMID 24026259), a signal not borne out in later outcome trials.

Interactions: With insulin or sulphonylureas, hypoglycaemia risk rises and those doses usually need reducing by the prescriber; SGLT2 inhibitors on their own carried hypoglycaemia risk similar to other agents (PMID 24026259). Pooled randomised trials show an excess of hypovolaemia (P=0.008) alongside a 20% reduction in acute kidney injury (RR 0.80, 0.74-0.87) (PMID 41767694), so volume status is worth watching, particularly alongside diuretics or blood-pressure medicines. Reduced carbohydrate intake, intercurrent illness, surgical stress and alcohol consumption are the recognised triggers for euglycaemic ketoacidosis on these drugs (PMID 42352947).

What the evidence says

Glucose-lowering here is a SURROGATE and it is modest: versus placebo, SGLT2 inhibitors lowered HbA1c by about 0.66 percentage points (95% CI -0.73 to -0.58; 45 studies, n=11,232), no better than active comparators (-0.06), with weight -1.80 kg and systolic blood pressure -4.45 mmHg (PMID 24026259); dapagliflozin monotherapy gave -0.60 points and fasting glucose -1.30 mmol/L (PMID 31348290), and the HbA1c effect shrinks with age (0.24 points less per 30-year increment) even as cardiovascular protection grows (PMID 39899304). The class earns its grade on HARD OUTCOMES: in EMPA-REG OUTCOME (7,020 patients at high cardiovascular risk, median 3.1 years) empagliflozin reduced the MACE composite (10.5% vs 12.1%, HR 0.86, 0.74-0.99), cardiovascular death by 38%, heart-failure hospitalisation by 35% and all-cause death by 32%, with no difference in MI or stroke (PMID 26378978); canagliflozin reduced MACE (HR 0.86, 0.75-0.97) but doubled amputations (6.3 vs 3.4 per 1,000 patient-years, HR 1.97, 1.41-2.75) in CANVAS (PMID 28605608). That MACE benefit is not uniform: dapagliflozin did not reduce MACE in DECLARE-TIMI 58 (8.8% vs 9.4%, HR 0.93, 0.84-1.03, P=0.17) though it did reduce cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation (HR 0.83) and kidney events (HR 0.76) (PMID 30415602), and ertugliflozin was merely noninferior in VERTIS CV (11.9% vs 11.9%, HR 0.97, 0.85-1.11), missing significance on the heart-failure composite too (HR 0.88, P=0.11) (PMID 32966714); pooled across 34,322 patients the 11% MACE reduction was confined to those with existing atherosclerotic disease (HR 0.86 versus 1.00 without), while the cardiovascular-death-or-heart-failure benefit (0.77) and kidney-progression benefit (0.55) held regardless (PMID 30424892). Kidney protection is the most consistent finding: canagliflozin cut the kidney-failure composite 30% (43.2 vs 61.2 events per 1,000 patient-years, HR 0.70) with end-stage kidney disease down 32% and no significant excess amputation or fracture in that trial (PMID 30990260); dapagliflozin HR 0.61 with a number-needed-to-treat of 19 in DAPA-CKD (PMID 32970396); empagliflozin HR 0.72 in EMPA-KIDNEY, though with no significant difference there in all-cause death (4.5% vs 5.1%) or the heart-failure/cardiovascular-death composite (4.0% vs 4.6%) (PMID 36331190) - both kidney trials showing similar results with and without diabetes. Against this: diabetic ketoacidosis was 0.3% vs 0.1% in DECLARE (PMID 30415602), pooled across 22 RCTs and 80,235 patients the risk ratio is 2.32 (1.64-3.27), significant where baseline HbA1c exceeded 7.9% (RR 2.24) but not below it (RR 1.05; interaction P=0.034) (PMID 40070044); and the 2025 BMJ living network meta-analysis of 869 trials rates genital infection (OR 3.29, 2.88-3.77) and ketoacidosis (OR 2.08, 1.45-2.99) as high-certainty harms and amputation (OR 1.27, 1.01-1.61) as a probable one (PMID 40813122), while a 13-trial meta-analysis of 84,581 participants found no significant amputation difference (P=0.07) alongside a 20% reduction in acute kidney injury (RR 0.80, 0.74-0.87) (PMID 41767694).

Doses studied: Empagliflozin 10 or 25 mg once daily (EMPA-REG OUTCOME; PMID 26378978) and 10 mg daily (EMPA-KIDNEY; PMID 36331190). Dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily (DAPA-CKD; PMID 32970396). Canagliflozin 100 mg daily (CREDENCE; PMID 30990260) and 100 or 300 mg (CANVAS; PMID 28605608). Ertugliflozin 5 or 15 mg daily (VERTIS CV; PMID 32966714).

Why this grade: Graded strong on HARD OUTCOMES rather than glucose-lowering, which is the honest basis for this class. Large placebo-controlled RCTs and their meta-analyses consistently show reduced hospitalisation for heart failure and slowed progression to kidney failure and dialysis; death is the inconsistent part - empagliflozin reduced cardiovascular death 38% and all-cause death 32% in EMPA-REG OUTCOME (PMID 26378978), while dapagliflozin (CV death HR 0.98, all-cause 0.93; PMID 30415602) and ertugliflozin (CV death HR 0.92; PMID 32966714) did not, and the pooled 0.77 below is for cardiovascular death OR heart-failure hospitalisation combined, not death alone: pooled across 34,322 patients, the composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation fell 23% (HR 0.77, 0.71-0.84) and progression of renal disease fell 45% (HR 0.55, 0.48-0.64) (PMID 30424892), with dedicated kidney trials confirming benefit in people with and without diabetes (PMID 30990260, 32970396, 36331190), and reviews describe them as first-line in established cardiovascular disease, heart failure and chronic kidney disease (PMID 42352947, 40813122). The honest limits: the HbA1c effect is modest and is a surrogate (about 0.66 points, PMID 24026259); the reduction in heart attack and stroke is small and confined to people who already have atherosclerotic disease, with two of the four dedicated cardiovascular outcome trials showing no MACE benefit (PMID 30415602, 32966714); ketoacidosis risk is roughly doubled (PMID 40070044); canagliflozin doubled amputations in CANVAS (PMID 28605608); and none of these trials tested the class for preventing diabetes in people with prediabetes.

Sources (11)
  1. Empagliflozin, Cardiovascular Outcomes, and Mortality in Type 2 Diabetes.
    Zinman B et al. (EMPA-REG OUTCOME), 2015 · PMID 26378978 · positive

    HARD OUTCOMES. 7,020 people with T2D at high cardiovascular risk, median 3.1 years, empagliflozin 10 or 25 mg daily: primary composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke 10.5% versus 12.1% (HR 0.86, 95.02% CI 0.74-0.99; P=0.04 for superiority). Cardiovascular death 3.7% versus 5.9% (38% relative reduction), heart-failure hospitalisation 2.7% versus 4.1% (35%), death from any cause 5.7% versus 8.3% (32%). No significant difference in MI or stroke. Genital infection increased; no other excess adverse events.

  2. Canagliflozin and Renal Outcomes in Type 2 Diabetes and Nephropathy.
    Perkovic V et al. (CREDENCE), 2019 · PMID 30990260 · positive

    HARD OUTCOMES. 4,401 people with T2D and albuminuric chronic kidney disease, median 2.62 years, canagliflozin 100 mg daily; stopped early for efficacy. Composite of end-stage kidney disease, doubling of creatinine, or renal/cardiovascular death: 43.2 versus 61.2 events per 1,000 patient-years (HR 0.70, 0.59-0.82; P=0.00001). End-stage kidney disease HR 0.68 (0.54-0.86); renal-specific composite 0.66; MACE 0.80 (0.67-0.95); heart-failure hospitalisation 0.61 (0.47-0.80). No significant difference in amputation or fracture in this trial.

  3. SGLT2 inhibitors for primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular and renal outcomes in type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cardiovascular outcome trials.
    Zelniker TA et al., 2018 · PMID 30424892 · mixed

    MIXED, and the key qualifier on this class. Three placebo-controlled outcome trials, 34,322 patients: MACE reduced 11% (HR 0.89, 0.83-0.96) but the benefit was seen ONLY in patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (HR 0.86, 0.80-0.93) and not in those without (HR 1.00, 0.87-1.16; interaction P=0.0501). The composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation fell 23% (HR 0.77, 0.71-0.84) and progression of renal disease fell 45% (HR 0.55, 0.48-0.64), both regardless of atherosclerotic disease or heart-failure history. Note this 0.77 is the COMBINED endpoint of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation, not cardiovascular death on its own.

  4. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors for type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
    Vasilakou D et al., 2013 · PMID 24026259 · mixed

    SURROGATE endpoints plus early harms. 45 placebo-controlled studies (n=11,232) and 13 active-comparator studies (n=5,175): HbA1c -0.66 percentage points versus placebo (-0.73 to -0.58) and -0.06 versus active comparators (-0.18 to 0.05); weight -1.80 kg (-3.50 to -0.11) and systolic BP -4.45 mmHg (-5.73 to -3.18) versus other agents. Urinary tract infection OR 1.42 (1.06-1.90) and genital infection OR 5.06 (3.44-7.45). Hypoglycaemic risk similar to other agents. Results for cardiovascular outcomes and death were inconclusive at that time, and an imbalance in bladder and breast cancer incidence was noted with dapagliflozin versus control. Most trials rated high risk of bias.

  5. Dapagliflozin and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Type 2 Diabetes.
    Wiviott SD et al. (DECLARE-TIMI 58), 2018 · PMID 30415602 · mixed

    MIXED. 17,160 people with T2D with or at risk of atherosclerotic disease (10,186 without established disease), median 4.2 years: dapagliflozin did NOT reduce MACE (8.8% versus 9.4%, HR 0.93, 0.84-1.03, P=0.17) and did not reduce cardiovascular death (HR 0.98, 0.82-1.17) or all-cause death (0.93, 0.82-1.04), but did reduce cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation (4.9% versus 5.8%, HR 0.83, 0.73-0.95) driven by heart-failure hospitalisation (HR 0.73, 0.61-0.88), and kidney events (4.3% versus 5.6%, HR 0.76, 0.67-0.87). Diabetic ketoacidosis 0.3% versus 0.1% (P=0.02); serious or discontinuation-causing genital infections 0.9% versus 0.1% (P<0.001).

  6. Cardiovascular Outcomes with Ertugliflozin in Type 2 Diabetes.
    Cannon CP et al. (VERTIS CV), 2020 · PMID 32966714 · null

    HARD OUTCOME, NULL. 8,246 people with T2D and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, mean 3.5 years, ertugliflozin 5 or 15 mg daily: MACE 11.9% versus 11.9% (HR 0.97, 95.6% CI 0.85-1.11) - noninferior but no benefit. Cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation 8.1% versus 9.1% (HR 0.88, 0.75-1.03; P=0.11, not significant). Cardiovascular death HR 0.92 (0.77-1.11); renal composite 0.81 (0.63-1.04). Amputations in 2.0% and 2.1% versus 1.6% on placebo.

  7. Canagliflozin and Cardiovascular and Renal Events in Type 2 Diabetes.
    Neal B et al. (CANVAS Program), 2017 · PMID 28605608 · mixed

    MIXED - benefit and the class's most serious harm in one trial. 10,142 participants with T2D and high cardiovascular risk, mean 188.2 weeks: primary composite 26.9 versus 31.5 per 1,000 patient-years (HR 0.86, 0.75-0.97; P=0.02 for superiority), with possible benefit on albuminuria progression (HR 0.73) and a 40% eGFR decline/renal-replacement/renal-death composite (HR 0.60), though not formally significant in the testing sequence. Amputation risk was increased: 6.3 versus 3.4 participants per 1,000 patient-years (HR 1.97, 1.41-2.75), primarily at toe or metatarsal level.

  8. Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors and Diabetic-Ketoacidosis in T2DM Patients: An Updated Meta-Analysis and a Mendelian Randomization Analysis.
    Wang Y et al., 2025 · PMID 40070044 · negative

    HARM. 22 RCTs, 80,235 people with T2D: SGLT2 inhibitors increased diabetic ketoacidosis (RR 2.32, 95% CI 1.64-3.27). Risk was significant where baseline HbA1c exceeded 7.9% (RR 2.24, 1.59-3.14) but not at or below 7.9% (RR 1.05, 0.49-2.26; interaction P=0.034), and was raised in chronic kidney disease trials (RR 2.70, 1.55-4.71) and high-atherosclerotic-risk trials (RR 2.46, 1.47-4.11) but not significantly in heart-failure trials (RR 1.23, 0.51-2.96). A Mendelian randomisation analysis supported a genetic association.

  9. [Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors in perioperative medicine: Effects, side effects and current recommendations].
    Nienhaus J et al., 2025 · PMID 40926068 · mixed

    SAFETY GUIDANCE, CONTESTED. Narrative review of perioperative management. International guidelines recommend withholding SGLT-2 inhibitors 3-4 days before elective surgery, because surgical stress, prolonged fasting and metabolic changes are considered the key triggers of postoperative (euglycaemic) ketoacidosis - which the review calls rare, potentially life-threatening and difficult to detect. In the opposite direction, an increase in undesired events has been described when the medication is discontinued in patients with heart failure, so the review concludes a one-size-fits-all approach is unsuitable and an individualised, risk-stratified strategy considering surgical risk, fasting duration and ketone monitoring is more appropriate. It also notes the evidence base is extremely limited, recommendations are heterogeneous, blood ketone testing availability is limited, and guidance on when to restart is unclear.

  10. Preoperative SGLT2 Inhibitor Use and Postoperative Diabetic Ketoacidosis.
    Dixit AA et al., 2025 · PMID 39969891 · null

    NULL, and the counterweight to the withholding advice. Retrospective cohort of 34,671 insured adults with T2D undergoing 1 of 13 emergency surgeries 2016-2022 (chosen precisely because these patients could not withhold the drug in advance); 2,607 (7.5%) used an SGLT2 inhibitor. Unadjusted postoperative diabetic ketoacidosis 4.9% exposed versus 3.5% unexposed; after adjustment for demographics, diabetes severity, comorbidities and surgery type, 3.8% versus 3.5%, average treatment effect 0.2% (95% CI -1.7% to 2.2%). Robust to alternate specifications (ICU-level care: ATE -1.0%, -2.9% to 1.1%). The authors note FDA guidance is to withhold for at least 3 days before surgery and conclude these findings may justify liberalising that withholding period.

  11. Diagnostic accuracy of point-of-care testing for diabetic ketoacidosis at emergency-department triage: beta-hydroxybutyrate versus the urine dipstick.
    Arora S et al., 2011 · PMID 21307381 · mixed

    DIAGNOSTIC, MIXED - and it corrects a common assumption. 516 emergency-department patients with blood glucose 250 mg/dL or above, 54 with diabetic ketoacidosis by ADA criteria. Urine dipstick and point-of-care capillary beta-hydroxybutyrate (cutoff >1.5 mmol/L) were EQUALLY sensitive, 98.1% each (95% CI 90.1-100). Beta-hydroxybutyrate was substantially more specific (78.6%, 74.5-82.2, versus 35.1%, 30.7-39.6) with a higher positive predictive value (34.9% versus 15%); negative predictive value was high for both (99.7% versus 99.4%). So the urine strip's weakness in this population was false alarms, not missed cases. Caveat for this entry: the cohort was hyperglycaemic by design, so it does not establish how urine strips perform in euglycaemic ketoacidosis on an SGLT2 inhibitor.
